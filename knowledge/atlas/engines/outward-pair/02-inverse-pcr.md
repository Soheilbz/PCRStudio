## Scope

Inverse PCR places primers facing away from one another on a known region so amplification traverses the unknown or flanking sequence after the template has been made circular or otherwise topology-compatible. The restriction digest, end repair/circularization, template topology and intended unknown interval are therefore part of the assay contract, not implementation details ([Green & Sambrook](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30710023/), [Ochman et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7866865/)).

The module owns outward orientation, circular-template coordinates, cleavage/circularization metadata, optional tails and junction validation. It must not silently behave like ordinary inward-facing PCR when the request lacks a topology or flanking interval ([Green & Sambrook](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30710023/)).

## Assay branches and topology contract

Inverse PCR is not one laboratory recipe. The atlas separates at least four branches: (1) restriction–self-ligation inverse PCR for recovering genomic or insert flanks, (2) random-fragment/blunt-end circularization followed by inverse PCR for integration-site or sequencing workflows, (3) whole-plasmid inverse PCR mutagenesis in which a circular plasmid is amplified into a linear mutant product and then repaired or recircularized, and (4) inverse-fusion or other cloning overlays that add a defined assembly step. These branches share outward extension but do not share assumptions about end chemistry, methylation, product topology, or post-PCR recovery ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Pavlopoulos](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065444/), [Inverse PCR for Point Mutation Introduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540701/)).

Across the literature, topology state is a first-class distinction: `circularized_fragment`, `circular_plasmid`, `random_blunt_circle`, `linear_control`, and `unknown` are not interchangeable. **Gen-1 executes two explicit topologies: `restriction-self-ligation` and `supplied-circular-template`. When a complete reference is supplied for the restriction branch, PCRStudio reconstructs the exact digest fragment/self-ligation topology and fails closed on ambiguous anchor placement. One-sided/internal-cut and unrelated mutagenesis/cloning topologies remain reference-only.** `linear_control` is diagnostic only; it must not be reported as a valid inverse-PCR substrate because linear molecules can produce minor single-primer or non-exponential products and, when multiple binding sites occur on one molecule, can produce ordinary PCR products ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

For a restriction-ligation branch, the expected amplicon is the linearized sequence path that crosses the ligated junction; for a whole-plasmid mutagenesis branch, the expected amplicon is the complete linear plasmid product. The same outward-facing geometry thus has different expected coordinates and different recovery requirements. The runtime must retain the branch identifier and must not infer it from primer orientation alone ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [NEB Q5 Site-Directed Mutagenesis](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale0552.pdf?hash=D53D03FFEEE28166D56BDF2BD463A69B&rev=04a4b1ab8cfd4578b02043ebaf468b28)).

**Internal software-contract fields:** The following fields are not scientific claims: `topology_branch`, `known_anchor`, `primer_pair_orientation`, `restriction_enzyme`, `known_anchor_site_count`, `circularization_provenance`, `end_chemistry`, `parental_template_methylation`, `expected_product_path`, `linear_background_control`, and `recovery_strategy`. They are required so that a result can be traced to the declared physical workflow rather than to a generic PCR default (internal software-contract; no external source).

## Extended topology and recovery rules

The standard inverse-PCR workflow is a physical sequence of restriction digestion, conversion of compatible fragments into circles by intramolecular ligation, and outward-primer amplification. In a genomic preparation this can create many unrelated circular fragments, so the record must distinguish the intended anchor-containing circle from the background fragment population and retain the digest/circularization provenance ([Current Protocols inverse PCR](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract)).

The restriction map is part of the design, not a post-hoc annotation. In the **current executable Gen-1 branch**, the complete submitted known anchor must remain on one restriction fragment, so the selected enzyme must have zero recognition sites inside that anchor. The two digest sites that bound the recoverable fragment are in flanking sequence that is not present in the submitted anchor; their positions therefore cannot be invented. The runtime reports one self-ligated anchor-containing fragment/circle and an unresolved or bounded combined-unknown-flank interval until fragment length is supplied. An internal-known-sequence cut belongs to a different topology and is not an executable branch of this engine ([Current Protocols inverse PCR](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Ochman et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7866865/), [Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html)).

Self-ligation and amplification range are separate failure modes. Alternative flanking-sequence methods identify circularization/self-ligation efficiency and polymerase reach as limiting variables, so the application must report them as unresolved experimental risks rather than infer a successful circle or a recoverable flank from the primer pair alone ([Cyclic digestion and ligation-mediated PCR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7044209/)).

## Protocol-specific digest and readout overlay

Thermo Fisher's inverse-PCR overview makes the standard workflow boundary explicit: genomic DNA is restriction-digested, the fragments are ligated before inverse PCR, the enzyme should not cut the known sequence, and the selected digest should produce fragments that can self-ligate. Sequencing follows amplification to identify the adjacent unknown sequence. These are workflow gates, not values that can be inferred from primer Tm or from a FASTA record alone ([Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/ht/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html)).

The module therefore treats `restriction_enzyme`, `known_anchor_site_count`, `fragment_size_expectation`, `ligation_method` and `sequence_confirmation` as one provenance bundle. A missing enzyme identity or unresolved external fragment boundary must remain an explicit workflow state; silently choosing a restriction enzyme would change which flanking molecule exists and could make an otherwise valid outward pair physically unreachable ([Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/ht/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html), [Current Protocols inverse PCR](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract)).

Primary inverse-PCR mutagenesis work also distinguishes non-overlapping, partially overlapping and fully overlapping mutant-primer arrangements, then couples the linear product to DpnI treatment, phosphorylation and recircularisation. These are different recovery contracts rather than interchangeable names for outward orientation. They are retained here as **reference-only adjacent workflows**; the current `outward-pair` runtime does not execute them and must not infer them from primer orientation ([Inverse PCR for Point Mutation Introduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540701/)).

## Numeric protocol overlays

The following values are retained as named protocol records, not promoted to universal inverse-PCR defaults. Thermo Fisher's restriction-digestion protocol uses approximately `0.1–0.5 µg` DNA in a `30 µL` reaction, with `2 µL` of `10X` buffer and `1–2 µL` restriction enzyme corresponding to `10–20 U`; when unpurified PCR product is used, the PCR fraction should not exceed `1/3` of the digest volume. The protocol gives an incubation window of `1–16 h` at the enzyme's optimal temperature. These values belong to that product/workflow family and do not establish a universal digest condition for genomic inverse PCR ([Thermo Fisher Digestion of PCR Products protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0011976_Digestion_PCR_Products_UG.pdf)).

For an inverse-fusion PCR recovery branch, the published method records a different numeric contract: `500 nM` each of primers B/C, `10 pM` circular template vector, `1 nM` gel-eluted insert, an approximate `1:100` vector:insert molar ratio, and about `5 pg/kb` vector plus `500 pg/kb` insert per `1 µL` of reaction mix. The Phusion concentration is `0.02 U/µL`; cycling is `98 °C` for `3 min`, then `25` cycles of `98 °C` for `20 s`, `58 °C` for `30 s`, and `72 °C` for `30 s/kb`, followed by `72 °C` for `7 min`. For recovery, `2 µL` PCR product is combined with `2.5 µL` of `2X` ligation buffer and `0.5 µL` T4 DNA ligase, then held `15 min` at room temperature. This is an inverse-fusion cloning overlay; it must not be emitted for ordinary flanking-sequence inverse PCR ([Inverse Fusion PCR Cloning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3328455/)).

The runtime must therefore keep `digest_volume`, `dna_input_mass`, `restriction_enzyme_units`, `digest_time`, `unpurified_pcr_fraction`, `inverse_fusion_primer_concentration`, `template_molarity`, `insert_molarity`, `polymerase_concentration`, `cycle_count`, `extension_time_per_kb`, `ligation_volume` and `ligation_hold` protocol-scoped. If the user has not selected a named overlay, these fields remain unresolved rather than receiving values copied from either record ([Thermo Fisher Digestion of PCR Products protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0011976_Digestion_PCR_Products_UG.pdf), [Inverse Fusion PCR Cloning](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3328455/)).

## End chemistry and topology-specific recovery

Restriction digestion and PCR create different terminal chemistry. A restriction enzyme normally leaves a 5′ phosphate on the digested duplex, whereas a PCR amplicon made with ordinary, unphosphorylated primers normally has 5′ hydroxyl ends. At least one ligated end must carry a 5′ phosphate; therefore the restriction-ligation genomic branch can often ligate directly after a compatible digest, while the whole-plasmid PCR branch must either use 5′-phosphorylated primers, phosphorylate the product with T4 PNK, or use a kit whose kinase is part of the recovery chemistry ([NEB traditional cloning guide](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cloning-guide), [NEB molecular cloning technical guide](https://www.neb.com/en-us/-/media/nebus/files/brochures/molcloning_tech_guide.pdf?hash=CE61408673A0B0908875BB7F41043B15&la=en-us&rev=4199dd98d543418484e7da84c7bb3580)).

Sticky-end and blunt-end ligation are separate protocol branches. NEB's T4 DNA-ligase guidance supports cohesive-end ligation at room temperature for `10 min`, whereas standard-concentration blunt-end ligation is given as `2 h` at room temperature; high-concentration ligase can shorten the blunt-end condition to `10 min`. The same guidance warns that ATP is an essential cofactor and recommends heat-inactivation or purification of restriction, kinase, or phosphatase reagents before the ligation step. These are vendor protocol values, not universal inverse-PCR defaults ([NEB T4 DNA Ligase protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/dna-ligation-with-t4-dna-ligase-m0202)).

For blunt-end random-fragment workflows, the sequence must record the end-repair and phosphorylation step before circularization. In the sonication inverse-PCR workflow of Alquezar-Planas et al., DNA was fragmented to approximately `2–7 kb`, blunt-end repaired and phosphorylated, then circularized; the study also used a `3–4 kb` average fragment workflow and a `16 °C`/`16 h` ligation with `70 °C`/`5 min` inactivation. These values belong to that sequencing workflow and cannot be transplanted to restriction-digested genomic DNA without a new validation ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

Intramolecular circularization must be separated conceptually from concatemer formation. The conventional inverse-PCR workflow lowers the concentration of digested fragments to favor self-ligation over intermolecular ligation, while blunt-end ligation is often aided by PEG. The application must expose the concentration and enhancer as selected protocol values and must not imply that more DNA always improves circle yield ([Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html), [Thermo self-circularization protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013192_Selof_Linear_DNA_UG.pdf), [Cyclic digestion and ligation-mediated PCR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7044209/)).

When linear-background depletion is selected, the enzyme scope must be explicit. Exonuclease III preferentially degrades linear or nicked double-stranded DNA from 3′ termini and can leave supercoiled duplex DNA intact, whereas T5 exonuclease initiates from 5′ termini or gaps and nicks of linear or nicked duplex DNA. Neither behavior proves that every intended circle is intact, so cleanup is an enrichment step and not a substitute for a circularization control or product sequencing ([NEB Exonuclease III protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/protocol-for-exonuclease-iii-m0206), [NEB T5 Exonuclease](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/m0663-t5-exonuclease)).

The whole-plasmid mutagenesis branch has a different recovery topology. The PCR product is linear and is recovered by DpnI removal of parental template followed by phosphorylation and ligation, host repair, or a named KLD-like chemistry. NEB's KLD mix explicitly combines kinase, ligase, and DpnI for rapid intramolecular circularization and parental-template removal; this must remain distinct from genomic inverse PCR, where DpnI may be irrelevant or may fail to remove the biological template ([NEB KLD enzyme-mix protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/protocols/kld-enzyme-mix-reaction-protocol-m0554), [Inverse PCR for Point Mutation Introduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540701/)).

## Primer placement and sequence-level constraints

In the flanking-sequence branch, both primers must bind uniquely within the known anchor and extend away from one another across the intended circle path. The selected restriction map must preserve both binding sites on the same fragment; if the enzyme cuts between them, the apparent pair is geometrically valid on the original reference but not co-resident on the circularized substrate. If the enzyme cuts the known anchor itself, each resulting circle must be treated as a separate branch rather than silently represented as one amplicon ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Pavlopoulos](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065444/)).

There is no evidence-based universal distance from a restriction site or anchor edge at which an inverse primer becomes valid. The design should instead expose primer coordinates, 3′ binding sequence, orientation, local ambiguity/repeat status, predicted product path, and distance to the ligated junction. A candidate that meets ordinary primer thermodynamics but crosses a cut, depends on an unconfirmed repeat copy, or leaves the intended flank outside the selected fragment is not a valid inverse-PCR design ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)).

Primer3 may be used as a named screening overlay. Its constraints cover primer length, melting temperature, GC percentage, product size, self-complementarity, hairpin or dimer risk, positional constraints, and ectopic priming; its defaults are software defaults rather than biological laws. NCBI Primer-BLAST should be used against an explicitly selected database and taxon because it checks the forward–reverse pair as well as forward–forward and reverse–reverse unintended pairings ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html), [NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/primertool.cgi/primerinfo.html)).

For the whole-plasmid Q5 mutagenesis branch, NEB specifies a non-overlapping back-to-back 5′ arrangement for exponential amplification. A substitution is placed in the mutagenic primer with at least `10` complementary nucleotides on its 3′ side; a deletion uses primers flanking the deleted sequence; insertions up to `6 nt` can be placed in a primer 5′ end, while larger insertions are split between both primers. These rules are branch-specific and must not be applied to the genomic flanking-sequence branch ([NEB Q5 Site-Directed Mutagenesis manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale0552.pdf?hash=D53D03FFEEE28166D56BDF2BD463A69B&rev=04a4b1ab8cfd4578b02043ebaf468b28)).

The current record must distinguish a true outward pair from two fully overlapping mutagenic primers. Both are sometimes called inverse primers, but their amplification and repair mechanisms differ; the literature describes non-overlapping, partially overlapping, and fully overlapping formats as separate arrangements ([Inverse PCR for Point Mutation Introduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540701/)).

## Polymerase and amplification branches

Polymerase choice is conditional on product length, template complexity, GC or secondary structure, desired fidelity, and whether the product will be cloned or sequenced. A high-fidelity enzyme is appropriate for mutagenesis or a sequence-claim workflow, but no single polymerase or cycle programme is scientifically justified for every inverse-PCR branch. The application should therefore retain polymerase, buffer, dNTP, additive, annealing model, extension rate, cycle count, and final-extension settings as a named protocol bundle ([Thermo Fisher PCR methods](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html)).

The NEB Q5 branch specifies a `25 µL` or `50 µL` reaction, `0.5 µM` each primer, `1–25 ng` template in the kit's `25 µL` format, `25` cycles, `98 °C` denaturation, `50–72 °C` annealing, and `20–30 s/kb` extension. NEB also allows a shorter `10 s/kb` extension for simple plasmid templates in its general Q5 guidance. These ranges are Q5 protocol values and do not define the performance of other polymerases ([NEB Q5 mutagenesis quick protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/protocols/2014/03/21/q5-site-directed-mutagenesis-kit-without-competent-cells-quick-protocol-e0552), [NEB Q5 PCR protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/pcr-using-q5-high-fidelity-dna-polymerase-m0491)).

The Thermo Phusion branch specifies `0.5 µM` each primer, `200 µM` of each dNTP, `0.02 U/µL` polymerase, `25–35` cycles, `98 °C` initial denaturation, `15–30 s/kb` extension, and a `5–10 min` final extension. Thermo describes `3%` DMSO as a GC-rich-template option and cautions against DMSO for very low-GC or `≥20 kb` amplicons; Phusion produces blunt-end products and does not support dUTP-containing templates. These are product-specific constraints and must remain a separate branch ([Thermo Phusion product information](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0012395_Phusion_HighFidelity_DNAPolymerase_UG.pdf)).

For template-removal in mutagenesis, DpnI is valid only when the parental DNA contains a susceptible methylated recognition site. NEB states that DpnI cleaves methylated recognition sites, that dam-methylated DNA is a substrate, and that overlapping CpG methylation can block cleavage; hemimethylated dam sites are cleaved much more slowly. The record must therefore capture host or in-vitro methylation state and must not promise parental depletion for unmethylated synthetic DNA or an incompatible methylation context ([NEB DpnI](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/r0176-dpni), [NEB DpnI methylation FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/what-s-the-difference-between-dpni-dpnii-mboi-and-sau3ai)).

## Controls, validation and failure-mode coverage

The minimum control design is branch-specific. A restriction-ligation recovery should include a digest/QC observation, a circularization blank, a no-template PCR control, and a non-circularized or linear-template control when enrichment for circles is claimed. The SIP study carried the water blank through circularization, used a separate PCR NTC, and ran a linear blunt-ended DNA control; the linear control exposed minor products that would otherwise be misread as successful circular-template amplification ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

For a mutagenesis branch, the control set should include a parental-template control, a no-template PCR control, a recovery control that bypasses or tests the DpnI/ligation step where appropriate, and a positive transformation or known-plasmid control. The intended mutation, the complete amplified region and any junction introduced during repair must be confirmed by sequencing; a band of the expected size cannot establish sequence correctness ([NEB Q5 control reaction](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/protocols/2014/03/21/protocol-for-control-reaction-e0552), [Inverse PCR for Point Mutation Introduction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540701/)).

The validation record must separate analytical checks from sequence claims. Analytical checks include complete in-silico cut-map enumeration, preservation of both primer sites on a candidate fragment, orientation and uniqueness checks, product-path calculation, and an explicit assessment of linear background and concatemer risk. Experimental checks include digest or fragment QC, circularization performance, control results, gel or capillary size assessment, and sequencing across each intended junction and flank ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

If the module is used for a quantitative or diagnostic claim, the validation scope must additionally declare matrix, inclusivity/exclusivity where relevant, precision, linearity, detection or quantification limits, robustness and measurement uncertainty. ISO 20395 covers these validation dimensions for qPCR and dPCR rather than establishing inverse-PCR-specific values, while ISO 22174:2024 provides general PCR requirements and reproducibility intent for its defined application domain. Neither standard supplies a universal inverse-PCR acceptance threshold ([ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/06/78/67893.html?browse=tc), [ISO 22174:2024](https://www.iso.org/cms/%20render/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/08/17/81777.html)).

The failure-mode matrix must preserve at least these distinctions because the reported inverse-PCR artifacts arise from different topology, end-chemistry, background, and polymerase mechanisms ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497), [NEB T4 DNA Ligase protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/dna-ligation-with-t4-dna-ligase-m0202)).

| Failure mode | Mechanistic interpretation | Required disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Enzyme cuts the anchor or separates the primer sites | no single candidate circle contains the assumed pair | reject or branch by cut map; do not rescue by ordinary-PCR geometry ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract)) |
| Incomplete digest or wrong methylation context | parental or uncut molecules remain and may create false products | record digest/QC evidence and retain as unresolved until verified ([Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html), [NEB DpnI](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/r0176-dpni)) |

Two primary flanking-sequence applications add useful, but non-universal, assay overlays. Yamada et al. used approximately `200 ng` of digested DNA in `80 µL` ligation buffer at `16 °C` overnight, then amplified in a `50 µL` reaction with `0.2 mM` dNTPs, `0.2 µM` each primer and `2.5 U` LA Taq using `94 °C/1 min`, `50` cycles of `94 °C/30 s`, `60 °C/30 s` and `72 °C/3 min`, followed by `72 °C/7 min`. A separate integron study selected `3–6 kb` restriction fragments and used `94 °C/1 min`, `30` cycles of `94 °C/30 s`, `55 °C/1 min` and `72 °C/5–10 min` (`1 min/kb`), followed by `72 °C/10 min`. These records demonstrate protocol diversity and must remain named overlays, not silently merged into the inverse-PCR defaults ([Yamada et al., 2008](https://enviromicrojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1462-2920.2007.01518.x), [Use of Inverse PCR for Analysis of Class 1 Integrons](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/aac.00988-10)).

## Current application evidence: inverse PCR to Sanger flank identification

A 2024 Cold Spring Harbor Protocols article provides a current application-level confirmation of the core inverse-PCR topology for mapping unknown transposon insertions: genomic DNA is restriction-digested, ligated under conditions favoring self-circularization, amplified with inside-out primers near one or both transposon termini, and the adjacent unknown chromosomal sequence is identified by Sanger sequencing. This recent protocol does not establish new universal enzyme, fragment-size or cycling numbers, but it strengthens the module's explicit requirement that digest identity, circularization state, outward-primer location and downstream sequence confirmation remain linked provenance fields rather than being inferred from the oligo pair alone ([Figueroa-Bossi, Balbontín & Bossi, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37188521/)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

The cited literature and vendor documentation do not establish a universal restriction enzyme, cut frequency, fragment-size window, circle-yield target, ligation concentration, primer-to-junction distance, maximum flank length, or polymerase/cycling recipe for all inverse-PCR applications. The software must keep these values unresolved until a named assay, template, chemistry and protocol provide them; ordinary-PCR defaults are not evidence for this module ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html)).

No universal acceptance threshold was found for residual linear template, concatemer or chimera fraction, minimum circularization efficiency, or the amount of product needed to support a complete flank claim. The reported SIP fractions are observations from one fragmentation/sequencing workflow, not portable quality limits ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

The methylation state of an arbitrary parental plasmid, the completeness of a restriction digest, the fraction of correctly circularized molecules, and the effect of sample-specific inhibitors cannot be inferred from sequence geometry alone. They require declared provenance and wet-lab controls; DpnI is not a general substitute for parental-template verification ([NEB DpnI](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/r0176-dpni), [Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497)).

There is no universal rule that converts an expected gel size into a confirmed flanking sequence. Sequencing coverage, read direction, repetitive sequence, chimeric ligation, and the chosen readout platform remain assay-specific. A result without junction/flank sequence evidence must remain `unconfirmed` ([Ochman et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7866865/), [Pavlopoulos](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065444/)).

**Internal software-contract gaps:** The following intentionally have no external scientific citation: typed per-end phosphate state, explicit linear-control provenance, named branch selection, named restriction-enzyme identity with zero sites in the known anchor, primer-to-ligation-junction path serialization, protocol-scoped numeric activation, and a result status that distinguishes `predicted`, `amplified`, `sequence-confirmed`, and `unresolved` (internal software-contract; no external source).

## Evidence

The primary protocols support outward primers on a compatible circular template, but they do not establish one universal restriction enzyme, circularization method, end-repair chemistry, polymerase, or maximum recoverable flank. These remain explicit workflow choices and are represented as protocol-scoped records ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [Pavlopoulos](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065444/)).

The evidence supports two different meanings of “inverse PCR”: flank recovery from a circularized restriction/random fragment and whole-plasmid mutagenesis from a circular plasmid. The former depends on a physical circle population and cut map; the latter depends on linear-product recovery and often parental template depletion. Treating both as one unconditional recipe would be an overclaim ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [NEB Q5 Site-Directed Mutagenesis](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale0552.pdf?hash=D53D03FFEEE28166D56BDF2BD463A69B&rev=04a4b1ab8cfd4578b02043ebaf468b28)).

## Verification record

**Implementation:** validate outward orientation, the complete intact known anchor, zero internal recognition sites for the named enzyme, circularization provenance, per-end chemistry, methylation branch, expected product path and unknown-flank bounds ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/2/pdb.prot095166.abstract), [NEB molecular cloning technical guide](https://www.neb.com/en-us/-/media/nebus/files/brochures/molcloning_tech_guide.pdf?hash=CE61408673A0B0908875BB7F41043B15&la=en-us&rev=4199dd98d543418484e7da84c7bb3580)).

**Internal test contract:** cover missing enzyme identity, enzyme recognition sites inside the complete known anchor, removed internal-cut/one-sided request fields, outward-pair geometry across the original endpoint join, unresolved versus exact self-ligated-fragment length, reversed unknown-flank bounds, missing preparation provenance, circular/linear control state, reverse-complement anchor input, and product-path ambiguity. These are acceptance criteria for the current implementation (internal software-contract; no external source).

**Wet-lab gate:** require digest or fragment QC, circularization blank, NTC, linear/non-circular control when relevant, and sequence evidence across each claimed junction/flank ([Alquezar-Planas et al.](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.13497), [Thermo Fisher inverse-PCR overview](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/pcr-education/pcr-reagents-enzymes/pcr-methods.html)).

**Limitation:** this profile is `limited`; a computed pair or expected-size band is not a confirmed flanking sequence, mutant plasmid, or junction. The product must be sequenced and interpreted against the declared enzyme, self-ligated-fragment preparation and branch ([Ochman et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7866865/), [Pavlopoulos](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22065444/)).
