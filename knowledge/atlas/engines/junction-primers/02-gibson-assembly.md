## Scope

Gibson Assembly joins DNA fragments through designed terminal overlaps that become compatible junctions in one reaction. The overlap is not an ordinary PCR primer tail: it is a typed assembly interface whose orientation, fragment order and junction sequence must be retained in the output ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)). Gibson's separate 2009 yeast oligonucleotide-assembly paper is retained only as adjacent sequence-assembly evidence; it describes in-vivo yeast assembly of overlapping oligonucleotides and is not the source for the canonical in-vitro Gibson three-enzyme chemistry ([Gibson 2009 yeast oligo assembly](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19745056/)).

The module owns junction geometry, fragment order, terminal overlaps, optional amplification tails and assembly validation. It must not claim that a correctly designed overlap proves an assembled clone, correct sequence or lack of rearrangement ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

When an amplified fragment is supplied as the exact sequence to be assembled, its PCR product boundary is part of the fragment contract: the forward primer must begin at the fragment's first base and the reverse primer must terminate at its last base. A free ordinary pair search can move both primers inward and silently produce a subfragment, so the runtime now uses a pinned terminal-pair search for this input form. This is consistent with the vendor workflow in which PCR primers generate the fragments and add the assembly homology; it does not remove the need to provide a longer source template when the intended PCR product includes sequence outside the supplied fragment ([NEB Gibson Assembly Protocol E5510](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/gibson-assembly-protocol-e5510?pdf=true), [Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

## Vendor protocol overlay: NEB Gibson Assembly E5510

The following values are a named NEB E5510 protocol overlay, not defaults for every Gibson-like or HiFi assembly reaction. For a 2–3-fragment assembly, NEB specifies a total fragment input of `0.02–0.5 pmol`; for a 4–6-fragment assembly it specifies `0.2–1 pmol`. The vendor also describes `50–100 ng` vector with a `2–3-fold` molar excess of each insert, and a `5-fold` excess for inserts shorter than `200 bp`. The assembly reaction is incubated at `50 °C` for `15 min` for 2–3 fragments or `60 min` for 4–6 fragments ([NEB Gibson Assembly Protocol E5510](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/gibson-assembly-protocol-e5510?pdf=true)).

When `assembly_protocol=neb-e5510` is selected together with the `gibson` chemistry and 2–6 fragments, the runtime emits these values as a named protocol object with the kit identifier, fragment-count branch, vector/insert molar inputs, PCR-volume ceiling and incubation branch. With no selection, no kit recipe is emitted. The overlay does not overwrite the module's ordinary PCR annealing fields or become a universal overlap rule; a 7–12-fragment plan is intentionally refused under this overlay because the cited E5510 branch does not define that range ([NEB Gibson Assembly Protocol E5510](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/gibson-assembly-protocol-e5510?pdf=true)).

The selected E5510 overlay follows the current direct protocol/fragment-count FAQ for executable overlap planning: `15–25 bp` for the 2–3-fragment/15-minute branch and `20–80 bp` for the 4–6-fragment/60-minute branch. A separate E5510 FAQ/manual answer states `≤25 bp` for 15 min and up to `40 bp` for 60 min; that narrower value is preserved below as source-scoped conflicting evidence and is not silently substituted for the direct protocol. This prevents the original Gibson method's historical overlap examples from leaking into a named NEB kit calculation and prevents the two current NEB guidance branches from being averaged into an invented range ([NEB Gibson Assembly Protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/gibson-assembly-master-mix-assembly), [NEB fragment-count FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en/faqs/are-there-any-differences-between-the-requirements-for-1-2-fragment-assemblies-versus-4-6), [NEB longest-overlap FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/what-are-the-longest-overlaps-that-can-be-used-with-this-method1)).

## Vendor protocol overlay: NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly

NEB's current NEBuilder HiFi protocol is a separate named overlay, even though it belongs to the same broad overlap-directed assembly family. The standard protocol is explicitly for dsDNA fragments `>120 bp` with varied overlaps of `15–30 bp`; fragments smaller than `120 bp` are directed to a dedicated ssDNA-bridge or annealed-oligonucleotide protocol rather than being forced through the standard dsDNA branch ([NEBuilder HiFi reaction protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/2014/11/26/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-reaction-protocol?pdf=true)).

For `2–3` fragments, the protocol recommends `15–20 bp` overlaps, a vector:insert molar ratio of `1:2`, total DNA of `0.03–0.2 pmol` and `50–100 ng` vector with a `2-fold` excess of each insert; inserts shorter than `200 bp` use a `5-fold` molar excess. For `4–6` fragments it recommends `20–30 bp` overlaps, approximately equimolar fragments (`1:1`) and `0.2–0.5 pmol` total DNA, with `0.05 pmol` per fragment suggested in the example ([NEBuilder HiFi reaction protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-reaction-protocol)).

The same protocol specifies a `20 µL` reaction containing `10 µL` NEBuilder HiFi Master Mix and water to volume; unpurified PCR product should not exceed `20%` of the reaction, i.e. `4 µL` in a `20 µL` reaction. Incubation is `50 °C/15 min` for `2–3` fragments or `50 °C/60 min` for `4–6` fragments. The protocol also gives `pmol = (ng × 1000)/(bp × 650 Da)` and worked examples of `50 ng` of `5000 bp` DNA ≈ `0.015 pmol` and `50 ng` of `500 bp` DNA ≈ `0.15 pmol` ([NEBuilder HiFi reaction protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-reaction-protocol)).

**Chemistry-capability boundary.** NEBuilder HiFi is not a synonym for Gibson Assembly. NEB states that NEBuilder HiFi can remove 5′- and 3′-end mismatch sequences, and its 3′-mismatch demonstration reports efficient removal of up to `10 bp` from a 3′ end before joining. NEB separately warns that primers/designs relying on NEBuilder-specific capabilities may not work with Gibson Assembly. PCRStudio must therefore represent mismatch-tolerant end processing as a chemistry capability flag rather than accepting such a junction under generic `gibson` chemistry ([NEBuilder HiFi mismatch-removal guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/video-library/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-removal-of-3-end-mismatches), [NEB chemistry comparison FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-us/faqs/i-am-not-sure-whether-to-choose-nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-or-neb-gibson-assembly)).

**R16 internal implementation record.** NEBuilder HiFi is now a distinct executable method with explicit `E2621`, `E5520` and `E2623` protocol identities, and typed ssDNA/annealed-oligo inputs are representable. The `<120 bp` guidance remains an explicit input/routing consideration rather than an automatic hidden conversion, and chemistry-specific end-mismatch removal remains diagnostic/reference capability unless a junction explicitly models that transformation. E5510 values are never inherited by the NEBuilder branch and NEBuilder-only capabilities are never relabelled as Gibson ([NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/0001/01/01/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-reaction-setup-protocol-e2621)).

## Vendor protocol overlay: Thermo Fisher GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi

GeneArt HiFi is a third, separate chemistry record. The current Rev. D user guide describes up to `5` DNA inserts plus a vector in a predetermined order, while current product pages summarize this as “up to `6` fragments.” PCRStudio must therefore distinguish `insert_count` from `total_part_count` and must not treat those two vendor phrasings as contradictory limits ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf), [GeneArt HiFi product page](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/A46628)). The same guide describes a nominal fragment-size range of `0.5–32 kb`; it also states that smaller `0.1–0.5 kb` fragments can be cloned but may show efficiency as low as `40%`, depending on factors including fragment count. These are vendor performance observations, not a sequence-only success probability ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

The guide recommends `20–40 bp` terminal homology when a fragment is `≤8 kb` and `40–100 bp` when it is `>8 kb`. It states that longer overlaps can improve higher-order assembly, that selecting overlap regions with `Tm >50 °C` can improve efficiency, and that the 3′ end of each PCR primer must remain fragment-specific. A typical primer is approximately `65 nt`, comprising `20–40 nt` of 5′ homology plus `18–25 nt` of fragment-specific sequence; the fragment-specific segment is advised to remain between `40%` and `60%` GC and to avoid tandem repeats, homopolymers and high secondary structure ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

The `20–40 bp` homology can be split between adjacent PCR-generated fragments in different proportions, including `12+18`, `15+15` or `25+5` for a `30 bp` total. In a `0+40` split, one primer can contain only `18–25 nt` of fragment-specific sequence with no overhang, while the adjacent primer can be `58–65 nt` (`40 nt` homology plus `18–25 nt` specificity). These are topology and primer-length alternatives: the runtime must not require both primers to carry the complete overlap, and the `65 nt` example must not become a universal primer-length rule ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

For PCR-generated fragments, GeneArt recommends `0.1–10 ng` circular-plasmid template to reduce background, or `5–100 ng` when using *E. coli* or human genomic DNA; its listed PCR primers are `100 µM` stocks. For vector linearization by restriction digestion, the guide describes `2–3 h` with extension to overnight when needed; a small-scale example uses `2–5 µg` vector with `30–50 U` enzyme in `100–200 µL`, while a batch example uses `20–50 µg` vector with `150–300 U` of each enzyme in `400–800 µL` and stores aliquots at `−20 °C`. These digest quantities belong to the GeneArt linearization branch and must not leak into the assembly reaction itself ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

For direct PCR assembly, the guide uses up to `1 µL` of each vector/insert PCR product, limits the combined PCR volume to `4 µL`, adds `10 µL` GeneArt HiFi Master Mix and fills to `20 µL`; incubation is `50 °C/15 min`. For purified fragments, it recommends a `1:1` molar ratio and gives `0.08 pmol` vector plus `0.08 pmol` of each insert for a `1–3`-insert example, with up to `10 µL` combined DNA, `10 µL` master mix and water to `20 µL`; incubation is `50 °C/15 min` for `1–3` inserts and `50 °C/60 min` for `4–5` inserts. Its molarity conversion is `pmol ≈ (ng × 1000)/(fragment length in bp × 660)`, which remains distinct from NEB's `650 Da` divisor ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

GeneArt records a direct-PCR ceiling of `4 µL` PCR product per `20 µL` reaction, a `1:4 (v/v)` dilution example (`3 µL` assembly plus `12 µL` water) before chemical transformation, a stated optimum of `5-fold` dilution, `5 min` on ice, `50 µL` plating and overnight incubation at `37 °C`. The experimental outline recommends screening `4–12` colonies; the guide further advises more colony screening for higher-order insert assemblies. These are downstream workflow values and must not be converted into a predicted cloning probability ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)). Current Thermo Fisher product guidance also recommends high-copy vectors for small inserts `<10 kb` and low-copy vectors for large inserts or toxic genes; this is a vector/propagation branch, not an overlap-design rule ([GeneArt HiFi product page](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/A46628)).

**GeneArt EX family boundary.** Thermo Fisher's GeneArt Gibson Assembly EX family is not the same executable recipe as HiFi. The current EX user guide states assembly of up to `15` DNA inserts plus a vector in a predetermined order, while the product page summarizes the specification as “up to `15` fragments”; this terminology difference must be retained through separate `insert_count` and `total_part_count` fields rather than silently normalized. EX uses a single-tube, two-step workflow, with `20–40 bp` overlap guidance for fragments `≤8 kb` and `40–100 bp` for fragments `>8 kb`. PCRStudio may expose EX only through a distinct chemistry/protocol identity; the HiFi one-step reaction recipe must not be reused for an EX request ([GeneArt Gibson Assembly EX User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019063_GeneArtGibsonEX_UG.pdf), [GeneArt EX product page](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/A46634)).

**R16 internal implementation record.** The runtime protocol selector now recognizes `neb-e5510` under method `gibson` and `neb-nebuilder-e2621`, `neb-nebuilder-e5520` and `neb-nebuilder-e2623` under the separate `nebuilder` method. GeneArt HiFi Rev. D and GeneArt EX values remain documentation-only until distinct protocol identifiers, request schemas and result contracts are implemented; they must not be emitted merely because the user selected a homology-assembly method ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

## Chemistry and topology rules

The canonical Gibson mechanism is a coordinated three-activity reaction: a 5′ exonuclease recesses the fragment ends to expose complementary single- stranded regions, a DNA polymerase fills gaps after those regions anneal, and a DNA ligase seals the remaining nicks. The activities are coupled in one reaction, so the design record must keep the overlap graph separate from the identity of the chemistry that performs chew-back, fill-in and nick sealing ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB Gibson Assembly Protocol E5510](https://www.neb.com/protocols/gibson-assembly-protocol-e5510?device=pdf)).

Each internal junction is a bidirectional compatibility constraint, not merely a string attached to one primer: the terminal sequence exposed from fragment `i` must be reverse-complementary to the exposed terminal sequence of fragment `i+1` in the selected orientation. A circular construct additionally requires the final-to-first junction, while a linear construct must not silently invent that edge. Repeated or palindromic terminal sequences can admit unintended paths; the NEB manual specifically advises unique overlaps for repetitive sequences and warns that highly palindromic overlaps can reduce recombinant colonies ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

The assembly output therefore needs two distinct sequence views: the intended construct sequence after junction resolution, and the primer oligo sequences including any 5′ homology tails. The annealing segment is the part that binds the PCR template; the homology tail creates the future assembly interface and must not be scored as though it were an ordinary template-annealing base. This separation is a design invariant, not a vendor-specific numerical default ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

## Design-planner and end-geometry precedents

NEB's current Assembly Tool 2.0 is an independent design precedent rather than proof of PCRStudio equivalence. R16 now covers several corresponding concepts—separate Gibson/NEBuilder method identity, linear/circular constructs, typed fragments including restriction-derived and oligo inputs, ordered construct graph and oligo output—but still does not claim every vendor-tool operation such as proprietary ranking, automatic site-regeneration policy, arbitrary fragment editing, hidden mismatch removal or long-fragment splitting. Each feature is claimable only when a corresponding PCRStudio field and regression contract exists ([NEBuilder Assembly Tool 2.0](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/video-library/nebuilder-assembly-tool-20-whats-new), [NEBuilder restriction-digest workflow](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/video-library/nebuilder-assembly-tool-20-restriction-enzyme-digest)).

For a restriction-linearized vector, overlap extraction must be based on the actual terminal sequence and end geometry rather than blindly counting the recognition site or assuming a blunt end. NEB's primer-design guidance treats 5′ overhang, 3′ overhang and blunt-ended restriction products explicitly and allows spacer bases when needed to preserve the intended junction/reading frame. The engine should therefore retain `restriction_end_geometry`, exact exposed terminal sequence, optional spacer and site-regeneration policy as typed provenance ([NEB primer design and fragment assembly guidance](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/video-library/primer-design-and-fragment-assembly-using-gibson-assembly)).

## Controls, validation and failure modes

| Check or control | Required interpretation | Evidence and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive assembly control | Run the kit positive control when a transformation yields no colonies; a successful result separates mix/transformation failure from design or sample failure | NEB states that successful positive-control assembly demonstrates functional mix and suitable transformation conditions ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| Competent-cell control | Transform the supplied pUC19 control to verify cell competency independently of assembly chemistry | NEB specifies `100 pg` pUC19 and reports an expected `1–3 × 10^9 colonies/µg` after overnight incubation at `37 °C` on LB-ampicillin ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| NTC / no-insert / vector-only branch | Distinguish contamination, uncut-vector background and self-assembly from a true insert-containing product; the exact control composition is protocol- and vector-specific | DpnI treatment, double digestion and vector-only background reduction are described as troubleshooting branches, not universal success criteria ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| Input-fragment gel/QC | Confirm a single band and expected size before assembly; non-specific bands, primer-dimers and excess primers can lower specificity and should trigger cleanup or gel purification | NEB recommends gel purification when PCR products contain non-specific products, primer-dimers or large quantities of unused primers ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| Assembly-reaction gel | Correct-size assembled material with disappearance of input fragments supports physical assembly, but does not prove sequence correctness or biological function | NEB lists gel analysis as a troubleshooting check; construct correctness still requires clone-level confirmation ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf), [Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)) |
| Junction PCR | Use primers flanking each junction or the assembled product to detect missing, reordered or incorrect joins; a positive band is not a substitute for sequence confirmation | NEB recommends PCR flanking the assembled product as a success check, while the primary work documents sequence-level variation in assembled constructs ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf), [Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)) |
| Full-construct confirmation | Confirm all junctions and the complete intended sequence by an orthogonal sequencing plan appropriate to construct size and repeats | The primary assembly study reports sequence errors/variation after cloning, so junction-only evidence cannot establish a correct full construct ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)) |
| Vector-only colonies | Investigate uncut plasmid carryover, incomplete digest, self-annealing ends and PCR template carryover; DpnI is applicable to methylated plasmid template carryover, not a universal substitute for vector QC | NEB lists DpnI for PCR-template carryover and recommends two restriction enzymes or purification for incompletely cut vector ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| Low/no colonies | Check overlap correctness, input concentration, PCR-volume burden, cell competency, transformation dilution and insert toxicity before changing the overlap algorithm | These are separate troubleshooting causes in the NEB manual; no single cause or universal rescue value is established ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)) |
| Incorrect-size or mixed colonies | Recheck PCR specificity, fragment identity and repetitive terminal sequence; confirm candidates by junction and full-construct sequencing | NEB associates incorrect inserts with non-specific PCR products and repetitive biology, while the primary paper documents that assembly/propagation can introduce sequence variation ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf), [Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)) |

The module must report the validation state as a staged claim: `designed` means the overlap graph and sequence reconstruction are internally consistent; `assembled-in-vitro` requires physical reaction evidence; `transformant` requires recovered colonies; and `sequence-confirmed` requires the selected orthogonal sequence checks. These labels prevent a gel band, colony count or positive junction PCR from being promoted to proof of a correct construct ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

## Adjacent sequence-assembly evidence that must not be promoted to Gibson chemistry

Gibson's separate 2009 yeast oligonucleotide-assembly study demonstrated that *S. cerevisiae* could assemble at least `38` overlapping single-stranded oligonucleotides together with a linear double-stranded vector in one transformation; the oligonucleotides could overlap by as few as `20 bp` and could be as long as `200 nt`. These values are useful historical evidence about overlap-based sequence assembly and synthesis, but the mechanism is yeast in-vivo recombination/assembly rather than the in-vitro Gibson Master Mix chemistry. They must not become Gibson overlap defaults, fragment-count limits or reaction-performance claims ([Gibson 2009 yeast oligo assembly](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19745056/)).

## Additional official numeric evidence

The NEB manual contains several operational values that must remain attached to the E2611/E5510 product family rather than becoming engine-wide defaults. It reports productive assembly with overlaps as short as `12 bp`, recommends at least `15 bp` with overlap `Tm ≥48 °C`, and distinguishes a `≤25 bp` overlap branch for a `15 min` reaction from overlaps up to `40 bp` when the reaction is extended to `60 min` ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

The same manual states that the chemistry is optimized at `50 °C` but has been shown to work between `40–50 °C`; it generally discourages reactions shorter than `15 min`, reports possible improvement with incubation up to `4 h`, and explicitly says not to incubate overnight. These are product-manual branches, not evidence for a universal kinetic optimum across other Gibson-like mixes ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

For downstream transformation, current NEB protocols distinguish chemical and electrocompetent workflows. The E5510 electrocompetent protocol uses a `3-fold` dilution (`5 µL` assembly + `10 µL` water) and `1 µL` of diluted product with `50 µL` cells, followed by `950 µL` SOC and `37 °C/60 min` recovery at `250 rpm`; the E5510 chemical protocol uses `2 µL` chilled assembly product, `30 min` on ice, `42 °C/30 s` heat shock, `2 min` on ice, `950 µL` SOC and `37 °C/60 min` recovery before plating `100 µL`. The E5510 chemical protocol is stated for assembled products `<20 kb` in supplied 5-alpha cells, while NEB's current Gibson guidance recommends 10-beta cells for assembled products `>10 kb`; the `15 kb` insert + `5.4 kb` vector = `20.4 kb` example is a demonstrated case, not the recommendation threshold ([NEB E5510 electrocompetent protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/electrocompetent-cells-transformation-protocol-e5510), [NEB E5510 chemical protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/gibson-assembly-transformation-protocol-e5510), [NEB large-construct FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/how-large-a-dna-fragment-can-i-assemble)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

The literature and vendor documents consulted here do not establish one sequence-only equation that predicts assembly yield from overlap length, GC, fragment number, fragment size and topology across all chemistries. The atlas therefore keeps overlap selection protocol-scoped and reports warnings rather than inventing a universal score or cutoff ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf), [GeneArt Gibson Assembly HiFi User Guide, revision D](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0019062_GeneArtGibsonHiFi_UG.pdf)).

The E5510 documentation itself contains scope-specific overlap guidance that must remain visible as a conflict: its FAQ reports `15–25 nt` for the `15 min` branch and `20–80 nt` for the `1 h` branch, while another FAQ answer limits the same product to `≤25 bp` at `15 min` and up to `40 bp` at `60 min`. The runtime must bind one selected protocol interpretation and preserve the alternative as source-scoped evidence; it must not silently merge `40` and `80 bp` into a new value ([NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

No universal numeric threshold is established here for palindromicity, secondary structure, repeat uniqueness, template purity, transformant count, acceptable vector background, or full-construct sequencing coverage. These remain explicit assay- and construct-dependent inputs because the primary paper and vendor manuals describe examples and troubleshooting branches rather than a common cross-platform acceptance standard ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB Gibson Assembly Master Mix/E5510 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale2611_e5510.pdf)).

**R16 internal software-contract boundary:** Gibson E5510 and NEBuilder HiFi (`E2621`, `E5520`, `E2623`) now have distinct executable method/protocol identities; typed fragment production modes, construct-graph output, fragment molarity provenance, declared feature/frame diagnostics and a bounded bench-evidence schema are represented. NEBuilder-specific behavior is never relabelled as Gibson. GeneArt HiFi/EX remain reference/watchlist authorities unless separately promoted, Golden Gate/type-IIS remains outside this engine, and no bench outcome is inferred from the computational assembly graph ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

## Evidence

The original in-vitro Gibson paper supports the one-pot overlap-directed three-enzyme assembly mechanism; the separate yeast oligonucleotide-assembly paper provides adjacent multi-fragment sequence-assembly context. Neither source supports hiding current vendor-specific recipe values in the shared engine or claiming a correct clone from sequence design alone ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [Gibson 2009 yeast oligo assembly](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19745056/)).

## Verification record

**Implementation:** validate the overlap graph, detect incompatible or duplicated junctions, preserve fragment provenance and emit an ordered construct map ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

**Test:** include exact junction reconstruction, reverse-complement orientation cases, missing-fragment refusal, chemistry-capability mismatch cases and full-construct validation-plan checks ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/), [NEB chemistry comparison FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-us/faqs/i-am-not-sure-whether-to-choose-nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-or-neb-gibson-assembly)).

**Limitation:** this profile is `limited`; assembly efficiency, clone correctness and rearrangement frequency require the selected protocol and bench evidence ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

## R16 implementation contract

R16 uses a typed ordered-fragment graph. Fragment production modes include PCR-amplified, restriction-digest, synthetic dsDNA, ssDNA oligo, annealed oligos and existing-linear material, each with orientation and provenance. Circular constructs are evaluated independently of the display origin; graph/junction semantics must therefore remain rotation-invariant ([Gibson et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19363495/)).

`gibson` and `nebuilder-hifi` are separate methods. `neb-e5510` may use only E5510 authority, while `neb-nebuilder-e2621`, `neb-nebuilder-e5520` and `neb-nebuilder-e2623` resolve only NEBuilder authority. Numeric overlap, fragment-input and chemistry-capability values cannot cross-inherit. Declared restriction-linearized vectors, feature/CDS/ORF integrity, approximate molarity and junction/repeat/secondary-structure diagnostics remain traceable to their inputs. Clone verification is measured evidence, never inferred from a predicted construct ([NEBuilder HiFi DNA Assembly](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/0001/01/01/nebuilder-hifi-dna-assembly-reaction-setup-protocol-e2621)).
