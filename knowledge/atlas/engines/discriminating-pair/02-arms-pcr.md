## Scope

ARMS-PCR places the interrogated allele at the 3-prime end of an allele-specific primer so extension is intended to differ between the reference and alternate templates. A deliberate additional mismatch can tune discrimination, but its effect depends on mismatch identity, position, neighbouring sequence and polymerase; it is not a universal magic base ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Kwok et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2179874/)).

The module owns allele orientation, terminal mismatch design, common-primer placement, product interpretation and control requirements. The shared engine owns only the variant/competition contract; it must not silently report a genotype from primer alignment alone ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/)).

The input record must preserve the reference-sequence accession and assembly, the strand on which the variant is represented, the allele-specific oligo sequences written 5′→3′, and the exact allele/base pair seen at the primer terminus. NCBI recommends a RefSeq accession where possible for primer-specificity checking, and Primer-BLAST evaluates forward/reverse, forward/forward and reverse/reverse products against the selected database; dbSNP is an authoritative source for RefSNP mappings and reported allele/population information, not a substitute for assay validation ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/), [NCBI dbSNP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/)).

## Extended mismatch-selection rules

The additional mismatch is a search dimension, not a fixed recipe. Published allele-specific designs place it at different positions near the 3-prime terminus, including the penultimate and antepenultimate positions, and report that both mismatch identity and local sequence context affect the balance between suppressing the non-target allele and retaining amplification of the intended allele ([Melt-MAMA functional study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3306377/), [IL28B allele-specific PCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23052312/)). The engine should enumerate the declared candidate positions and bases, retain the reason each was accepted or rejected, and never silently choose a “best” mismatch from a universal table ([Melt-MAMA functional study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3306377/)).

Mismatch strength must be evaluated as a primer-template pair. The tetra-primer literature reports a practical relationship between the native terminal mismatch class and the added mismatch class, but this is a design heuristic that still requires matched/non-matched control reactions; it is not a thermodynamic constant or a portable discrimination ratio ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)). A result therefore reports the exact terminal pair, deliberate pair, position from the 3-prime end, polymerase and control evidence instead of reducing the chemistry to one score ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/)).

The mismatch class itself must remain evidence-scoped. Kwok et al. measured approximately `100-fold` lower overall product yield for selected `A:G`, `G:A` and `C:C` mismatches and approximately `20-fold` lower yield for `A:A` in a specific Taq/HIV-1 model; they also reported substantial amplification for some terminal `T` mismatches. Huang et al. measured purified-Taq extension efficiencies spanning roughly `10^-2` to `<10^-6` of a matched control, with the `C(primer)–T(template)` mismatch unusually permissive in that system. These observations support a ranking vocabulary and a warning about context dependence, not a universal claim that a terminal mismatch blocks extension in every polymerase or template ([Kwok et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2179874/), [Huang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1408758/)).

The original ARMS rationale and the later Taq studies therefore support a default 3′→5′-exonuclease-negative Taq branch, but not a universal enzyme prohibition. Platinum II Taq is explicitly described as lacking 3′→5′ exonuclease activity, while QIAGEN HotStar HiFidelity and NEB Q5 are described as proofreading enzymes with that activity; an unmodified proofreading enzyme can remove or alter a mismatched 3′ residue and must not inherit the ordinary ARMS call rule without validation ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Platinum II Taq User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/manuals/MAN0017534_Platinum_II_Taq_HS_DNA_Pol_UG.pdf), [HotStar HiFidelity PCR Handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/us/resources/kithandbook/en-hotstar-hifidelity-pcr-handbook), [NEB Q5](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/m0491-q5-high-fidelity-dna-polymerase)).

A proofreading-compatible exception must be represented as a different chemistry branch, not as a relaxed version of the default. Hu et al. used a 3′ phosphorothioate primer modification with a proofreading polymerase, reported elimination of mismatch amplification in their assay, and validated the approach against PCR-RFLP on `60` human DNA samples covering `2` SNPs; the branch remains assay-specific because primer modification, enzyme, template and cycle count travel together ([Hu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17631854/)).

### Recent computational-design precedent: ARMSprimer3 (2025)

ARMSprimer3 provides a directly relevant modern precedent for an automated ARMS design engine. Its workflow retrieves the target SNP and surrounding genomic sequence, identifies neighbouring common SNPs, masks those neighbouring variant positions before primer design, generates deliberate-mismatch templates at both the `−2` and `−3` positions, and asks Primer3 to search both `force-left` and `force-right` orientations so that either primer direction can carry the allele-specific 3′ terminus. PCRStudio should preserve the same *classes of design decision*—variant-mask provenance, deliberate-mismatch position/base, and allele-specific orientation—even when it uses a different data source or ranking algorithm ([Guo et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104711/)).

The ARMSprimer3 example uses `500 bp` on each side of the target (`1001 bp` total) and keeps intermediate files for the source sequence, neighbouring variants, masked sequence, deliberately mutated templates and Primer3 parameters. Those values and files are useful reproducibility precedents, not universal biological requirements: the required context window depends on candidate-search space, genome build and off-target workflow. The important canonical requirement is to retain enough sequence and variant context to reconstruct why a candidate was available or excluded ([Guo et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104711/)).

The paper also validated four SYBR-Green real-time ARMS assays whose designed primer Tm values were close to `60 °C` and products were `<150 bp`; the named laboratory protocol used `95 °C/15 s` plus `60 °C/60 s` for `50` cycles, two reactions per sample, at least `5 ng` DNA, and `30` known-genotype reference DNAs per locus, reporting `100%` accuracy in that local validation. These numbers belong only to those four diagnostic implementations and must **not** replace the current generic ARMS search envelope or become a universal performance claim ([Guo et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12104711/)).

### Primer balance and multiplex competition

In a single-tube ARMS or tetra-primer design, primer concentrations are part of allele discrimination. Waterfall and Cobb found that the two allele-specific sets could compete for priming and that the best concentrations depended on primer stability, binding efficiency, product size and other reaction components; their matrix optimization selected conditions that produced similar product yields for the two intended alleles and correctly classified a blinded `14`-sample set ([Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/)). Equal molarity is therefore a candidate condition to test, not an acceptance criterion.

For tetra-primer ARMS-PCR, Ye et al. reported that reducing outer-primer concentration to an outer:inner ratio such as `1:10` enhanced the shorter allele-specific products, while a touchdown start at `5 °C` above the average inner-primer Tm followed by `1 °C` decrements for `10` cycles reduced artificial products in some assays. They also observed an assay for which allele specificity failed despite these design principles, requiring redesign or an alternative genotyping method ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)).

The balance record must therefore retain the concentration of every primer role, the product sizes, the observed band intensities or other endpoint measurements, the titration matrix and the selected condition. A branch that is weak because its primer is limiting must not be interpreted as absence of the allele, and a strong nonspecific branch must not be repaired by changing the genotype call threshold without rerunning matched controls ([Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/)).

Rare-allele detection is a separate operating point from ordinary germline genotyping. In a JAK2 V617F ARMS study, raising the mutant-specific primer concentration, lowering the common-primer concentrations, changing annealing from `60 °C` to `58 °C`, and increasing the cycle count from `30` to `40` improved visualization of a mutant band and produced an approximately `two-order-of-magnitude` sensitivity improvement in that assay; those changes are a named low-frequency branch, not a universal genotype recipe ([JAK2 modified ARMS-PCR study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1867436/)).

### Endpoint controls and call rule

For a biallelic endpoint assay, the minimum interpretation set is a known reference-homozygote control, a known alternate-homozygote control, a known heterozygote control when available, and a no-template control; a matrix/extraction negative and an internal amplification control are added when extraction or inhibition can suppress a true product. The control plan is assay- and matrix-specific, but the purpose is fixed: distinguish allele absence from failed amplification, inhibition, contamination or nonspecific product formation ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/)).

The endpoint call rule is: reference-only or alternate-only is reportable as a homozygote only when the opposite-allele control path is demonstrably valid; both expected allele products is a heterozygote only when both products are at the declared sizes and all controls pass; any missing internal control, unexpected band, failed positive control or positive no-template control is invalid/no-call. This is a conservative operational rule derived from the published competing-product patterns and the validation requirement to define limitations and interpretation before routine use ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/)).

Band intensity is not a portable allele dosage measure. Product length, primer competition, magnesium/dNTP balance and enzyme choice can change relative yield; initial validation should compare the declared endpoint pattern with an orthogonal method such as sequencing or RFLP on known genotypes, and ongoing quality monitoring should retain the control history and invalid/no-call rate ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/)).

### In-silico specificity and sequence maintenance

Primer-BLAST should be run against the relevant RefSeq reference or a declared organism-specific database for each allele-specific and common/outer primer set. Its specificity model explicitly considers unintended paired products, 3′-end mismatch stringency, product size, low-complexity/repeat regions and primer secondary structure; a passing in-silico screen is a risk reduction step, not evidence of analytical sensitivity or specificity ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/)).

The reference sequence and variant identifier must be versioned because dbSNP and reference assemblies are updated, and newly represented variation can affect primer binding or create an unrecognized allele. The assay record should be re-screened when the reference/assembly changes or when a relevant nearby variant is found; the National Academies BioWatch PCR framework likewise treats periodic in-silico database screening and laboratory re-testing as part of maintaining PCR signatures; that source is a BioWatch/public-health framework and is used here only for the transferable maintenance principle, not as an ARMS-specific genotyping standard ([NCBI dbSNP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/), [National Academies BioWatch PCR framework (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305487/)).

## Vendor protocol overlay: Invitrogen Platinum II Taq ARMS technical note

The Thermo Fisher Platinum II Taq ARMS technical note is a named, non-universal protocol example. It uses a non-proofreading hot-start Taq, two allele-specific reaction branches, an internal control primer pair in each branch and agarose readout. Its example uses `20 ng` human genomic DNA, `0.2 µM` of each present primer, `0.2 mM` dNTPs, `0.2 U` polymerase in a `20 µL` reaction and a `94 °C/2 min`, `94 °C/15 s`, `60 °C/15 s`, `68 °C/15 s` programme for `30` cycles; a GC enhancer is listed for targets above `65%` GC ([Platinum II Taq ARMS technical note](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/Technical-Notes/snp-detection-arms-pcr-tech-note.pdf)).

The same note compares strong and weak penultimate mismatches and retains the internal control rather than interpreting an absent allele band as a negative genotype by itself. These details are useful as a protocol-bound validation template, but the note is a research-use example for one enzyme/buffer and does not replace optimization or control design for other Taq formulations, polymerases, matrices or readouts ([Platinum II Taq ARMS technical note](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/Technical-Notes/snp-detection-arms-pcr-tech-note.pdf)).

The current runtime does not silently select this overlay. If it is later implemented, it must be represented as a named `arms_protocol` object carrying enzyme, buffer, primer concentrations, internal-control roles, cycling and readout together; the shared `taq-standard` profile must not inherit the vendor's `60 °C` annealing claim ([Platinum II Taq ARMS technical note](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/Technical-Notes/snp-detection-arms-pcr-tech-note.pdf)).

## Evidence

The evidence supports allele-specific extension as a design mechanism, but not a universal mismatch table, universal primer ratio, universal chemistry choice or universal sensitivity/specificity value. The module must expose the exact mismatch design, enzyme exonuclease status, concentration matrix, endpoint call rule and orthogonal validation method, and leave performance to the declared control panel ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Huang et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1408758/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/)).

Genuine unresolved gaps remain: the literature does not provide a portable mapping from every possible terminal/penultimate mismatch and sequence context to a pass/fail threshold; no single primer-balance ratio transfers across untagged ARMS, tetra-primer ARMS, fluorescent competitive ASA and multiplex matrices; and the cited vendor protocols do not establish diagnostic performance for this profile. The runtime therefore records these as validation obligations rather than filling them with universal defaults ([Kwok et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2179874/), [Ayyadevara et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10933850/), [Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [National Academies BioWatch PCR framework (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305487/)).

## Verification record

**Implementation:** validate both allele orientations, reference accession/assembly, terminal placement, deliberate-mismatch metadata, per-role primer concentrations, polymerase exonuclease status, competing-allele products and Primer-BLAST specificity results ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/), [NCBI dbSNP](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/snp/)).

**Test:** cover reference-homozygote, alternate-homozygote, heterozygous, no-template, matrix/extraction-negative where applicable, internal-control failure, unexpected-band and incompatible-enzyme cases; titrate primer balance and chemistry branch; require a declared call rule and orthogonal method before a genotype is displayed ([Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/), [National Academies BioWatch PCR framework (NCBI Bookshelf)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305487/)).

**Limitation:** this profile is `limited`; an in-silico match, band intensity, primer score or vendor starting recipe is not a genotype call or a validated discrimination ratio, and an unmodified proofreading enzyme must not be treated as equivalent to the default exo− Taq branch ([Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/), [Waterfall and Cobb](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11726702/), [Platinum II Taq User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/manuals/MAN0017534_Platinum_II_Taq_HS_DNA_Pol_UG.pdf), [HotStar HiFidelity PCR Handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/us/resources/kithandbook/en-hotstar-hifidelity-pcr-handbook)).

## R16 implementation contract

R16 normalizes the variant before ARMS design and preserves `REF`, `ALT`, coordinate system, strand, accession/build and optional nearby-variant panel provenance. ARMS remains an SNV/single-anchor discrimination topology: an indel or complex event is not coerced into a fake SNV. MNV reduction, when used, records the selected discriminatory anchor and retains the other differences as context/masking evidence ([PCRStudio discriminating protocol authority](https://github.com/pcrstudio/pcrstudio/blob/main/contracts/chemistry/discriminating-protocols.json)).

Terminal and deliberate near-terminal mismatch effects are stored as **evidence-scoped, polymerase-aware heuristics**, not a universal mismatch truth table. Caller-supplied VCF masking can exclude candidate-binding sites containing declared nearby variation. Endpoint/real-time bench evidence is recorded separately from primer ranking and cannot by itself create a validated genotype claim ([ARMS mismatch evidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40421167/)).
