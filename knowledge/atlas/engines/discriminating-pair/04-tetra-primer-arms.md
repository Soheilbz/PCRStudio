## Scope

Tetra-primer ARMS combines two outer primers with two allele-specific inner primers so one reaction can produce allele-informative products and a control product. The four-primer geometry must be represented as one coupled scheme; independently valid pairs can still interact or produce indistinguishable bands in the mixture ([Ye et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/), [Medrano & de Oliveira](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519268/)).

The module owns product-size ordering, inner/outer placement, deliberate mismatch policy, no-proofreading chemistry and gel interpretation. It must not claim a genotype from predicted bands without a control band and representative sample validation ([Medrano & de Oliveira](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519268/)).

For a single biallelic target, the orientation is fixed by the two inner primers pointing inward from opposite sides of the variant and the two outer primers flanking it. The control amplicon is the outer-forward/outer-reverse pair; each allele-specific amplicon is one inner primer paired with the opposite outer primer. The outer primers are intentionally placed at unequal distances from the variant so the two inner products have different lengths, while the exact band sizes must be calculated from the reference sequence and primer 5′ coordinates rather than inferred from a generic range ([Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [caprine Fec-gene tetra-primer study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4287864/)).

The implementation should therefore retain primer orientation, strand/allele assignment, genomic coordinates, expected control size, both expected allele-product sizes, and the pairwise size differences. A candidate is not gel-resolvable merely because every individual primer pair is valid: the selected gel system must be shown to separate the actual products, especially the two shorter products ([Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Thermo Scientific DNA electrophoresis recommendations](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012614_Gen_Recommend_DNA_Electrophoresis_UG.pdf)).

Reference provenance is also part of the geometry: store the reference accession/version or assembly, the variant identifier and REF/ALT alleles, and the strand used for primer notation. NCBI recommends a RefSeq accession when possible for Primer-BLAST specificity checking, and its Variation Viewer/dbSNP documentation distinguishes reference-SNP identifiers from assembly-specific placements; this implies that a strand or assembly mismatch could silently reverse the allele assignment even when the four oligos are syntactically valid ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/), [NCBI Variation Viewer help](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/variation/view/help/), [NCBI dbSNP orientation guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/core/assets/snp/docs/RefSNP_orientation_updates.pdf)).

## Extended tetra-primer mismatch and band rules

The original tetra-primer method places a deliberate mismatch at position `−2` from the 3-prime terminus of each inner primer to strengthen allele discrimination, but later assay designs also use other near-terminal positions and must optimize them against the particular SNP and primer sequence. The `−2` value is therefore a documented method example, not a universal runtime default ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Melt-MAMA functional study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3306377/)).

Ye et al. also record a mismatch-strength pairing heuristic: a strong terminal mismatch (`G/A` or `C/T`) is likely to need a weak second mismatch (`C/A` or `G/T`), a weak terminal mismatch is likely to need a strong second mismatch, and a medium terminal mismatch (`A/A`, `C/C`, `G/G` or `T/T`) is likely to need a medium second mismatch. This is a design starting point only; terminal mismatch extension is sequence- and polymerase-dependent, so the selected mismatch position/base must be tested on both matched and non-matched allele templates ([Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Newton et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2785681/)).

The mismatch type itself can dominate the outcome: in a primer-template model study, 3′-terminal `A:G`, `G:A` and `C:C` mismatches reduced product yield by about `100-fold`, `A:A` by about `20-fold`, while several other terminal mismatches amplified efficiently; `G:G` was more sensitive to sequence context and dNTP concentration, and terminal `T` mismatches were comparatively permissive. These measurements are from an HIV-1 model and must be treated as comparative chemistry evidence, not a transferable ARMS error rate ([Kwok et al., mismatch study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2179874/)).

Related competitive MAMA experiments found no functional advantage of choosing the `−2` rather than `−3` position in their assay set, and rescued poorly balanced assays by making the problematic allele-specific primer `2×`, `3×` or `4×` less concentrated than its competitor. This supports exposing position and per-inner-primer concentration as independent optimization fields, while keeping the result scoped to the related MAMA chemistry rather than promoting it to a tetra-primer default ([Melt-MAMA functional study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3306377/)).

The single-locus assay has three possible products, not four: a homozygous sample is expected to show the control product plus the matching allele product, while a heterozygote should show both allele products and the control. Missing control, weak allele band or co-migrating products must remain an unresolved state rather than being converted into a genotype by pattern matching alone ([Ye et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/), [Medrano & de Oliveira](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519268/)). The module consequently records expected sizes, measured gel observations, ladder/system and call rule separately.

### Primary-method parameter disposition

The original tetra-primer ARMS method gives a concrete starting architecture: inner primers were approximately `28 bases`, the inner:outer final-concentration ratio was `10:1`, and either constant or touchdown annealing was used. Its touchdown example began at `5 °C` above the mean inner-primer Tm, decreased by `1 °C` per cycle for the first `10` cycles, and then held the final annealing temperature; the authors also demonstrated that lowering outer-primer concentration improved the shorter allele-specific products and that the assay could fail for a particular SNP despite the same general method ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)).

The primary paper also reports a complete `10 µL` reaction example: `30 ng` template DNA, `10 pmol` of each inner primer, `1 pmol` of each outer primer, `200 µM` of each dNTP, `20 mM` Tris-HCl at pH `8.4`, `50 mM` KCl, `0.05% (v/v)` W1, `0.5 U` Taq polymerase and an MgCl₂ concentration selected per assay table ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)). In the TNF −308G→A example, the reported Mg²⁺ condition was `2.5 mM` and the allele-specific products were `154 bp` and `224 bp`, with a `323 bp` outer control product ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)). For constant cycling, Ye et al. used `2 min` at `95 °C`, `35` cycles of `95 °C/1 min`, assay-specific annealing for `1 min` and `72 °C/1 min`, followed by `72 °C/2 min`; their touchdown description instead gives `72 °C` for the first cycle, followed by a `1 °C` decrease per cycle until the assay's listed annealing temperature, then continuation at that temperature. The generic text example of starting `5 °C` above the mean inner-primer Tm and the table’s actual `72 °C` start must remain separate fields rather than being reconciled into one invented value ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)). These values are retained as a named primary-method record and are not promoted to the current generic profile.

The same primary study validated calls against an independent restriction-digest method and documented failed PCR controls, which supports keeping an independent confirmation route and missing-control state in the result rather than treating an expected band pattern as proof of genotype ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)).

## Computational-design precedents

The original Ye implementation already treated tetra-primer design as an algorithmic search problem: users specify the target sequence/polymorphic site plus primer Tm, GC, length, complementarity and product-size criteria, and the program evaluates candidate sets rather than relying on a hand-written four-primer recipe ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)). This supports keeping the four coupled primer roles, deliberate mismatches, product geometry and interaction constraints as one set-level optimization problem.

BatchPrimer3 later implemented high-throughput SNP genotyping design on top of Primer3, including allele-specific and tetra-primer ARMS modes. For tetra-primer ARMS it can construct the **two theoretical orientation sets** for a SNP and explicitly implements an additional mismatch at the third position from the 3′ end as a published design option. PCRStudio should therefore enumerate both feasible orientation families rather than treating the first viable orientation as canonical, and should retain which mismatch rule generated each candidate. BatchPrimer3 is an algorithmic precedent, not evidence that the third-position mismatch or any Primer3 default is universally optimal ([You et al., BatchPrimer3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2438325/)).

These precedents also strengthen a reproducibility requirement: input target/assembly, polymorphism orientation, excluded or masked regions, candidate orientation, primer-design parameter set and rejection reason should be retained with the final four-primer set. A software-generated set remains a candidate until the coupled products, interaction risk, gel-resolvability and genotype controls pass the module's validation rules ([Ye et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [You et al., BatchPrimer3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2438325/)).

## Optimization and enzyme overlays

The ten-SNP evaluation by Okayama et al. identifies two opposing failure modes that the runtime must keep separate: the shorter allele-specific products can be weaker than the outer control, in which case increasing the relative inner-primer concentration can help; non-specific short products can instead be reduced by lowering the inner:outer ratio, raising the annealing temperature for the first `5–10` cycles, or using hot-start PCR. These are diagnostic branches, not a single universal primer ratio ([Okayama et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15061374/)).

A published mouse assay provides a second, fully specified gel-based overlay: `12.5 µL` total reaction with `2 µL` crude extract containing approximately `28–36 ng` DNA, `6.25 µL` DreamTaq `2X` master mix and `0.25 µL` of each `10 µM` primer (reported final concentration `0.2 µM`); cycling was `95 °C/3 min`, then `33` cycles of `95 °C/30 s`, `58 °C/30 s` and `72 °C/1 min`, followed by `72 °C/7 min`. The products were resolved on `1.5%` agarose with a `100 bp` ladder. In that assay, a `56–72 °C` gradient was explored, clear allelic discrimination was observed from `56` to `66.1 °C`, and the `369 bp` outer control disappeared above `66.1 °C`; `58 °C` was selected for subsequent runs. The reported products were `253 bp` wild-type, `171 bp` mutant and `369 bp` control, and the genotype panel contained `24` mice. These values are an assay-specific validation record, not a generic tetra-primer programme ([mouse leptin tetra-primer ARMS-PCR study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9558987/)).

The caprine Fec-gene study demonstrates why the module must expose per-assay optimization rather than hard-code one ratio: all tested primer sets used `2 mM` MgCl₂, but the outer:inner ratio was `1:1` for BMPR1B and `1:10` for GDF9 and BMP15. Its optimized overlay used `25 µL` reactions, `50–100 ng` genomic DNA, `32` cycles after `95 °C/4 min`, assay-specific annealing for `45 s`, `72 °C/45 s` extension and `72 °C/8 min` final extension, followed by `3%` agarose and a `50–1000 bp` ladder. The reported product sets were `239/168/125 bp`, `323/207/170 bp` and `425/222/257 bp` (control/allele 1/allele 2), and `3` samples per genotype were checked by sequencing. The differing annealing temperatures (`54`, `68` and `67 °C`) further show that Tm and locus context dominate the protocol choice ([caprine Fec-gene tetra-primer study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4287864/)).

Mg²⁺ must be optimized as free cofactor under the selected buffer and dNTP conditions, not copied from another assay: Thermo’s standard Taq manual gives a `1–4 mM` recommended MgCl₂ range, warns that low Mg²⁺ reduces yield and excess Mg²⁺ can increase nonspecific products and reduce fidelity, and notes that EDTA/chelators consume Mg²⁺. The same manual recommends `0.2 mM` of each dNTP and stepwise `1–2 °C` annealing optimization when nonspecific products appear; these are generic Taq branches and do not override the tetra-primer-specific inner/outer balance ([Thermo Scientific Taq DNA Polymerase manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012759_Taq_DNA_Polymerase_AB0192B_UG.pdf)).

An independent NEB Taq guidance branch gives `1.5–2.0 mM` Mg²⁺ as optimal for most Taq products and a typical annealing range of `45–68 °C`, while noting that absent product may require a lower annealing temperature. Because these values are manufacturer-general rather than tetra-primer-specific, the module should record the enzyme/buffer identity, Mg²⁺ titration, annealing gradient and the observed control/allele-band pattern together ([NEB Taq DNA Polymerase guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/taq-dna-polymerase-guidelines-for-pcr-optimization)).

For gel selection, Thermo’s DNA electrophoresis recommendations list effective separation ranges of `200–3000 bp` for `1.5%` agarose, `100–2000 bp` for `2.0%`, `25–1000 bp` for `3.0%`, and `60–400 bp` for `8%` non-denaturing polyacrylamide. These are broad operating ranges, not a claim that any two bands within a range will resolve; the assay record must retain agarose/PAGE percentage, TAE/TBE or other buffer, ladder, run conditions, image and an acceptance decision for each adjacent product pair ([Thermo Scientific DNA electrophoresis recommendations](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012614_Gen_Recommend_DNA_Electrophoresis_UG.pdf)).

For the named `taq-standard` capability, the official Thermo Scientific DreamTaq protocol is an enzyme baseline only: `50 µL` reactions, `0.1–1.0 µM` of each primer, `10 pg–1 µg` template, `1.25 U` polymerase and approximately `2.0 mM` MgCl₂ from the supplied buffer; its generic cycling guidance is `95 °C/1–3 min`, `25–40` cycles with `95 °C/30 s`, annealing at `Tm−5 °C` for `30 s`, `72 °C/1 min`, and `72 °C/5–15 min` final extension. The manufacturer also permits MgCl₂ optimization up to `4.0 mM` and identifies the product as research-use-only. These settings must remain an explicit enzyme overlay and must not replace the tetra-primer-specific ratio, Tm and band-resolution validation ([Thermo Scientific DreamTaq protocol](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/Reference-Materials/dreamtaq-dna-polymerases-labaid.pdf)).

The module should offer an independent confirmation field. A beta-thalassemia comparison reported `100%` sensitivity, specificity and accuracy for its tetra-primer assay against sequencing, but that result belongs to the named loci, samples and protocol and cannot be generalized to all targets. For a release-quality call, the record therefore needs the confirmation method, sample set, genotype concordance and unresolved-band count ([beta-thalassemia comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24946023/)).

For a validation-quality record, CDC guidance calls for samples representing every reportable genotype/result, suitable control or reference materials, and established accuracy, precision, analytic sensitivity and analytic specificity before reporting results; it also requires the assay limitations and whether published performance claims reproduce locally to be documented. FDA sequencing guidance adds that a PCR product used for Sanger confirmation should be a single distinct band of expected size, that bidirectional sequencing improves confidence, and that chromatograms should be inspected with objective quality criteria. These are validation requirements for the chosen laboratory method, not universal tetra-primer performance numbers ([CDC good laboratory practices for molecular genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [FDA guidelines for validation of nucleic-acid sequencing methods](https://www.fda.gov/media/121751/download)).

## Evidence

The method papers support the four-primer geometry and gel-based interpretation, but they do not justify one universal product-size separation, gel protocol or genotype error rate. These remain explicit module and validation inputs ([Ye et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/), [Medrano & de Oliveira](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519268/)).

Unresolved evidence gaps are the minimum band-size delta that guarantees separation on a named agarose/PAGE system, an assay-independent inner:outer concentration ratio, a universal Mg²⁺ optimum, a universal annealing temperature/touchdown schedule, and an objective intensity threshold for calling a weak allele band. The primary studies instead show locus- and chemistry-specific optimization, while CDC/FDA guidance requires local performance establishment and confirmation rather than supplying tetra-primer-specific cutoffs ([Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Okayama et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15061374/), [CDC good laboratory practices for molecular genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [FDA guidelines for validation of nucleic-acid sequencing methods](https://www.fda.gov/media/121751/download)).

## Verification record

**Implementation:** validate all four roles, allele orientation, outer-control containment, product ordering, interaction checks and the polymerase gate ([Ye et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/), [Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/)).

**Test:** cover homozygous reference, homozygous alternate, heterozygous, missing-control and unresolved-band states; include a no-template control and characterized material for every reportable genotype/result where available; do not convert a predicted pattern into a call automatically ([Medrano & de Oliveira](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519268/), [CDC good laboratory practices for molecular genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm)).

**Confirmation:** for Sanger confirmation, require a single distinct expected-size amplicon, objective chromatogram review and bidirectional reads where practicable; record the reference sequence, read quality, concordance and any ambiguous bases ([FDA guidelines for validation of nucleic-acid sequencing methods](https://www.fda.gov/media/121751/download)).

**Limitation:** this profile is `limited`; gel visibility, allele dropout, terminal-mismatch failure, matrix effects and call accuracy require bench validation. The literature does not establish a universal inner:outer ratio, Mg²⁺ concentration, annealing temperature, gel percentage, minimum band-size difference or genotype error rate for all loci and chemistries ([Ye et al., original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC55900/), [Okayama et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15061374/), [CDC good laboratory practices for molecular genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm)).

## R16 implementation contract

R16 retains Tetra-ARMS as an SNV/single-anchor four-primer topology with three expected products: two allele-specific products and an outer control product. Readout context is first-class: gel/PAGE mode, declared gel percentage or run context, ladder and minimum band-separation requirement are preserved with the candidate set ([Ye et al., Tetra-primer ARMS-PCR](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/)).

PCRStudio does not promote a universal inner:outer primer ratio or universal base-pair separation into a biological rule. Band resolvability is reported against the declared readout context, while actual gel bands and genotype calls remain measured evidence with `decision_impact=none` relative to sequence ranking ([Tetra-primer ARMS-PCR method](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11522844/)).
