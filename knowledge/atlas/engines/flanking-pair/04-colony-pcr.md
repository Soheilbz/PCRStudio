## Evidence collected

Colony PCR is a screening workflow on crude colony material, not ordinary PCR with a different title. Common designs use two vector-flanking primers to test insert size, or one vector primer plus one insert primer to test presence and orientation. The result is normally interpreted by gel size and needs appropriate positive, empty-vector and negative controls ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr), [Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/6/pdb.prot095224), [Bergkessel & Guthrie](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24011056/)). Direct colony transfer also has a pre-analytical constraint: bacterial cell contents and carryover medium can inhibit polymerase, so colony amount and transfer procedure must remain visible assay inputs ([NEB OneTaq colony-PCR application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr)).

Host and lysis method are not interchangeable implementation details. Comparative and protocol studies describe different workflows for bacterial colonies and yeasts, and direct colony PCR is specifically limited by DNA release and inhibitors; therefore the module must ask for host class and lysis branch before treating a crude-template screen as operationally supported ([Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/), [Azhahianambi et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19024172/), [Lin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23065821/)). *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, filamentous fungi, and microalgae are not safe implicit fallbacks for the bacterial direct-transfer branch because published protocols use additional enzymatic, mechanical, chemical, or dilution steps and report organism-dependent success ([Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/), [Packeiser et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12010-012-0043-8), [Colony-PCR of hyphomycetes](https://www.mdpi.com/2309-608X/2/2/12)).

The colony is also a sampled biological population, not a guaranteed single plasmid molecule. A mixed or co-transformed colony can yield a composite or misleading screen, so the result must retain the colony identifier and replica/stock and should be re-isolated before treating a positive as a clonal construct ([High rates of plasmid cotransformation in *E. coli*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9262894/)).

## Audited pre-analytical contract

The assigned assay is a qualitative transformant screen. The following inputs must be captured as assay metadata because they change the meaning of a negative or positive band ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/standard-protocols/one-pcr-protocol-m0489)):

| Input or decision | Defensible rule | Failure if omitted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colony selection | Pick a single, well-isolated colony from the stated selective plate and record host, plate medium/selection, colony age and colony identifier. NEB specifies well-isolated colonies and Thermo’s BAC/PAC procedure starts from a freshly streaked plate; neither source supports silently treating a mixed or poorly isolated growth as clonal ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Thermo BAC/PAC screening manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/custom_screening_service_man.pdf)). | Mixed, satellite or cross-contaminated growth can make the PCR tube and the retained stock represent different templates; the result is screen-inconclusive until re-isolation ([High rates of plasmid cotransformation in *E. coli*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9262894/)). |
| Replica/stock before sampling | Touch the picked colony to a fresh selective plate or growth medium before/after transfer according to the named protocol, and retain the replica identifier. NEB documents both 3 ml growth medium and a fresh selective-agar restreak as retention branches; Thermo’s BAC/PAC manual recommends master stocks before use ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Thermo BAC/PAC screening manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/custom_screening_service_man.pdf)). | A positive band cannot be recovered for confirmation if the physical clone was consumed or misidentified. |
| Sampling tool | Record plastic pipette tip, sterile loop, or toothpick. A peer-reviewed study specifically reports wooden-toothpick inhibition, while NEB protocols permit a sterile toothpick; this is a genuine tool-dependent conflict, so a wooden toothpick must not be assumed equivalent to plastic ([Lee & Cooper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7537048/), [NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf)). | Tool-derived inhibition can appear as a false negative or weak/irregular amplification and is not distinguishable from poor lysis without a lysis/process control. |
| Inoculum amount | Use a small amount and preserve the method’s amount descriptor. Named examples are 1–2 mm well-isolated colony (NEB), approximately 1 mm whole colony into 100 µl water with 5 µl lysate in 50 µl PCR (Promega), or one colony directly into a reaction (NEB M0689); Sigma explicitly warns that overloading cells interferes with PCR ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf), [NEB M0689 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/supplemental-protocol-2-using-colony-pcr-to-identify-positive-clones-neb-m0689), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr)). | Too much biomass and agar/medium carryover can inhibit polymerase or increase nonspecific products; too little biomass can give a lysis/template-limited false negative ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf)). |
| Direct-transfer branch | Directly dip/stir the colony into a named direct-PCR master mix only when that mix documents crude-colony use. NEB and Sigma document this branch for named mixes; it must not be generalized to an arbitrary polymerase/buffer ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr)). | A standard purified-DNA PCR recipe may fail because intact cells, medium and cell debris remain in the reaction. |
| Water- or buffer-lysis branch | A separate crude lysate is permitted only with its buffer, heating, cooling, centrifugation and template-volume fields recorded. Examples include 100 µl water/95 °C/5 min/5 µl into a 50 µl Promega reaction and 10 µl TE/99 °C/5 min/ice 2 min/brief spin for Thermo BAC/PAC screening ([Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf), [Thermo BAC/PAC screening manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/custom_screening_service_man.pdf)). | Omitting the lysate branch makes a negative result uninterpretable as lysis failure versus primer/chemistry failure. |
| Host branch | Bacterial, yeast, fungal and algal templates require separate support declarations. *S. cerevisiae* methods use additional lysis steps; red yeast, fungi and microalgae studies report organism-specific methods and success boundaries ([Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/), [Lin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23065821/), [Colony-PCR of hyphomycetes](https://www.mdpi.com/2309-608X/2/2/12), [Chlorella colony PCR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3835426/)). | Applying the *E. coli* direct-transfer branch to a yeast, fungus or microalga risks failed DNA release, inhibitor carryover and false-negative screening. |
| Medium/agar carryover | Record visible medium carryover and whether the selected mix has an empirical tolerance claim. Sigma reports, for its JumpStart REDTaq system, robust amplification with up to 20% LB and up to 10% LB agar or Terrific Broth in a spiked-template experiment; those are product-specific observations, not a general acceptance limit ([Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr)). | A clean NTC does not rule out sample-specific inhibition; a target-negative colony with no process control cannot be called empty vector. |

Addgene's current colony-PCR guidance is useful as a practical topology cross-check: insert-specific primer pairs answer primarily whether the insert is present; vector/backbone-flanking primers can reveal presence and approximate insert size; an orientation-specific screen combines an insert primer with a vector primer. Addgene also warns that picking too much bacterial material can inhibit PCR or increase nonspecific products and that an expected-size colony-PCR band does not rule out mutations, so final positive constructs should be sequence-verified ([Addgene colony PCR](https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-colony-pcr)). These are screening/verification rules, not a substitute for the selected polymerase protocol.

## Current implementation assessment

- **Current software contract:** `screen` remains the assay purpose, but PCRStudio no longer carries a universal colony-lysis hold or cycle count. The request must declare `colony_host_class`, `colony_preparation`, `colony_protocol_name` and the separate `colony_protocol_provenance`; the result preserves the declared SOP identity and source/revision authority as pre-analytical provenance and stays `in-silico-screen-design` until bench observations exist. This matches the documented variation between direct-transfer, short-heat and host-specific protocols.
- A true orientation screen needs vector/insert role information because a generic target sequence alone cannot identify which primer is in the backbone or prove orientation ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)).
- An `in-silico-only` validation plan for empty-vector, positive and negative controls plus clone sequencing is required because a predicted band is not proof of a correct transformant ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/6/pdb.prot095224)).
- A positive band is not equivalent to a correct clone; expected-size logic must be separated from sequence confirmation or a second independent primer geometry ([Expresso cloning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791521/)).

Positive ΔG means no favorable duplex was found and is valid, not an error ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)). | `backbone.design`, `cross_dimer_temperature_c`; backbone regressions. | This is a nearest-neighbour structure proxy, not a kinetic or wet-lab interaction guarantee. |
| Product size and cycles | soft starting points | **Current software contract:** the screen product envelope remains assay/profile-scoped, but no universal colony-PCR cycle count is encoded; cycling comes from the selected laboratory/vendor SOP. Named protocols must override these values when selected: the NEB example uses `30` cycles and named crude-template products extend beyond this envelope ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [NEB insert-screening protocol E1202](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/insert-screening-protocols-e1202), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf)). | `screen` purpose and profile cycling. | Gel resolution depends on instrument, stain and sample; no universal colony-PCR product envelope is established. |

## Extended colony-screening rules

Colony PCR is a screening decision, not a direct clone-identity assay. The interpretation must retain the primer geometry, expected empty-vector/product sizes, electrophoresis result and control outcomes as separate fields; NEB explicitly distinguishes insert presence, size and orientation use cases and interprets the product by gel alongside a size marker ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)).

| Additional rule | Resolution for Colony PCR | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive/negative interpretation | “No band” is not automatically an empty clone: lysis, template amount, inhibitors and primer geometry can all affect a crude-colony result. Require a positive amplification control and a negative/host or NTC control before interpreting a screen ([Green & Sambrook](https://cshprotocols.cshlp.org/content/2019/6/pdb.prot095224), [NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf)). | The engine can define the control matrix but cannot observe lysis or gel quality. |
| Orientation | A vector primer paired with an insert primer can create a product only for the expected orientation; two insert-only primers do not prove orientation ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)). | A vector/junction map is required for a complete orientation claim. |
| Empty-vector discrimination | Report the expected empty-vector and insert-containing product sizes before the run and require a gel resolution check; a single size window without vector context is insufficient ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)). | Band size does not prove sequence identity or absence of rearrangement. |
| Colony input | Preserve the selected lysis/initial-denaturation protocol as a kit- and host-dependent starting condition; NEB documents both direct-to-reaction and short-heating approaches ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)). | Host, colony size, plasmid copy number and lysis efficiency remain experimental variables. |
| Inhibition traceability | Record colony size estimate, direct-transfer versus pre-lysis, medium carryover and whether the positive control amplified; cell contents and medium can inhibit polymerase in direct colony PCR ([NEB OneTaq colony-PCR application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf)). | A computational result cannot distinguish failed lysis from polymerase inhibition or a primer/product problem. |
| Clone traceability | Link each reaction to a retained replica or stock before interpreting a positive band; NEB's protocol explicitly keeps the picked colony available for later growth or restreaking ([NEB OneTaq colony-PCR application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf)). | The engine can require a traceability field but cannot verify physical sample identity. |
| Final confirmation | Sequence representative positives or use an independent primer geometry before treating a colony as a confirmed construct ([Expresso cloning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791521/)). | A PCR band alone cannot establish full insert sequence, junction correctness or orientation. |

The profile should consequently use the status vocabulary `screen-compatible`, `screen-inconclusive` and `identity-unconfirmed` rather than collapsing a predicted product into “correct clone” ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr), [Expresso cloning protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5791521/)).

## Control matrix and run acceptance

The controls below are deliberately separated because a purified-template positive control does not test colony lysis, and an NTC does not test sample-specific inhibition. ISO 22174:2024 distinguishes positive PCR, positive process, negative PCR, negative process and internal amplification controls; its application here is a case-by-case research adaptation, not a claim that colony PCR is a food-chain diagnostic method ([ISO 22174:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/81777.html), [ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)).

| Control | Material and path | Required interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive PCR control | Known target DNA or a previously confirmed construct in the same primer/chemistry/cycling/detection system, without colony matrix. ISO defines this as a reaction containing target nucleic acid in a defined amount/copy number ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)). | Must produce the expected assay product or the run is invalid for calling colony negatives; it does not prove that a colony lysed. |
| Positive process/lysis control | A known positive colony or cell suspension carried through the same sampling, lysis, PCR and gel path. ISO defines a positive process control as a spiked sample treated through the same PCR-based method ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)). | Distinguishes reagent/cycler failure from crude-template release or inhibitor failure; keep it separate from the purified-template positive control. |
| Negative PCR control / NTC | All PCR components except target template, using PCR-grade water or another target-free PCR-inert substrate ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [WHO PCR laboratory guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/70765/WHO_IVB_11.09_eng.pdf)). | Any target-sized band is contamination or an unresolved nonspecific reaction; do not accept sample positives until the contamination event is investigated and the run is repeated. |
| Negative host/process control | Untransformed host, or target-free host material, carried through the colony sampling and lysis path. | A band here indicates host-background amplification, carryover or nonspecific priming; it is not interchangeable with an NTC because it contains the biological matrix ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [WHO good laboratory practice](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/68762/WHO_IVB_04.10.pdf)). |
| Empty-vector control | A confirmed empty backbone processed with the same primer geometry. | Establishes the empty-vector band, if any, and prevents a generic band/no-band rule from being mistaken for insert-size discrimination ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)). |
| Optional internal amplification/lysis control | A distinguishable host or exogenous target in the same reaction, only if the multiplex chemistry, product sizes and primer interactions have been validated. ISO requires a homologous internal-control product to be distinguishable from the target; NCBI notes that multiplex primer interactions can reduce sensitivity or create nonspecific products ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [NCBI multiplex-PCR guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305477/)). | Validation-only for this module. A failed internal-control band makes a target-negative colony inconclusive, but a present internal control does not prove plasmid insert identity. |

The run-level decision is therefore: (1) reject or mark invalid if the positive PCR/process control fails or the NTC/negative-process control is positive; (2) call a colony screen-positive only when the expected geometry and size are observed in a control-valid gel; (3) call no-band results screen-negative only when the lysis/process evidence is valid; and (4) retain all other results as screen-inconclusive. This conservative separation follows the control logic in ISO/WHO guidance and the manufacturer protocols, while the exact acceptance threshold remains a laboratory validation decision ([ISO 22174:2024](https://www.iso.org/standard/81777.html), [WHO PCR laboratory guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/70765/WHO_IVB_11.09_eng.pdf), [NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr)).

## Gel readout and failure modes

Gel readout is an observed analytical result, not a property inferred from the primer design. Run a DNA ladder adjacent to the samples and select the gel percentage for the expected size range; Thermo’s manufacturer table gives approximate efficient ranges of 1.0% agarose for 400–8,000 bp, 1.5% for 200–3,000 bp, 2.0% for 100–2,000 bp, 3.0% for 25–1,000 bp, and 4.0% for 10–500 bp ([Thermo nucleic-acid electrophoresis guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/in/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/na-electrophoresis-education/na-electrophoresis-workflow.html)). These are separation ranges for the named agarose workflow, not universal acceptance limits or a guarantee that two close products are resolved.

Named loading branches must also remain visible: NEB OneTaq Quick-Load loads 4–6 µl directly, NEB M0689 examines 5–10 µl, Thermo BAC/PAC transfers 10 µl reaction plus 2 µl 10X loading buffer, and a WHO agarose example loads 5 µl sample with 2 µl 5–6X dye and 4 µl ladder ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [NEB M0689 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/supplemental-protocol-2-using-colony-pcr-to-identify-positive-clones-neb-m0689), [Thermo BAC/PAC screening manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/custom_screening_service_man.pdf), [WHO agarose gel guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/336499/9789240009424-eng.pdf)). Do not convert one loading volume into a platform-independent rule.

| Observed gel pattern | Disposition | Main failure modes to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single band at the predicted insert/vector size, controls valid | screen-compatible, identity-unconfirmed | Correct size does not exclude point mutations, rearrangement, wrong junction, mixed colony or carryover insert; sequence confirmation remains required ([NEB Colony PCR guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-analysis/colony-pcr), [Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833)). |
| No colony band, positive PCR/process control valid | screen-negative only for the tested geometry | Poor lysis, too little/too much biomass, inhibitor carryover, lost plasmid, primer-site mutation, wrong orientation or an amplicon too large for the selected chemistry; do not label it empty vector without the empty-vector control ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf)). |
| No band in colony and positive control/process control fails | run-invalid / repeat | Reagent, thermocycler, primer, lysis or detection failure; colony results cannot be interpreted ([WHO PCR laboratory guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/70765/WHO_IVB_11.09_eng.pdf)). |
| Multiple bands, smear, strong low-molecular-weight product or unresolved close bands | screen-inconclusive | Nonspecific amplification, primer-dimer, overloaded cells, crude-template inhibition, mixed colony, unsuitable annealing/extension, or gel resolution failure; do not select a band for sequencing unless a unique band is isolated and purified ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833), [Thermo multiplex troubleshooting](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/BID/Product-Bulletins/D11085~.pdf)). |
| Target-sized band in NTC, empty-vector or negative-host control | run-invalid / contamination investigation | Amplicon carryover, ligation-insert carryover, agar/plate contamination, reagent contamination or nonspecific host amplification; use unidirectional workflow and repeat with fresh/decontaminated reagents as appropriate ([Dallas-Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.2144/98244bm14), [Agrawal & Roy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ab.2007.11.029), [WHO good laboratory practice](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/68762/WHO_IVB_04.10.pdf)). |

## False-positive and false-negative audit

The following mechanisms must remain visible in the result record; a generic positive or negative flag is insufficient for crude-colony material ([WHO good laboratory practice](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/68762/WHO_IVB_04.10.pdf)).

| Mechanism | Evidence and mitigation | Result disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unligated insert or plate DNA | Dallas-Yang et al. showed that colony PCR can amplify DNA from the agar plate and reported a high false-positive frequency when both primers came from the insert; they recommend including a vector-derived primer. Agrawal and Roy describe insert carryover from the ligation mix adhered to colonies or agar and a nuclease-preincubation approach ([Dallas-Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.2144/98244bm14), [Agrawal & Roy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ab.2007.11.029)). | An insert-only positive is `identity-unconfirmed`; prefer at least one backbone/junction primer or a separately validated carryover-control strategy. Nuclease pretreatment is a named experimental branch, not a universal default. |
| Amplicon/reagent/workspace carryover | WHO guidance identifies PCR-product carryover and aerosols as contamination hazards and recommends separated pre-/post-PCR areas, barrier tips, aliquoted reagents, careful uncapping, positive controls and well-characterized negatives ([WHO good laboratory practice](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/68762/WHO_IVB_04.10.pdf)). | A positive NTC or negative-process control invalidates the run for positive calls until the event is investigated and the assay is repeated. |
| Host-background or nonspecific product | A target-free host control can reveal background priming that an NTC cannot. ISO defines the negative process control as a target-free sample run through the analytical process; its primer-dimer definition also notes that primer interactions can consume primers and create false-positive signals ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)). | A band in the host control, a low-molecular-weight primer-dimer or an unresolved extra band is not an insert call; mark `screen-inconclusive` and redesign/optimize or re-test with purified template. |
| Mixed or co-transformed colony | A colony is sampled, not exhaustively genotyped. Published *E. coli* work documents co-transformed and mosaic colonies, so a PCR signal from a sampled portion cannot prove colony homogeneity ([High rates of plasmid cotransformation in *E. coli*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9262894/)). | Re-isolate from the retained replica/stock and confirm the recovered clone independently; do not treat a single band as proof of monoclonality. |
| Lysis/template inhibition | Bacterial cell contents, medium and colony amount can inhibit amplification; yeast, fungi and algae require distinct lysis conditions. Too much biomass can inhibit, while too little or inefficient lysis can cause a no-band result ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr), [Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/)). | A no-band colony is only a screen-negative when positive process/lysis evidence is valid; otherwise it is `screen-inconclusive`. |
| Tool/medium/gel artifact | Wooden toothpicks can inhibit bacterial colony PCR; medium carryover has product-dependent tolerance; band sizing depends on ladder, agarose percentage, loading and imaging ([Lee & Cooper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7537048/), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr), [Thermo nucleic-acid electrophoresis guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/in/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/na-electrophoresis-education/na-electrophoresis-workflow.html)). | Record the tool, carryover, ladder and gel conditions; repeat before attributing a failure to primer design. |

## Sequencing confirmation contract

A colony-PCR band is evidence for the tested primer geometry and approximate size only. NEB’s screening protocols permit sequencing either the PCR product or a plasmid prepared from the retained colony; the latter can better preserve clone context, while a PCR product must first be shown to be a unique band and purified ([NEB M0689 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/supplemental-protocol-2-using-colony-pcr-to-identify-positive-clones-neb-m0689), [NEB insert-screening protocol E1202](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/insert-screening-protocols-e1202), [Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833)).

The confirmation handoff must retain the colony/replica identifier, source material (purified plasmid versus gel-purified amplicon), PCR primer pair, sequencing primer(s), reference accession or exact construct sequence and the regions actually covered. NCBI recommends accession-based template identity when possible for specificity work, and Sanger data must be aligned to the intended reference rather than judged from a visually plausible chromatogram alone ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/), [NCBI primer-design guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/guide/howto/design-pcr-primers/), [NCBI GenBank](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/genbank/)).

For the capillary-Sanger branch, the AAVLD guidance reports typical automated read support of about `800–1,000 bp`, low-quality sequence near the first `15–40 bp`, Phred quality score `≥20` as reliable base identification, and a rule of thumb of `<5%` ambiguous bases after trimming; overlapping peaks or secondary peaks at about `≥20%` of the main peak require a mixed/low-quality interpretation or repeat. These are sequencing-quality guidance values, not colony-PCR acceptance limits, and the application-specific criterion may be stricter ([Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833)).

Minimum confirmation outcome ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/)):

- A unique expected-size band plus clean, adequately covered sequence matching the intended insert and required junctions: `identity-confirmed` for the covered regions.
- A unique band with incomplete coverage, poor-quality ends, ambiguous/mixed chromatogram, or only a short internal read: `identity-unconfirmed`.
- A sequence mismatch, unexpected junction, mixed sequence or non-target alignment: `screen-failed` or `mixed/contaminated`, with the exact evidence retained.

Full-plasmid identity, copy number, rearrangements outside the sequenced regions and colony homogeneity remain outside a single endpoint colony-PCR plus partial-Sanger result; use miniprep/long-read/whole-plasmid or re-isolation validation when those claims matter ([Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833), [NHGRI DNA sequence validation workshop](https://www.genome.gov/10001513/nhgri-workshop-on-dna-sequence-validation)).

- Addgene colony-PCR screening logic: insert-specific presence, vector-flank size/presence, insert+vector orientation screen, avoid excessive colony biomass, and sequence final positives: [Addgene colony PCR](https://blog.addgene.org/plasmids-101-colony-pcr)

ISO distinguishes these roles, and WHO requires well-characterized positive and negative controls with contamination controls ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [WHO good laboratory practice](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/68762/WHO_IVB_04.10.pdf)). | Positive-control failure or target-sized NTC/negative-process signal invalidates the corresponding run interpretation; exact repeat action belongs to the laboratory SOP. |
| Named chemistry and lysis | Record the actual vendor/product, direct-transfer versus water/TE lysate branch, colony amount, lysis temperature/time, cooling/spin step, reaction volume, primer concentration, Mg²⁺/buffer and cycle programme. The cited NEB, Thermo, Promega and Sigma protocols intentionally disagree on these values ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Thermo BAC/PAC screening manual](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/custom_screening_service_man.pdf), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf), [Sigma-Aldrich colony-PCR guidance](https://www.sigmaaldrich.com/US/en/technical-documents/technical-article/genomics/pcr/colony-pcr)). | A result without a named chemistry/lysis branch is not transferable to another vendor, host or protocol. |
| Gel observation | Record ladder identity, agarose percentage, buffer, loading volume/dye, run/imaging metadata and observed band count/size/intensity; use the manufacturer separation ranges only as planning guidance ([Thermo nucleic-acid electrophoresis guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/in/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/na-electrophoresis-education/na-electrophoresis-workflow.html), [WHO agarose gel guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/336499/9789240009424-eng.pdf)). | A screen-positive call requires a unique expected-geometry band in a control-valid gel; unresolved bands/smears are `screen-inconclusive`. |
| False-positive audit | For insert-only or junction assays, record vector/backbone primer use, carryover controls and any NTC/plate/ligation contamination investigation. Agar/ligated-insert carryover is documented as a false-positive mechanism ([Dallas-Yang et al.](https://doi.org/10.2144/98244bm14), [Agrawal & Roy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ab.2007.11.029)). | A positive band with a failed contamination control is not an identity call, even if its size is expected. |
| Sequencing confirmation | Retain the colony/replica ID, source material, PCR and sequencing primers, reference accession/construct, covered bases, trimming, quality metrics and chromatogram review. A unique band should be purified before amplicon sequencing; Sanger calls must be aligned to the intended reference ([NEB M0689 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/supplemental-protocol-2-using-colony-pcr-to-identify-positive-clones-neb-m0689), [Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833), [NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/)). | A band alone is `screen-compatible, identity-unconfirmed`; only clean, adequately covered sequence matching the required insert/junctions is `identity-confirmed` for those covered regions. |
| Internal execution boundary | **Internal software contract:** the engine records design/profile/validation metadata but does not itself select a colony, perform lysis, run a thermocycler, inspect a gel or generate a chromatogram. | No automated result may be reported as physically verified without externally supplied observations. |

## Unresolved evidence gaps

**Internal audit state:** the evidence search and parameter reconciliation are complete for the evidence currently available, but these gaps remain real and must not be hidden behind the internal profile:

- **Host breadth:** No single cited study or manufacturer protocol establishes one direct-transfer lysis, biomass or cycle programme across bacteria, yeast, filamentous fungi and microalgae; the published workflows are organism- and lysis-specific ([Azevedo et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28540704/), [Packeiser et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12010-012-0043-8), [Colony-PCR of hyphomycetes](https://www.mdpi.com/2309-608X/2/2/12), [Chlorella colony PCR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3835426/)).
- **Cross-vendor optimum:** The available official protocols provide product-specific starting values and genuine conflicts (`2`, `2–5`, `3`, `5` and `5–10` minute heat/lysis branches, among others); no cited comparative study resolves a universal optimum across these chemistries ([NEB OneTaq application note](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/application-notes/appnote_colony_pcr.pdf), [Thermo PCR Master Mix FAQ](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/K0172/faqs), [Promega GoTaq Hot Start clone-screening note](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/promega-notes/101/recombinant-clone-screening-using-the-gotaq-hot-start-green-master-mix.pdf)).
- **Internal profile validation:** **Internal software contract:** the `150–500 bp` design envelope has no assay-specific wet-lab sensitivity, specificity, false-positive rate or false-negative rate in this repository record. Host/preparation/SOP provenance is required, and lysis/cycle values remain external protocol observations rather than inferred software defaults.
- **Physical-control coverage:** **Internal software contract:** this engine cannot verify that positive-process, negative-host, empty-vector, NTC or gel controls were actually run. A laboratory must supply the observations and its repeat/decontamination SOP; ISO/WHO control definitions do not supply the missing physical measurements ([ISO 22174 control definitions](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en), [WHO PCR laboratory guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/70765/WHO_IVB_11.09_eng.pdf)).
- **Multiplex branch:** Dedicated multiplex guidance gives concentration ranges and interaction checks, but does not validate this module's crude colony template, biomass tolerance, product-spacing rule or primer balance; singleplex-to-multiplex wet-lab validation is still required ([NEB Multiplex PCR guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/multiplex-pcr-guidelines-for-multiplex-pcr-5x-master-mix-m0284), [NCBI multiplex-PCR guidance](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK305477/)).
- **Gel acceptance tolerance:** Manufacturer gel ranges and loading examples do not define the size tolerance, intensity threshold, imaging threshold or replicate rule for this assay; those criteria remain platform- and laboratory-specific ([Thermo nucleic-acid electrophoresis guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/in/en/home/life-science/cloning/cloning-learning-center/invitrogen-school-of-molecular-biology/na-electrophoresis-education/na-electrophoresis-workflow.html), [WHO agarose gel guidance](https://iris.who.int/bitstream/handle/10665/336499/9789240009424-eng.pdf)).
- **Sequence scope:** Partial Sanger confirmation cannot establish full-plasmid identity, copy number, rearrangements outside covered regions or colony homogeneity; miniprep/whole-plasmid, long-read or re-isolation validation is needed when those claims matter ([Crossley et al.](https://doi.org/10.1177/1040638720905833), [NHGRI DNA sequence validation workshop](https://www.genome.gov/10001513/nhgri-workshop-on-dna-sequence-validation)).
