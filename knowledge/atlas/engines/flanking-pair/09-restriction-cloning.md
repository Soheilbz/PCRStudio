## Evidence collected

Restriction-cloning primers have two chemically different regions: the 3-prime annealing segment and the 5-prime ordered tail. Recognition sites must be absent from the insert, orientation/overhang must match the vector, and extra 5-prime bases are needed for efficient cleavage ([NEB cleavage guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments), [Moreira & Noren](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7669297/)). Protective-base count and sequence are enzyme-dependent; generic tail text cannot prove cleavage efficiency. Added sequence also needs palindrome and primer-dimer review ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)).

Adjacent sequence is a real restriction-enzyme variable, but it is not a universal “AT is always better” rule. In a controlled EcoRI oligonucleotide study, A/T base pairs adjacent to the recognition site enhanced cleavage while G/C base pairs slowed it, and cleavage also depended on the duplex substrate; this supports enzyme-specific flanking-sequence evidence rather than a cross-enzyme filler heuristic ([Alves et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6323183/)).

Golden Gate assembly requires a stronger model than checking whether two ends are Watson–Crick complements. Type IIS digestion makes user-defined junctions, but the ligase can also join non-complementary ends; in a systematic assay, mispairing was common, each four-base overhang had multiple measurable non-complementary partners, and junction efficiency was not reducible to GC content or a single adjacent-base rule ([Pryor et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0238592)). Therefore an assembly record must keep the selected Type IIS enzyme, ligase, reaction profile, ordered fragment graph, every junction overhang, and all internal recognition-site findings together before it can make a fidelity claim ([Pryor et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0238592), [NEB Golden Gate overview](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-assembly-and-cloning/golden-gate-assembly)).

Ligation fidelity is empirical and ligase-specific rather than a transferable property of an overhang string. The comprehensive T4 DNA-ligase assay measured mismatch tolerance and sequence bias across three-base overhangs and found strongly inefficient ligation for some sequence classes; it also states that the method must be extended or remeasured for other ligases, end types and reaction contexts ([Potapov et al.](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/46/13/e79/4993781)). The Golden Gate profiling study likewise found that, under its tested conditions, fidelity and bias were driven primarily by the ligase and reaction conditions, while the Type IIS enzyme choice had a smaller effect; its measurements cannot be promoted to a universal matrix for a different ligase, buffer or cycling program ([Pryor et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0238592)).

Assembly complexity must be treated as a first-class input. Conventional Golden Gate workflows commonly join five to eight fragments, while yield and accuracy can fall as complexity grows; data-optimized assembly has demonstrated larger assemblies only by using measured ligation data and an explicitly designed overhang set ([Sikkema et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37755329/), [Pryor et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0238592)). The same high-complexity protocol identifies amplicon purity and molar stoichiometric balance as outcome-relevant variables, so the engine must not silently infer either from sequence length or assign one universal fragment ratio ([Sikkema et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37755329/)).

Overhang thermodynamics is a useful secondary signal, not a universal pass/fail rule. A 2024 ten-fragment study found that stronger overhangs improved efficiency in its tested BsaI-HFv2/T4-ligase conditions while retaining high fidelity, but the conclusion is tied to that assay and does not replace measured ligase/condition-specific fidelity data ([Strzelecki et al.](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/19/e95/7786162)). The implementation must consequently distinguish junction compatibility, predicted fidelity, ligation efficiency, reaction provenance and experimental confirmation instead of collapsing them into one “good overhang” score ([Strzelecki et al.](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/19/e95/7786162), [Pryor et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0238592)).

## Current implementation assessment

The design decision keeps tails out of annealing Tm and rejects enzymes whose sites occur in the supplied insert; the distinction between the annealing core and the complete ordered oligo is required by the [Primer3 input semantics](https://primer3.org/manual.html) and the [NEB cleavage guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments). Every selected enzyme still needs a validated protective-base rule and complete ordered-oligo interaction review. The executable Gen-1 branch now requires an exact circular recipient-vector sequence, but exact enzyme formulation, methylation, double-digest and final junction/frame claims remain separate evidence gates.

Recognition-site scanning checks both the catalogue spelling and its reverse complement for non-palindromic enzymes, with the cut coordinate mirrored inside the site. The shipped bench catalogue is mostly palindromic, so this is a forward-compatibility and correctness rule rather than a change to the current common-enzyme counts. NEB's recognition-specificity table writes recognition sequences 5′→3′ and records strand-specific cleavage offsets, which is the convention used for this decision ([NEB recognition sequences](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/selection-charts/alphabetized-list-of-recognition-specificities)).

For palindromic sites whose opposite-strand cleavage is derivable by symmetry, the runtime now reports blunt/5′/3′ end polarity, the exposed overhang and whether the **two insert-end families** are sequence-compatible. This catches cases such as BamHI/BglII or XbaI/SpeI, where different enzyme names can generate compatible cohesive ends ([NEB compatible cohesive ends](https://www.neb.com/en-ca/tools-and-resources/selection-charts/compatible-cohesive-ends-and-generation-of-new-restriction-sites)). The result deliberately does **not** infer vector compatibility, directionality, ligation efficiency or whether the hybrid ligation product is recleavable; NEB's compatibility table shows that recleavage is combination-dependent, so that question requires an explicit enzyme-pair/junction record rather than overhang equality alone.

The full ordered-oligo interaction is retained in the pair contract and shown in the result UI. It is deliberately presented as an in-silico review flag rather than an automatic wet-lab failure; tail-tail duplex geometry does not by itself establish that a productive primer dimer will extend ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)).

The executable Generation-1 branch is deliberately narrower than a generic numeric tail editor. [NEB's current guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments) recommends six base pairs for enzymes not represented in its table and also gives six base pairs as the Golden Gate starting recommendation; the older downloadable table records a six-to-eight-base alternative, so the two versions must not be silently merged. `[internal software-contract]` `tailProtocol=neb-general-6bp` therefore requires exactly **six explicit A/C/G/T protective bases on each end**. PCRStudio does not invent their sequence and no arbitrary or per-end numeric override is accepted in this named branch; another required length needs a separately named supplier/enzyme-specific authority. The catalogue still carries the supplier table's first non-minus flanking-base observation as descriptive evidence where available, not as an executable rewrite or a universal cleavage guarantee. Unknown enzymes remain unclassified (`null`) because supplier formulation and substrate context still matter ([Moreira & Noren](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7669297/)).

## Digest, dephosphorylation and ligation audit

The restriction-cloning hand-off must distinguish a sequence design from a validated digest. Recognition-site absence, a designed tail and a predicted overhang do not establish complete cleavage: buffer, temperature, incubation time, enzyme formulation, DNA topology, methylation, contaminants and the molar concentration of recognition sites all affect the reaction ([Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf), [REBASE](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/D1/D629/6786197)).

### Restriction-site and flanking-base design

For ordinary Type II restriction cloning, choose one or two sites that flank the insert, are unique in the intended vector, do not occur inside the insert, and provide the desired orientation; if the two ends are compatible or blunt, vector recircularization becomes a background risk and must be handled explicitly ([NEB restriction-enzyme digestion](https://www.neb.com/en-us/applications/cloning-and-synthetic-biology/dna-preparation/restriction-enzyme-digestion), [Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/)). The enzyme record must include recognition sequence, cleavage coordinates, overhang type/sequence, methylation sensitivity, formulation and supplier; REBASE is a referenced database for recognition/cleavage and methylation information, but the current product manual remains authoritative for the purchased formulation ([NEB recognition sequences](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/selection-charts/alphabetized-list-of-recognition-specificities), [REBASE](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/D1/D629/6786197)).

The protective-base evidence is deliberately non-universal. NEB's current guidance recommends `6 bp` for unlisted enzymes and for Golden Gate, while its enzyme table and older downloadable records show enzyme-specific variation; Promega reports that `≥4 bp` is advisable for the majority of enzymes, and a terminal-site experiment found enzyme-dependent outcomes at `0`, `1`, `2` and `3 bp` from the product end ([NEB cleavage guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments), [Promega Subcloning Notebook](https://www.promega.com/~/media/files/resources/product%20guides/subcloning%20notebook/pcr_subcloning_row.pdf?la=en), [Zimmermann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9564528/)). Therefore `6 bp` remains only a transparent profile starting point; a product-specific minimum, experimental terminal-site result or vendor formulation must override it, and the ordered tail must still be checked for palindromes, primer dimers and unintended sites ([NEB cleavage guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-ca/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments)).

Adjacent sequence can change cleavage kinetics without supporting a cross-enzyme filler rule. The EcoRI study in this record found sequence- and duplex-context effects, whereas the terminal-PCR study found different minimum distances for different enzymes; these data support enzyme-specific advisories only ([Alves et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6323183/), [Zimmermann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9564528/)).

### Double-digest compatibility and failure modes

For NEB standard enzymes, first test the current performance chart/Double Digest Finder rather than assuming a shared buffer. NEB reports that more than `210` enzymes are `100%` active in rCutSmart; otherwise choose the buffer with the highest common activity, account for star activity, and do not treat a legacy buffer table as a substitute for the current product record ([NEB Double Digest Protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-au/protocols/double-digest-protocol-with-standard-restriction-enzymes), [NEB performance chart](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/nebuffer-performance-chart-with-restriction-enzymes)).

When both NEB enzymes are compatible with rCutSmart, NEB's double-digest guidance uses `5–10 U/µg` DNA in a `50 µl` reaction; it gives `5–15 min` when both enzymes are Time-Saver qualified and `1 h` when they are standard enzymes. The incubation still belongs to the enzyme/formulation record, because individual product pages can specify alternate temperatures, time, heat inactivation or methylation constraints ([NEB double-digest video/protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/video-library/neb-restriction-enzyme-double-digest-protocol), [NEB restriction digest protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/restriction-digest-protocol)).

If no common buffer gives both enzymes more than `50%` activity, NEB's sequential branch uses the enzyme with the lower-salt buffer first, then adjusts salt toward the second enzyme's conditions; NEB gives `0 mM` salt for 1X rCutSmart/NEBuffer r1.1, `50 mM NaCl` for 1X r2.1 and `100 mM NaCl` for 1X r3.1 as reference values. Heat-inactivate the first enzyme when its product permits it, or clean up the DNA before adding the second enzyme ([NEB Double Digest Protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-au/protocols/double-digest-protocol-with-standard-restriction-enzymes)). A one-tube digest is therefore a named buffer/formulation decision, not a generic “double digest” setting.

NEB's RE-Mix branches are separate from standard-enzyme double digests: one RE-Mix plus one standard enzyme uses up to `1 µg` DNA in `17 µl` water, `2 µl` 10X RE-Mix and `1 µl` standard enzyme, incubated at `37 °C` for `15 min` for Time-Saver enzymes or `1 h` for standard enzymes; the two-RE-Mix branch uses up to `1 µg` DNA, `2 µl` of each 10X RE-Mix and `15 min` at `37 °C`, with a `20 µl`/`1 µl`-each alternative requiring at least `30 min`. The one-RE-Mix protocol names incompatible standard enzymes for which sequential digestion is recommended ([NEB one-RE-Mix protocol](https://www.neb.com/protocols/double-digest-protocol-using-one-re-mix-and-one-standard-restriction-enzyme), [NEB two-RE-Mix protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/double-digest-using-re-mix)).

Thermo branches must remain product-specific. Thermo's FastDigest and Anza systems use their own buffer and heat-inactivation rules; the Anza manual defines a universal `37 °C/15 min` digestion symbol and `80 °C/20 min` complete heat-inactivation symbol, while individual enzymes can be assigned alternative temperatures, methylation sensitivities or no heat-inactivation. The runtime must store the actual product manual and not translate an Anza or FastDigest value into an NEB value ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf), [Thermo FastDigest BclI guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0012494_FastDigest_BclI_UG.pdf)).

Promega's compatibility branch is also not equivalent to NEB's. Its enzyme-specific optimal buffer is defined as `100%` activity for the accompanying enzyme; MULTI-CORE is supplied for enzymes with at least `25%` activity in that buffer and may be useful for multiple enzymes while compromising individual activity. Promega recommends sequential reactions when enzyme requirements differ substantially, with buffer or salt adjustment before the second enzyme ([Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf)).

The Thermo Anza manual also carries an enzyme-by-enzyme terminal-flank table: its values were tested with `0.2 µg` PCR product in `20 µl` at `37 °C` for `15 min`. Representative entries are NotI `2`, NcoI `3`, BamHI `2`, NheI `5`, XhoI `4`, NdeI `6`, EcoRI `3`, XbaI `2` and HindIII `6` additional bases on each side of the recognition site. These values conflict with the NEB general `6 bp` starting rule and the Promega `≥4 bp` majority-enzyme advice; the selected Anza enzyme's table entry must be preserved as a vendor/test-condition advisory, not normalized across vendors ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf), [NEB cleavage guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/cleavage-close-to-the-end-of-dna-fragments), [Promega Subcloning Notebook](https://www.promega.com/~/media/files/resources/product%20guides/subcloning%20notebook/pcr_subcloning_row.pdf?la=en)).

Anza's digest input is also a separate branch: for one or two enzymes it uses `0.2–1 µg` DNA in `20 µl` with `2 µl` 10X buffer and `1 µl` of each enzyme; for three enzymes it uses `3 µl` buffer and `30 µl` total volume, and the manual permits linear scaling up to `5×`. Under the named Anza protocol, `1 µl` enzyme or enzyme mixture is reported as complete within `15 min` and flexible for up to `16 h` without deleterious star activity; this is a formulation claim tied to that protocol, not evidence for other suppliers ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf)).

NEB's current restriction-enzyme handling tips also strengthen the post-PCR handoff: cleanup of PCR fragments before digestion is recommended because PCR components can inhibit restriction enzymes and residual polymerase can modify freshly cut ends by blunting them; to limit star activity, final glycerol should not exceed `5%`, excessive enzyme and prolonged incubation should be avoided, and heat inactivation is recommended when the chosen enzyme permits it before downstream cloning ([NEB restriction-enzyme tips](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/restriction-enzyme-tips)). These are digest-workflow controls, not primer-score thresholds.

### Star activity, methylation and DNA quality

Star activity is a loss of cleavage specificity under off-target reaction conditions, not a sequence-only property. A primary EcoRI study linked star activity to osmotic pressure, and the Fidelity Index study lists high glycerol (`>5% v/v`), high enzyme:DNA ratio (usually `>100 U/µg` DNA), low ionic strength (`<25 mM` salt), high pH (`>8.0`), organic solvents and Mn²⁺/Co²⁺ substitution as general risk conditions; individual enzymes differ ([Robinson & Sligar](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8230215/), [Fidelity Index study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2396408/)). NEB's practical rule is final glycerol `<5%`; in a `50 µl` reaction it gives a total enzyme-volume ceiling of `5 µl` as the example that keeps this limit ([NEB Double Digest Protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-au/protocols/double-digest-protocol-with-standard-restriction-enzymes)). These thresholds are risk controls, not universal safe/unsafe boundaries.

Methylation must be evaluated against the actual DNA source. Dam, Dcm and CpG effects can block or impair cleavage when the modification overlaps the recognition site; a sequence-only scan cannot infer whether plasmid DNA was propagated in a methylating host. Record host/strain, methylation state if known, enzyme sensitivity and any dam⁻/dcm⁻ rescue branch ([NEB methylation guidance](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/effects-of-cpg-methylation-on-restriction-enzyme-cleavage), [REBASE](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/51/D1/D629/6786197), [Thermo methylation FAQ](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/15226012/faqs)).

DNA mass alone is insufficient for all substrates. Promega states that supercoiled plasmid DNA or DNA containing many target sites can require `2–10×` more units per microgram than the linear unit-assay substrate; for PCR products and oligonucleotides, enzyme recognition-site molarity must be considered. Salt, EDTA, alcohol, detergent, organic solvent, primers and primer dimers can inhibit or compete with the digest, and adding more DNA volume increases contaminant carryover ([Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf), [Promega Subcloning Notebook](https://www.promega.com/~/media/files/resources/product%20guides/subcloning%20notebook/pcr_subcloning_row.pdf?la=en)). A failed digest must therefore branch into incomplete cleavage, methylation block, terminal-site failure, buffer/temperature mismatch, contamination or star activity rather than being reported as a primer-design failure.

### Vector dephosphorylation

Dephosphorylation is conditional: it is commonly required when a single enzyme, compatible cohesive ends or blunt ends allow vector self-ligation, but it is not a universal step for every directional two-enzyme digest ([Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/), [NEB traditional cloning guide](https://www.neb.com/en/new-to-cloning/~/link.aspx?_id=AE160791EC294352AF887CCFD1CFEBB6&_z=z)). The phosphatase must be applied to the vector branch only, or the insert must be separated before ligation; a one-tube phosphatase treatment that also dephosphorylates the insert can suppress the intended ligation ([Pyrite cloning study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6192209/)).

The current NEB Quick CIP branch is `1 µl` per `1 pmol` DNA ends (about `1 µg` of a `3 kb` plasmid) in `2 µl` 10X rCutSmart, to `20 µl`, at `37 °C` for `10 min`, followed by `80 °C` for `2 min`; NEB allows it during or after the digest. If any restriction enzyme in that reaction cannot be heat-inactivated, DNA cleanup is required before ligation ([NEB Quick CIP protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/protocol-for-dephosphorylation-of-5-ends-of-dna-using-quick-cip-neb-m0525), [NEB end-modification tips](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/end-modification-tips)). Do not transfer these conditions to rSAP, Antarctic phosphatase, CIP, Thermo Anza alkaline phosphatase or another supplier without that product's manual.

NEB's phosphatase selection chart preserves a formulation conflict that must remain visible: Quick CIP is listed as `80 °C/2 min`, rSAP as `65 °C/5 min`, and Antarctic phosphatase as `80 °C/2 min`; Antarctic phosphatase requires a Zn²⁺ additive, while Quick CIP is the supplier's recommended rapid, additive-free branch ([NEB phosphatase selection chart](https://www.neb.com/en/tools-and-resources/selection-charts/phosphatase-selection-chart)). Thermo Anza separately labels phosphatase handling as One-Step, Two-Step/Heat or Two-Step/Column; the selected branch determines whether heat inactivation or purification is mandatory ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf)).

For Thermo Anza, dephosphorylation is recommended for blunt or self-compatible vector ends, and the plasmid should be free of RNA and genomic DNA. The one-step branch combines `0.2–1 µg` plasmid, `2 µl` 10X Anza buffer, `1 µl` restriction enzyme and `1 µl` Anza alkaline phosphatase in `20 µl`, incubating at `37 °C/15 min` and `80 °C/20 min`; the two-step/heat branch uses `0.2–1 µg` digested plasmid in `19 µl` plus `1 µl` phosphatase, then `37 °C/15 min` and `80 °C/20 min`; the two-step/column branch uses `17 µl` purified DNA, `2 µl` buffer and `1 µl` phosphatase, then `37 °C/15 min` and `80 °C/5 min`. The selected restriction enzyme's Anza compatibility symbol controls whether one-step, heat or column handling is permitted ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf)).

For non-phosphorylated PCR amplicons, Thermo's Anza branch uses T4 PNK on `0.2–1 µg` linear dsDNA or `10–50 pmol` oligonucleotide in `20 µl`, with `2 µl` 10X PNK buffer and `1 µl` PNK, at `20 °C/15 min` followed by `80 °C/5 min`; this is required when a dephosphorylated vector is to be joined to a non-phosphorylated insert. Anza's separate blunt/end-repair branches use `0.2–1 µg` DNA, `2 µl` 10X buffer and `1 µl` enzyme mix in `20 µl` at `20 °C/15 min`, followed by purification; they are not interchangeable with restriction-site ligation ([Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf)).

### Ligation inputs, ratios and end chemistry

Restriction cleavage normally preserves 5′ phosphates and 3′ hydroxyls, but the ligase still requires compatible annealed ends and the correct phosphate state; dephosphorylated vector ends prevent vector-vector phosphodiester-bond formation until a phosphorylated insert is joined ([NEB cloning with restriction enzymes](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/video-library/cloning-with-restriction-enzymes), [Thermo vector dephosphorylation note](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/brochures/generating-recombinant-dna-clones-white-paper.pdf)). Record end type separately as cohesive, blunt, single-base overhang or incompatible; do not use a sticky-end default for a proofreading-polymerase product or a TA product ([Thermo TOPO-TA User Guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/topota_man.pdf), [Thermo Phusion guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0016317_Phusion_Green_Hot_Start_II_High_Fidelity_PCR_Master_Mix_UG.pdf)).

The NEB standard T4 ligase example is `20 µl` with a `4 kb` vector at `50 ng` (`0.020 pmol`), a `1 kb` insert at `37.5 ng` (`0.060 pmol`), `2 µl` 10X buffer and `1 µl` ligase; it uses a `1:3` vector:insert molar ratio, with cohesive-end incubation at `16 °C overnight` or room temperature for `10 min`, and a separate standard protocol reports heat-inactivation at `65 °C/10 min`. NEB also recommends `20–30 fmol` vector, total vector-plus-insert concentration `1–10 ng/µl`, and vector:insert ratios from `1:1` to `1:10` for single insertions (up to `1:20` for short adaptors); concentrations below `1 ng/µl` can promote intramolecular circularization ([NEB T4 ligase protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/dna-ligation-with-t4-dna-ligase-m0202)).

NEB's Quick Ligation branch is intentionally incompatible with the standard heat-stop assumption: combine `50 ng` vector with a `3-fold` molar excess of insert, adjust to `10 µl`, add `10 µl` 2X Quick Ligation Buffer and `1 µl` Quick T4 DNA Ligase, incubate at `25 °C/5 min`, chill on ice and transform or store at `−20 °C`; NEB warns not to heat-inactivate this reaction because transformation efficiency is reduced ([NEB Quick Ligation protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/setting-up-a-ligation-reaction-with-the-quick-ligation-kit-m2200)).

Thermo's general T4-ligase branch is a deliberately different protocol: cohesive-end ligation uses `10–100 ng` total DNA in `20 µl`, a `3:1` insert:vector ratio, `0.1 Weiss unit` and room temperature for `30–60 min`; its blunt-end branch uses `100–1000 ng`, the same nominal `3:1` ratio, `1.0 Weiss unit` and room temperature for `4 h` or `14–16 °C overnight`, with higher ligase concentration preferred for blunt or single-base ends. Thermo also recommends testing several ratios such as `1:5` and `1:10` when the first ratio is not known to be optimal ([Thermo T4 ligase buffer FAQ](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/46300018/faqs)). These values must not be merged with NEB units, buffers or incubation branches.

For a fragment with one cohesive end and one blunt end, Thermo directs users to the more stringent blunt-end branch: `5 U` ligase, `14 °C` and `16–24 h`, because the blunt end remains the limiting ligation step. This mixed-end case must not inherit the cohesive-end time merely because one end is sticky ([Thermo T4 ligase buffer FAQ](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/46300018/faqs)).

The primary Topcu study is a separate empirical condition set: after T4 polynucleotide kinase treatment, it reported highest recombination efficiency at a `3:1` insert:vector molar ratio, `10 µg/ml` total DNA, `20 U` T4 ligase, `12 h` and `4 °C` for its tested PCR-insert system ([Topcu](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11310983/)). It is evidence for that substrate and assay, not a universal ligation optimum. The current module must therefore preserve ratio, DNA concentration, ligase identity/unit definition, buffer, temperature and duration as one protocol object and emit `unresolved` when those fields are absent.

An independent primary study of T4 DNA ligase joining restriction-generated termini found maximal joining at `4 °C` under its assay conditions, with temperature sensitivity differing between blunt HaeIII ends and cohesive EcoRI/PstI ends. This supports preserving end type and temperature together, but does not establish one optimum for every ligase, overhang sequence or buffer ([Ferretti & Sgaramella](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6259621/)).

### DNA input and verification criteria

NEB's standard digest starting point is `1 µg` DNA in `50 µl` with `5 µl` 10X buffer and `5–10 U` enzyme, while Promega's analytical branch uses `0.2–1.5 µg` in `20 µl`, a `2–10×` enzyme excess and `1–4 h`; Promega notes that overnight digestion is usually unnecessary and may degrade DNA. Addgene's preparative subcloning branch instead recommends `1.5–2 µg` donor plasmid and `1 µg` recipient plasmid, with digestion for at least `4 h` and up to overnight to support gel purification. These are named workflow branches, not a universal DNA input ([NEB digest protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/restriction-digest-protocol), [Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf), [Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/)).

Direct digestion of PCR product is also branch-specific. Promega tested selected enzymes in a `25 µl` GoTaq/PCR-master-mix context with `1–6 U` enzyme and `2 h` incubation, but warns that residual thermostable polymerase can fill the newly made sticky ends and that purification can remove small restriction fragments that interfere with ligation. NEB's BsaI-HFv2 amplicon branch allows a specific product to enter assembly without purification only when a gel check shows a specific amplicon, limits the carryover to `≤1 µl`, and otherwise requires spin-column purification ([Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf), [NEB BsaI-HFv2 insert considerations](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/insert-considerations-when-using-neb-golden-gate-assembly-kit-bsai-hfv2-neb-e1601)).

The digest verification set should include an uncut experimental DNA lane, a commercially supplied control-DNA digest and a no-enzyme mock digest; Promega also recommends independent size markers in more than one gel lane ([Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf)). For cloning, keep a vector-only ligation control, and if its background is high, compare vector-only with and without ligase to distinguish uncut plasmid from self-ligation; Addgene also recommends an uncut sample during gel troubleshooting ([Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/)).

Post-cloning acceptance is not a band-size guess. Addgene's workflow picks `3–10` colonies according to control background, uses a diagnostic digest on `100–300 ng` purified plasmid and expects the vector and insert bands; single-enzyme or compatible-end cloning additionally needs an orientation-specific diagnostic digest. Colony PCR can screen presence/orientation, but sequencing is required to establish the final insert sequence and orientation ([Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/), [Thermo Anza User Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/Anza_man.pdf)). A correctly sized band, antibiotic-resistant colony or in-silico site absence is therefore an intermediate criterion, not final construct validation.

### Failure-mode routing

The report should route failures using evidence rather than one generic warning: (1) no/partial digest → check terminal flanking distance, site sequence, DNA topology, recognition-site molarity, buffer activity, temperature/time, methylation and contaminants; (2) unexpected extra bands → check star-activity risks, nuclease contamination and incorrect site/mapping assumptions; (3) high empty-vector background → check incomplete digest, self-compatible ends, phosphatase coverage and phosphatase inactivation/removal; (4) no colonies → check ligase buffer/ATP, end phosphorylation, compatible annealing, DNA input, transformation positive control and antibiotic; (5) correct size but wrong construct → check orientation, frame, junction, internal sites and sequence confirmation. Each branch is supported by the digest/ligation/verification protocols above ([NEB Double Digest Protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-au/protocols/double-digest-protocol-with-standard-restriction-enzymes), [Promega TM367](https://france.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/restriction-enzymes-protocol.pdf), [Addgene subcloning](https://www.addgene.org/protocols/subcloning/)).

## Numeric protocol overlay: NEBridge Golden Gate BsaI-HFv2

The NEBridge Golden Gate records are retained as named Type IIS assembly overlays. In the standard NEB E1601 formulation, the destination plasmid is `0.05 pmol` (the pGGAselect example is `75 ng`), each insert is `0.05 pmol` at an approximately `1:1` insert:vector molar ratio, the reaction contains `2 µL` of `10X` T4 DNA Ligase Buffer and `1–2 µL` Golden Gate Enzyme Mix, and water is added to `20 µL`. The standard multi-insert programme is `(37 °C/5 min → 16 °C/5 min) × 30`, then `60 °C/5 min`, followed by `4 °C`; the kit manual also documents a `37 °C/1 min → 16 °C/1 min` version for `30` cycles and permits extended cycling up to `60` cycles for this enzyme/ligase system ([NEBridge Golden Gate Assembly Protocol E1601](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/golden-gate-assembly-protocol-for-using-neb-golden-gate-assembly-mix-e1601), [NEBridge E1601 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale1601.pdf)).

The same supplier publishes higher-complexity branches that must not be flattened into the standard record. Its `24`-fragment protocol uses a `25 µL` reaction with `75 ng` destination plasmid, `75 ng` of each precloned insert, `2.5 µL` of `10X` ligase buffer, `0.5 µL` T4 DNA ligase at `2000 U/µL` (`1000 U`), and `1.5 µL` BsaI-HFv2 at `20 U/µL` (`30 U`); cycling is `(37 °C/5 min → 16 °C/5 min) × 30 → 60 °C/5 min → 4 °C`. The `52`-fragment example instead uses `20 µL`, `3 nM` of each insert, `2 µL` enzyme mix and `37 °C/48 h → 60 °C/5 min → 4 °C`. These are complexity- and protocol-specific records, not evidence for a universal fragment limit or concentration ([NEB 24-fragment protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/protocols/24-fragment-golden-gate-assembly-using-bsai-hfv2), [NEB 52-fragment protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/52-fragment-assembly-protocol-using-the-neb-golden-gate-assembly-kit-bsai-hfv2-neb-e1601)).

For the selected BsaI-HFv2 branch, the cut-offset record is also explicit: NEB writes the recognition/cutting pattern as `5′-GGTCTC(N1)/(N5)-3′`, which leaves a user-defined `4-base` overhang. The enzyme orientation, the four-base overhang on each end and the fragment order must therefore be stored together; recognition-site presence alone is not enough to infer a compatible junction ([BsaI-HFv2 product specification](https://www.neb.com/products/r3733-bsai-hf-v2)).

The module must preserve `assembly_protocol`, `fragment_count`, `reaction_volume`, `vector_molarity_or_mass`, `insert_molarity_or_mass`, `insert_to_vector_ratio`, `ligase_buffer`, `enzyme_volume`, `enzyme_units`, `cycle_count`, `temperature_time_schedule`, `terminal_hold`, `recognition_site`, `cut_offset` and `overhang_sequence` as one versioned protocol object. The current flanking-pair runtime does not yet execute a multi-fragment Golden Gate graph or ligase-fidelity model, so these values remain documentation/hand-off data until a dedicated assembly contract exists ([NEBridge E1601 manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manuale1601.pdf), [NEB Golden Gate optimisation guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-us/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/technical-tips-for-optimizing-golden-gate-assembly-reactions)).

## Current Gen-1 executable topology: exact submitted insert or bounded circular-donor extraction

Scientific-Strict Generation 1 treats the submitted linear sequence as the **exact insert to be retained end-to-end**. The annealing cores are pinned to the two submitted boundaries and the PCR product must therefore equal the full submitted insert. An ordinary flanking-pair search is not acceptable here because it may move both primers inward and return a convenient internal subamplicon while the user believes the complete insert is being cloned. Primer3 exposes forced left/right primer coordinates separately from supplied primer sequences, and PCRStudio also verifies the returned endpoint coordinates and exact product length as a postcondition. ([Primer3 source/API](https://github.com/primer3-org/primer3/blob/main/src/libprimer3.h))

The branch now accepts either an exact submitted linear insert or an exact circular donor plasmid with explicit zero-based `target_start` plus `target_length`; the latter is extracted deterministically and records donor/insert hashes plus whether the interval crosses the arbitrary origin. Arbitrary excluded primer-placement regions and user product-size overrides remain outside this reviewed topology ([Primer3 source/API](https://github.com/primer3-org/primer3/blob/main/src/libprimer3.h)). Known variant coordinates, when supplied through an API-compatible profile, may only remove a terminal candidate; they do not redefine the insert boundaries.

The generic downstream label `cloning` supplies no universal `500–3000 bp`, pair-ΔTm or GC-clamp validity envelope. Annealing-core constraints must come from the explicit assay/polymerase design profile; the cloning purpose itself only describes what the product will be used for ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)).

### R12 donor/coding/calculator closure

**Internal software contract.** Restriction cloning now separates four concerns: insert extraction, primer/tail design, source-backed bench workflow and coding/fusion intent. Circular-donor extraction uses explicit zero-based start+length with origin wrapping and hashes both donor and extracted insert.
