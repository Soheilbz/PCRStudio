## Evidence collected

Digital PCR is endpoint amplification followed by partition counting. Primer design remains qPCR-like, but acceptance also involves uniform partition behaviour, inhibition tolerance, a clean negative population, and an amplicon short enough for reliable endpoint amplification ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/), [digital-PCR review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4355013/)). dMIQE2020 treats primer/probe sequences, target identity, assay conditions, controls and partition/readout details as reporting requirements; they are not all computable from a sequence alone ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/)).

The dMIQE2020 terminology makes the non-sequence boundary concrete: `n` is the total/accepted partition count, `V_p` is partition volume, `k` and `w` are positive and negative counts, `lambda` is the mean target occupancy, and threshold, baseline, cluster separation and rain describe the measured fluorescence population ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/)). These are platform- and run-specific observations; there is no scientifically defensible universal default for them in a primer design request ([RDML/dMIQE resource](https://rdml.org/dmiqe.html)). The result therefore carries them as required acquisition and reporting fields, not as guessed values. When the request is RNA-derived, PCRStudio additionally promotes the no-RT control plus RNA-input/quality and reverse-transcription provenance to required validation evidence; final partition counts alone cannot establish RNA integrity, RT recovery or absence of contaminating DNA.

The standard one-target calculation is `lambda = -ln(w/n)` and concentration is `lambda / V_p`; the measured reaction volume is `n × V_p`, not necessarily the prereaction volume. The calculation requires the accepted/analysable partition count, negative count, partition-volume convention, dilution/rescaling and uncertainty method to be reported. A zero-negative or near-saturated well is not a universal “high concentration” success: it can leave too little information for the Poisson estimate ([dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [Bio-Rad ddPCR Applications Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6407.pdf)).

Partition-volume provenance is a material accuracy issue, not documentation trivia. Published measurements on Bio-Rad droplet systems differ from historical QuantaSoft assumptions: one reference-material study measured `0.834 nL` versus the then-used `0.91 nL`, while a later inter-laboratory study measured approximately `0.715–0.778 nL` against a software value of `0.85 nL`; the differences were associated with generator, cartridge and supermix conditions. Those results are platform/version-specific and do not replace the current vendor/software volume model ([Corbisier et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4336415/), [Laurens et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5670190/), [dPCR GMO assessment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6010488/)).

Primer-only EvaGreen and probe-based hydrolysis assays have different failure signatures. EvaGreen reports dsDNA and can also bind ssDNA; excess primer or input DNA can raise the negative signal, reduce cluster separation and expose primer-dimer/off-target populations. Hydrolysis probes add sequence-specific detection and are generally preferred for specificity and multiplexing, but probe hydrolysis, mismatches, fluorophore spillover, quencher choice and matrix-dependent signal loss still require validation ([Bio-Rad ddPCR Applications Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6407.pdf), [dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [multiplex dPCR validation study](https://academic.oup.com/lambio/article/78/1/ovae137/7929004)).

Inhibition is reduced by partitioning in some assays, not eliminated. In a 2025 chip-based EvaGreen environmental study, dPCR was less inhibited than qPCR by ethanol and humic acid but more inhibited by tannic acid; effects varied between extract operators and reagent lots. The study observed a dPCR inhibition signal at a `40%` extract-control spike, a tannic-acid `IC50` of approximately `0.5 ng/µL` with complete inhibition at `800 ng/µL`, and a humic-acid `IC50` of `7.45 ± 2.96 ng/µL` with complete inhibition at `12.5 ng/µL`. These are not cross-platform limits: they were measured with that assay, matrix and chip protocol, and fluorescence loss can masquerade as rain or cluster failure ([validated 16S chip-dPCR study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf115)).

An earlier primary ddPCR comparison likewise found greater tolerance than qPCR for SDS and heparin, but not for EDTA. The reported log-`IC50` separation favored ddPCR by `0.554` and `0.628` for two SDS targets and by `0.655` and `0.855` for two heparin targets, whereas the EDTA differences were only `0.116` and `0.0198`; inhibited positive droplets retained signal but required an analysis threshold appropriate to the inhibited run ([Sidstedt et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247175/)).

For low-biomass or rare-target specimens, extraction and reagent blanks are part of the assay, not optional decoration. The same study found contaminant microbial DNA in both dPCR and qPCR reagents that was not removed by its tested UV or DNase treatments, and it recommends at least `2` technical replicates for samples below `30 copies/µL`; increasing template volume above `4 µL` can improve precision only if inhibition remains acceptable ([validated 16S chip-dPCR study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf115)).

The current short-product profile is directionally correct, but product length and primer thermodynamics cannot guarantee low rain, no false positives, or acceptable partition occupancy ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/), [large-amplicon ddPCR study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653534/)). These need explicit validation/reporting fields, not fabricated design scores. Primer3 documents the thermodynamic input fields separately from the platform protocol ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)); actual assay concentrations, partition volume and platform protocol remain validation inputs ([Bio-Rad QX200 protocol](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/12005493enRevB.pdf)). For high-molecular-weight genomic DNA, template fragmentation can also be part of the pre-analytical design: dMIQE2020 and QIAcuity guidance describe restriction digestion as a way to reduce physical linkage and improve random partition distribution, but require that the chosen enzyme leave the target amplicon intact ([dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [QIAcuity User Manual Extension](https://qa.qiagen.com/us/resources/download.aspx?id=5d19083d-fa10-4ed2-88a0-2953d9947e0c&lang=en)).

### Audit boundary and transfer rule

The word “dPCR” does not identify one reaction volume, partition volume, partition count, fluorescence model, master mix, or software algorithm. Droplet, fixed-chamber, chip and crystal systems therefore appear as separate conditional branches below. A value is transferable only within the named vendor/platform/consumable/manual revision and assay chemistry; otherwise it is evidence for a validation question, not a runtime default ([dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html), [JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)).

For sequence specificity, the external database branch is also explicit: NCBI Primer-BLAST uses Primer3 plus BLAST/global alignment to screen forward/reverse, forward/forward and reverse/reverse combinations, and NCBI recommends a RefSeq accession where possible; this is an in-silico screen and not wet-lab specificity evidence ([NCBI Primer-BLAST information](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/primerinfo.html), [NCBI Primer-BLAST guide](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/guide/howto/design-pcr-primers/), [NCBI RefSeq](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/refseq/)).

## Deep-search enrichment — current dPCR platform and software generations

A deep search of current vendor resources identified material platform generations that were not represented in the previous record. These are kept as platform/software overlays rather than being promoted to primer-only defaults ([Bio-Rad digital PCR system guidance](https://www.bio-rad.com/en-us/category/digital-pcr)).

**QIAcuity software lifecycle.** QIAGEN's current resource page now lists QIAcuity Software Suite `3.5`, QIAcuity Instrument Control Software `3.5`, API `v3.5.0.0`, backup/restore scripts for version `3.5`, and a batch-specific Volume Precision Factor resource; the same page lists VPF `Version 13 (July 2026)` on one current regional resource mirror. QIAGEN also issued a December 2025 Important Note for an audit-trail logging issue in Software Suite versions `3.1` and `3.2`. These records make software version, API version, VPF version and audit-trail remediation explicit provenance fields. They do not invalidate historical `3.1.1` high-multiplex evidence, but `3.1.1` must no longer be called the current software release ([QIAGEN current QIAcuity resources](https://www.qiagen.com/cm/products/instruments-and-automation/pcr-instruments/qiacuity-digital-pcr-system), [QIAGEN 3.5/API/VPF resource listing](https://www.qiagen.com/es-mc/products/instruments-and-automation/pcr-instruments/qiacuity-digital-pcr-system)).

**Roche Digital LightCycler branch.** Roche's current Digital LightCycler system uses three plate formats: high-sensitivity `45 µL / 20,000 partitions`, universal `30 µL / 28,000 partitions`, and high-resolution `15 µL / 100,000 partitions`. The system is documented with `6` channels, `8–96` samples per run and `5×` DNA/RNA master mixes. These values are platform/plate properties and must be stored with plate identity rather than generalized as dPCR partition defaults ([Roche Digital LightCycler](https://diagnostics.roche.com/us/en/products/systems/digital-lightcycler-system-sys-362.html)).

**Stilla Nio/Ruby generation.** Stilla's current Nio family is a `7`-color Crystal Digital PCR platform, and current Ruby Chip documentation specifies `5–80 µL` total sample loading when distributed across up to `16` chambers, approximately `17,000` droplets per chamber, a smallest documented droplet volume of `0.22 nL`, and a vendor-stated `5-log` dynamic range. Stilla also markets color-combination multiplexing above the physical-channel count; such target-count claims are assay/software capabilities and must never be inferred from channel count alone ([Stilla Nio](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/), [Stilla Ruby Chip and reagents](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/multiplex-pcr/digital-pcr-reagents/)).

**Absolute Q official-source conflict.** Thermo Fisher's current US Absolute Q page describes `four` analytical optical channels while also listing `five` physical optical channels with ROX used as reference; another official regional page states `five optical channels available` while retaining the same five-channel/ROX-reference wording. The system also lists `20,480` microchambers per reaction and `95%` sample analyzed. PCRStudio therefore records an official-documentation conflict rather than resolving the analytical multiplex capacity from wording alone; assay/channel support must be tied to the exact instrument/software/manual revision ([Thermo Fisher US Absolute Q](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/life-science/pcr/real-time-pcr/real-time-pcr-instruments/quantstudio-systems/models/quantstudio-absolute-q.html), [Thermo Fisher regional Absolute Q](https://www.thermofisher.com/io/en/home/life-science/pcr/digital-pcr/quantstudio-absolute-q-system.html)).

**Template-neighbourhood GC context — 2026 evidence.** A Scientific Reports study using a plasmid containing a `300 bp` region at `79% GC` found that six nearby amplicons with only `38–61%` amplicon GC did not behave equivalently on the tested QIAcuity nanoplate dPCR workflow. The nearest amplicon recovered only `9%` of the reference value; restriction digestion that physically separated targets from the GC-rich region restored approximately `94–100%` recovery for the separated targets. DMSO `1.88–3.0%` yielded `99–105%` recovery in the study assay and betaine `0.75–1.5 M` yielded `96–102%`. The same proximity effect was not observed on the tested Bio-Rad ddPCR chemistry, but the authors explicitly state that this does not establish ddPCR immunity. PCRStudio therefore records template neighbourhood, fragmentation/digestion state, platform/master mix and pre-activation conditions separately from amplicon GC ([Scientific Reports 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-55452-6)).

**Rare-transcript / exon-junction dPCR branch.** A separate 2026 Scientific Reports study provides current design/validation evidence for ddPCR assays targeting exon–exon junctions and rare splice variants with hydrolysis-probe and EvaGreen readouts. This branch requires transcript/isoform and junction provenance plus empirical discrimination against highly similar junction backgrounds; it must not be collapsed into genomic-template specificity or inferred from a primer pair alone ([Scientific Reports 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-67542-6)).

| `validated_chip_eva_green_inhibition_values` | A 2025 chip-based EvaGreen 16S study found dPCR less susceptible than qPCR to ethanol and humic acid but more susceptible to tannic acid. | `sidstedt_ddPCR_inhibitor_comparison` | In a primary CMV ddPCR/qPCR comparison, ddPCR had a greater-than-half-log `IC50` advantage for SDS and heparin: log differences were `0.554` and `0.628` for two SDS targets and `0.655` and `0.855` for two heparin targets; EDTA differences were `0.116` and `0.0198` ([Sidstedt et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4247175/)). | Droplet ddPCR versus qPCR, CMV targets, inhibitor-specific and target-specific log-IC50 comparisons. | `matrix evidence`; retain inhibitor identity, target, threshold method and raw fluorescence. | The study supports conditional resilience, not an inhibitor-free claim; EDTA, thresholding and target effects demonstrate non-universal behavior. |
| `rt_ddpcr_inhibitor_and_rain_rule` | In a primary RT-ddPCR inhibitor study, practical “no relevant inhibition” was defined as `0% inhibition ±10%`; rain was operationally counted between the negative mean plus `3 SD` and the positive mean minus `3 SD` of accepted droplets ([RT-ddPCR inhibitor study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4307183/)). | Reverse-transcription ddPCR with PMMoV RNA and plant/soil/water inhibitor matrices; study-specific operational definitions. | `RT/matrix validation example`; retain the no-inhibitor control, inhibitor identity, signal distribution and rain definition. | These cutoffs are not dMIQE/ISO universal gates and cannot be transferred to another target, RT chemistry or platform. |
| `biorad_rt_evagreen_cdna_input_example` | Bio-Rad's ddPCR EvaGreen gene-expression assay permits up to `6 µL` cDNA per `20 µL` reaction and limits that input to the equivalent of `50 ng` initial RNA; its standard mix uses `10 µL` 2× supermix and `1 µL` 20× primers for `250 nM` primers ([Bio-Rad ddPCR Gene Expression EvaGreen Assays](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/2022-07/10000156270.pdf)). | Named Bio-Rad QX200 EvaGreen RT/cDNA workflow; cDNA is post-RT input and does not prove RT recovery. | `RT platform overlay`; record RNA input, RT kit, cDNA volume, no-RT control and dilution. | The limit is product/protocol-specific and does not apply to one-step RT-dPCR, QIAcuity or Absolute Q. |
| `digital_rain_mechanism` | A mechanistic dPCR study distinguishes rain from reduced amplification efficiency versus template inaccessibility: reduced efficiency broadens the amplification-time distribution, while inaccessible template produces a distinct exponential-tail pattern; both can bias partition classification ([Berden et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36377427/)). | Peer-reviewed modeling plus experimental validation; no universal rain percentage. | `failure-mode classification`; retain the plot pattern, threshold method, template treatment and assay optimization evidence. | A threshold or primer score alone cannot identify the mechanism; raw partition/amplification data are required. |
| `ffpe_multiplex_rain_failure_mode` | A clinical multiplex ddPCR protocol observed more rain in heterogeneous FFPE-derived DNA than in fresh tissue/normal blood and more rain after multiplexing; inefficient amplification of degraded/heterogeneous templates was implicated ([robust multiplex ddPCR protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4990255/)). | Clinical tissue specimen and multiplex probe-ddPCR context. | `specimen/chemistry failure mode`; require FFPE DNA integrity and singleplex-to-multiplex validation. | The observation is not a universal FFPE threshold and does not establish that a given primer pair will fail on all FFPE extracts. |
| `jrc_dPCR_inhibition_validation` | For GMO dPCR methods, JRC/EURL guidance recommends at least `2` serial dilution levels, each in at least duplicate; dilution-adjusted copies per reaction should differ from the highest-concentration level by `≤25%` as an inhibition benchmark ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)). | Regulatory GMO-method validation branch; the highest concentration must meet the submitted PCR input requirement. | `validation criterion`; retain dilution factor, replicate count, copies/rxn and relative deviation. | The `25%` benchmark is a GMO-testing guidance value, not a universal release criterion for all specimen classes. |

| `jrc_gmo_dynamic_range_branch` | For combined GMO and taxon-specific PCR modules, JRC/EURL guidance specifies a dynamic range of at least `0.09–4.5%` GMO mass fraction, assessed for trueness and precision; copy-number-to-mass-fraction conversion must use the applicable certified-reference-material conversion factor or a justified zygosity factor ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)). | Regulated GMO food/feed relative-quantification branch; combined GM and taxon-specific modules only. | `regulated dynamic-range criterion`; retain the conversion factor, material identity and expected/observed scale. | This is not a general dPCR copies-per-reaction range and must not be transferred to clinical, environmental or single-target assays. |
| `jrc_gmo_specificity_control_material_branch` | In the JRC/EURL guidance for the NGT/GM-animal specificity branch, matrix or genomic-DNA reference materials are suitable for specificity testing, whereas plasmid control samples are not ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)). | Conditional GMO/NGT food/feed specificity-control branch. | `control-material selection`; retain matrix/species, reference-material identity and control type. | This is a scope-specific suitability rule; it does not establish that every dPCR specificity study must exclude plasmid controls. |
| `lob_lod_loq_conflicting_guidance` | A 2025 peer-reviewed multiplex-dPCR protocol recommends a prespecified rain impact below `10%`, notes that `≥3` positive droplets has been used as a detection threshold, and gives an example of `4/5` or `8/10` positive replicates for `95%` detection probability with more than `50%` posterior credibility. It also notes prior qPCR LoQ CoV precedents of `25–35%`, while recommending that dPCR report CoV continuously because it rises exponentially near zero ([multiplex dPCR validation study](https://doi.org/10.1093/lambio/ovae137)). | Laboratory recommendations and examples, not dMIQE or ISO universal gates. | `LoB/LoD/LoQ reporting`; retain the chosen statistical model, threshold, rain policy, replicate design and CoV/bias target. | These values conflict in status with dMIQE2020's non-prescriptive threshold guidance and cannot be silently enforced for every assay. |
| `jrc_amplicon_and_fragmentation_branch` | JRC recommends amplicons not exceeding `150 bp` for its GMO quantitative methods; a justified longer amplicon requires evidence of no relative-quantification bias on fragmented DNA. The same guidance requires restriction enzymes not to cut the amplicon, to be methylation-insensitive, and rejects non-reproducible random fragmentation such as sonication for that method branch ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)). | Regulated GMO food/feed method branch; it is stricter than some vendor research-use product suggestions. | `specimen/pre-analytical validation`; record DNA integrity, fragmentation/digest enzyme, recognition sites and matched target/reference amplicon evidence. | This does not make `150 bp` a physical universal dPCR limit; it is a conditional acceptance recommendation. |
| `qiacuity_software_311_multiplex_capability` | QIAcuity Software Suite `3.1.1` supports assays up to `12-plex` using amplitude multiplexing across `6` optical channels, with two amplicons in a channel and a custom cross-talk matrix feature ([QIAGEN QIAcuity product/software page](https://www.qiagen.com/cm/products/instruments-and-automation/pcr-instruments/qiacuity-digital-pcr-system), [Software Suite v3.1 release note](https://www.qiagen.com/us/resources/download.aspx?id=bb43619e-21aa-4aff-a66f-cb6982e6347f&lang=en)). | Versioned QIAcuity probe/multiplex platform capability. | `platform/software provenance`; retain software version, channel map and compensation/cross-talk settings. | This module is primer-only/dye scoped; the `12-plex` value must not be exposed as a capability of the current dye worker. |

The formulas and symbols above are retained as evidence records rather than implemented as a new calculator in this pass; a future result model may implement them only after the platform, accepted partitions, volume model and replicate structure are explicitly supplied ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117)).


## Current implementation assessment

- The 60–150 bp window and tighter pair-Tm difference are reasonable design defaults and should remain defaults, not guarantees ([dMIQE2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/clinchem/hvaa125), [digital-PCR review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4355013/)).
- The engine exposes product composition and specificity information; the dPCR response carries an explicit `in-silico-only` validation plan, separating design-time predictions from post-run partition QC, controls, threshold/rain handling and matrix validation ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/)).
- Multiplex compatibility must be evaluated at set level: pairs can pass alone and still interact or create unreadable cluster separation together ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/)).

## Extended dPCR evidence rules

dPCR is not made quantitative by a primer pair alone. dMIQE2020 requires the analysis to disclose partition classification, thresholding, partition volume, software, controls, replication, normalization/rescaling, uncertainty and the handling of ambiguous “rain”; the underlying concentration calculation relies on explicit partition and distribution assumptions ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117)).

| Additional rule | Resolution for primer-only dPCR | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Partition model | The design result must record that copy-number inference is downstream of partition count, defined/effective volume and the chosen statistical model; it must not infer concentration from product size or primer score ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)). | Partition volume may vary by platform and requires platform-specific documentation or validation. |
| Poisson assumptions | For the standard model, positive/negative discrimination, known/equal or modelled partition volume and random target distribution must be assessed before interpreting copy number ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117)). | The current engine does not inspect partitions or prove these assumptions. |
| Threshold and rain | Threshold method, positive/negative controls and the treatment of rain must be retained. Rain can arise from reduced efficiency, template accessibility, inhibition or sequence mismatch and cannot be universally discarded or included by a fixed rule ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [rain-mechanism study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36377427/)). | Classification is platform-, assay- and control-dependent. |
| Controls | Use NTCs, matrix/background negatives, positive controls that resemble the sample, and internal positive controls when a negative result must be shown amplifiable; low-copy work may need more distributed negative controls ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117)). | The engine specifies the control plan but cannot measure inhibition or contamination. |
| Uncertainty and replication | Report more than Poisson error: pipetting, sampling, partition-volume variation, extraction/RT and between-reaction repeatability can contribute to total uncertainty. Where the intended claim spans the full assay, replicate from an appropriate pre-analytical starting point rather than duplicating only the final partition/readout step, so extraction, RT when applicable, dilution, fragmentation and between-run handling are represented ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [variance-components study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25147026/)). | A primer-only result cannot provide a concentration interval or prove full-process repeatability/reproducibility. |
| Data exchange | Preserve raw/processed partition data and the software/threshold method needed for independent reanalysis; dMIQE2020 identifies RDML or an equivalent reproducible record as part of transparent reporting ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [RDML/dMIQE](https://rdml.org/dmiqe.html)). | The current module does not parse instrument partition files. |
| Occupancy and dilution | Plan loading/dilution so the platform has enough negative and positive partitions for the intended precision; dMIQE2020 notes that high occupancy, partition-volume variation and low-copy sampling can bias or widen the result, so the chosen dilution and replicate rationale must be recorded ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [Poisson Plus model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28851915/)). | No platform-independent occupancy target is imposed by the primer-only engine. |
| Template fragmentation | For high-molecular-weight gDNA, plasmid linkage or CNV assays, evaluate whether restriction digestion is needed before partitioning; if used, record the enzyme, digestion mode and evidence that neither target nor reference amplicon is cut ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [QIAcuity User Manual Extension](https://qa.qiagen.com/us/resources/download.aspx?id=5d19083d-fa10-4ed2-88a0-2953d9947e0c&lang=en), [Bio-Rad ddPCR Dx Universal Kit](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/12005433.pdf)). | The module can flag sequence-level incompatibility when the enzyme site and amplicon are known; it cannot prove complete digestion, methylation independence or recovery of fragmented input. |
| Concentration calculation | Use `lambda = -ln(w/n)` and `concentration = lambda/V_p`; report `n`, `w`, `k`, `V_p`, dilution/rescaling, software and the confidence-interval method ([dMIQE2020 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [Bio-Rad ddPCR Applications Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6407.pdf)). | The engine cannot infer `w`, `n`, `V_p`, occupancy, threshold or uncertainty from a primer pair. |
| Platform QC and no-call | QX200-specific software behavior includes a no-call for fewer than `10,000` droplets, quality score `<0.85` or too few negative droplets; QIAcuity uses image/reference-channel QC and Absolute Q reports QC-passed chamber counts ([Bio-Rad ddPCR Applications Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6407.pdf), [QIAcuity User Manual, 12/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-us/resources/download/usermanual/hb-2717-010-um-qiacuity-1224-ww), [Absolute Q existing-assay guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/GSD/Scientific%20Guides/quantstudio-absoluteq-using-existing-assays.pdf)). | Store vendor-specific no-call/invalid-image reason and accepted-partition/chamber count; never synthesize a cross-platform QC score. |
| Inhibition test | Use matrix-spiked material and at least two dilution levels in duplicate where the selected regulatory branch requires it; the JRC GMO benchmark is dilution-adjusted deviation `≤25%`. Inspect suppressed positive amplitude, elevated negative background, V-shaped signals and target-specific effects ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf), [validated 16S chip-dPCR study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf115), [Stilla inhibitor application note](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/digital-pcr/naica-application-notes/effect-of-inhibitors-crystal-digital-pcr-vs-qpcr/)). | No “inhibition-resistant” claim is emitted from sequence design; dilution can improve inhibitor tolerance but can also reduce rare-target molecules and must be validated. |
| LoB/LoD/LoQ and false positives | Establish a representative blank/LoB before LoD; empirical LoD commonly targets `95%` detection, while conditional examples include Stilla `≥30` blanks for stated `95%` confidence, prior `≥3` positive-droplet thresholds, and JRC GMO high-similarity `60` replicates with `≥59` positives ([Stilla LoB/LoD note](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/digital-pcr/naica-system-analysis/how-to-characterize-the-limit-of-blank-and-the-limit-of-detection-in-crystal-digital-pcr/), [multiplex dPCR validation study](https://doi.org/10.1093/lambio/ovae137), [JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf)). | Preserve the selected statistical standard and matrix; do not turn any example into a universal threshold. |
| Multiplex asymmetry | Evaluate each target singleplex first, then all combinations and expected abundance ratios; conditional JRC GMO guidance tests asymmetric LOQ/LOD with up to `20,000` copies per reaction of the other target(s), while Stilla's 6-color guidance requires spillover compensation ([JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf), [Stilla 6-color multiplex guidance](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/digital-pcr/naica-system-analysis/guidelines-for-6-color-multiplex-assay-design-for-optimized-performance-with-crystal-digital-pcr/)). | The current primer-only worker can expose pair interactions but cannot validate optical clusters, bleed-through or asymmetric performance. |

The dPCR module therefore returns an assay-design candidate and a platform-aware validation handoff, not a copy number, fractional abundance, empirical analytical sensitivity/specificity, validated measurement range, LoB/LoD/LoQ or “no inhibition” conclusion. The international quantification standard covers qPCR and dPCR performance evaluation and is currently marked for revision, so the selected edition and platform method must remain visible ([ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).

## Heavy-pass enrichment — standardized validation and uncertainty

`PCR-ValiPal` (Analytica Chimica Acta, 2026) provides a current cross-platform workflow for standardized dPCR validation and computes LoB, LoD, LoQ, precision, trueness and linearity. It is a validation workflow/tool, not an ISO standard and not an instrument capability claim. PCRStudio should therefore retain the validation software/version, input series/control materials and calculation method whenever such a workflow is used ([PCR-ValiPal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41730601/)).

Uncertainty estimation is also method-dependent. A 2025 iScience study introduced `NonPVar` and `BinomVar` and showed that simple binomial-assumption methods can misestimate standard error when additional variability is present. A dPCR result must therefore retain its variance/uncertainty method rather than implying that Poisson correction alone defines total measurement uncertainty ([Flexible methods for uncertainty estimation of digital PCR data](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2589004225000318)).

These methods complement dMIQE 2020 and ISO 20395; they do not replace platform-specific partition-volume, threshold/rain, accepted-partition and software provenance ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32145632/), [ISO 20395](https://www.iso.org/standard/68085.html)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

The following remain intentionally unresolved because the external literature and manuals make them assay/platform/matrix dependent rather than supplying one defensible universal value ([dMIQE2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32746458/)):

- No universal dPCR amplicon maximum: the conservative dMIQE/JRC recommendation is `≤150 bp`, while Bio-Rad publishes `60–200 bp` and `80–250 bp` research-use branches and a peer-reviewed probe assay quantified an amplicon up to `925 bp`; any override needs assay-specific evidence ([dMIQE2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/clinchem/hvaa125), [JRC/EURL GMFF dPCR performance requirements](https://gmo-crl.jrc.ec.europa.eu/doc/JRC125975_01.pdf), [Bio-Rad ddPCR Applications Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6407.pdf), [large-amplicon ddPCR study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6653534/)).
- No platform-independent accepted-partition count, RFU threshold, rain percentage, occupancy target, LoB/LoD/LoQ, or inhibitor concentration is defensible. The published examples above are tied to named software, platform, chemistry, matrix and statistical design ([dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).
- Absolute concentration remains sensitive to the partition-volume model. Historical Bio-Rad values (`0.91`, `0.868`, `0.834`, `0.85`, `0.715–0.778 nL`) and current QIAcuity values (`0.82` and `0.34 nL`, with optional VPF) cannot be averaged or substituted for one another ([Corbisier et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4336415/), [Laurens et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5670190/), [QIAcuity User Manual, 08/2025](https://www.qiagen.com/en-us/resources/download/usermanual/hb-2717-011-um-qiacuity-0825-ww)).
- Some platform-range values remain manufacturer guidance rather than independent validation: the QIAcuity copy-range FAQ is an assay-optimization article, and the Stilla Sapphire/Opal input/dynamic-range brochure is an older product document. They are retained as dated, conditional evidence; current kit revision, accepted partitions, effective volume and assay-specific precision still require confirmation for a deployed method ([QIAcuity assay-optimization FAQs](https://www.qiagen.com/us/knowledge-and-support/knowledge-hub/science-matters/pcr-solutions/top-10-most-frequently-asked-questions-about-digital-pcr-assay-optimization-on-the-qiacuity), [Stilla naica 6-color brochure](https://www.stillatechnologies.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/Stilla-naica-system-6-color-Brochure.pdf)).
- The Bio-Rad family vocabulary is broader than the executable dye catalogue. R13 now has exact reviewed naica ddPCR Mix / EvaGreen routing for QX700, Nio and naica, but QX600, QX ONE and QX Continuum still remain recognized platform identities without an inferred dye recipe unless a reviewed chemistry is explicitly promoted. Family-level marketing compatibility is not used as a substitute for model/kit/manual evidence ([Bio-Rad ddPCR instrument comparison](https://www.bio-rad.com/en-us/life-science/droplet-digital-pcr?ID=M9HE2R15), [naica ddPCR Mix IFU](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/2025-11/10000171490-naica-ddPCR-Mix-IFU.pdf)).
- Inhibition tolerance is not transferable across targets or matrices: the 2025 chip-EvaGreen study found opposite relative susceptibility for humic versus tannic acid, operator/lot variation and low-copy variability; it does not establish clinical blood, FFPE, plasma, wastewater or Bio-Rad limits ([validated 16S chip-dPCR study](https://doi.org/10.1093/ismeco/ycaf115)).
- The current document does not claim a completed empirical assay validation. A selected platform, kit/chemistry, specimen, target sequence, control material, raw partition dataset and intended use are still required to close the remaining performance evidence under the applicable standard/guideline ([ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html), [dMIQE2020](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/66/8/1012/5880117)).

### R13 platform / consumable / protocol routing

**Internal software contract.** The runtime separates **platform**, **consumable/partition format** and **dye chemistry** and validates both platform×consumable and protocol×platform pairs from generated canonical tables. QX200/QX600/QX ONE/QX700/QX Continuum, Bio-Rad Nio/naica and QIAcuity identities can therefore retain different sample/partition contexts without pretending they share one volume model. Effective partition volume and dilution evidence belong to the independent dPCR quantification/qualification layer and never modify primer ranking.
