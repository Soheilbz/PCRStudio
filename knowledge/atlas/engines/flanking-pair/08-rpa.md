### Runtime contamination/readout boundary (2026-09-03)

For the Thermo/Invitrogen Lyo-ready RPA/RT-RPA branch, the runtime now exposes a structured contamination-control hand-off because the current supplier manual identifies environmental/carry-over contamination as a false-positive source and recommends controls including NTC-first preparation, filtered tips and separation of endpoint amplicon analysis from setup when reaction vessels are opened. These controls are attached to that named branch rather than copied onto TwistAmp products without matching authority. The executable RPA contract remains `plain-acgt-two-primer`: modified Exo/Nfo/Fpg reporters, THF/dSpacer sites, fluorophores, quenchers, affinity labels and 3′ blocks remain non-executable until PCRStudio has a typed modified-oligo/readout model. Closed-tube detection can reduce contamination risk when a validated readout supports it, but PCRStudio does not infer a universal detection modality from the primer pair ([Thermo Lyo-ready Bst guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0029128-lyo-ready-Bst-DNA-polymerase_UG.pdf), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).
## Evidence collected

RPA primers are commonly 30–35 nt, with guidance to avoid extreme GC content and long homopolymers/repeats. Reviews describe an approximately 80–400 bp useful product span, with 100–200 bp often preferred ([RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/), [RPA review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7112910/)). Unlike PCR, primer melting temperature is not the primary loading mechanism: the recombinase system targets homologous DNA at a much lower operating temperature ([Piepenburg et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16756388/)). This does not remove specificity requirements; low-temperature reactions can amplify non-targets and design commonly needs empirical screening ([PrimedRPA](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6379019/)).

Mismatch behaviour is context-dependent rather than a universal switch. Experimental studies show that mismatch identity, count, position and sequence context can change RPA inhibition, including at the primer 3′ end ([RPA mismatch study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36116701/), [earlier RPA specificity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25481659/)). The engine therefore reports mismatch topology from the actual primer 3′ end as **descriptive evidence only** and requires empirical non-target challenge for the selected chemistry. It does not convert one terminal mismatch—or any fixed mismatch count—into a universal RPA exclusivity score, amplification probability or pass/fail threshold.

The current [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf) makes the distinction between a useful default and a biological law especially clear. It recommends 30–35 nt primers, warns against long homopolymers/repeats and primer interactions, and describes 100–200 bp as the preferred rapid-assay product range while advising that the amplicon generally remain below 500 bp. Its suggested software exploration settings are slightly wider (30–36 nt, 20–70% GC, Tm 50–100 °C and a maximum mononucleotide run of five); those are candidate-generation settings, not a claim that every resulting oligo is equally good. This build deliberately keeps the high-sensitivity default at 30–35 nt, 30–70% GC and 100–200 bp. A target that needs the documented broader envelope must be requested with an explicit constraint override and must retain the resulting validation note. The manual also recommends screening multiple candidate pairs experimentally: sequence alone cannot predict RPA activity, time-to-signal or product/noise ratio.

For probe-based detection, the probe is a chemically modified oligonucleotide, not a third plain DNA primer. Exo probes are typically 46–52 nt with an internal THF/dSpacer, a fluorophore and quencher arrangement, and a blocked 3′ end. Nfo probes use the THF cleavage architecture with a 5′ affinity tag and a 3′ polymerase-extension block; a commonly documented geometry has at least 30 nt before THF and about 15 nt after it. THF replaces a template base, it is not inserted between bases. Probe/primer dimers and probe hairpins must be screened, and the selected chemistry determines the readout (fluorescence, lateral flow or another format) ([RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). The plain ACGT oligo schema in this engine cannot encode these internal modifications or labels, so emitting a plain sequence as an Exo/Nfo/Fpg order would be unsafe.

The chemistry branches must remain separate. The original RPA implementation used T4 UvsX recombinase, T4 UvsY mediator, T4 gp32 single-stranded DNA-binding protein, Bsu strand-displacing polymerase, ATP and an ATP-regeneration/crowding system; in that specific reconstruction, reactions were run at `37 °C` for `120 min` with `300 nM` primers, `10 mM` MgOAc, `200 µM` total dNTPs, `3 mM` ATP, `20 mM` phosphocreatine, `100 ng/µl` creatine kinase, `600 ng/µl` gp32, `200 ng/µl` UvsX, `60 ng/µl` UvsY and `30 ng/µl` Bsu. These are a historical component-level recipe, not the current TwistAmp pellet composition or a universal RPA default ([Piepenburg et al., original RPA study](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204)).

The original RPA size experiment is a useful limit, not a hard universal cutoff: with its `37 °C/120 min` component recipe, a `300 bp` product produced some hairpin-mediated `2×` and `3×` length products, while RPA failed to produce the tested `1,500 bp` or larger products. The current profile's `100–200 bp` search window and the manufacturer's broader below-`500 bp` guidance should therefore be retained as assay-design starting points, while any longer target requires a named chemistry, readout and empirical product/sensitivity validation ([Piepenburg et al., original RPA study](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

SIBA is not a two-primer RPA preset. Its defining third oligo is a recombinase-loaded invasion oligonucleotide (IO) that opens the target duplex; the amplification primers are deliberately below the approximately `25–30 nt` length needed to form a recombinase filament, while the IO contains a `2′-O-methyl RNA` region at its `3′` end and leaves approximately `12–16 nt` of duplex peripheral to the insertion site. In the original SIBA demonstration, the two primers and IO were each `200 nM`, the reaction was `20 µl` at `40 °C` for at least `60 min`, and the component recipe included `10 mM` MgOAc, `2 mM` ATP, `200 µM` dNTPs, `5%` DMSO, `5%` PEG 1000, `250 ng/µl` gp32, `140 ng/µl` UvsX, `22 ng/µl` UvsY and `0.0625 U/µl` Bsu. The `2′-O-methyl` region is a specificity/extension-control feature; a plain RPA primer pair cannot stand in for the IO ([SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/)).

SIBA temperature and MgOAc values also remain assay-specific. In an independent *S. pyogenes* SIBA assay, target was detected from `37–48 °C`, fastest at `42–45 °C`, and not detected above `48 °C`; MgOAc was tested from `2.5–30 mM`, detection occurred above `5 mM`, the fastest range was `10–15 mM`, and concentrations above `20 mM` were inhibitory. The authors then used `43 °C` and `12.5 mM` MgOAc for that assay, with `300 nM` each primer and IO. These observations cannot be transferred to TwistAmp RPA or used to infer a universal SIBA optimum ([S. pyogenes SIBA study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7099210/)).

Inhibitor tolerance is matrix-, target-load- and assay-dependent, not a generic RPA property. Published RPA work reports activity in the presence of serum, `50 g/L` hemoglobin, `4% v/v` ethanol and `0.5 U` heparin, but whole blood can inhibit RPA because background genomic DNA is high; in one study, tolerated background DNA depended on the assay and a lateral-flow enrichment step enabled `10^4` HIV-1 copies in `0–14 µg` background DNA, whereas without enrichment the maximum reported background was `2 µg` with `10^6` target copies. The same study found that changing incubation time or primer concentration had little effect once background DNA was high ([Rohrman and Richards-Kortum](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ac504365v)). The current TwistAmp manual likewise warns that strong detergents, EDTA/EGTA, high salt and extreme acid/base can affect RPA, while offering `1%` Triton X-100, heating, or sample dilution with `0.2 M` NaOH/KOH as example lysis approaches; these are sample-preparation options to validate, not permitted-inhibitor thresholds ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

Carryover is a first-class RPA failure mode because high-yield amplicons and open endpoint readouts can aerosolize into later reactions. The original RPA study observed eventual nonspecific signal in water controls with dye-based monitoring, whereas a probe/nuclease readout added duplex-dependent proofreading; this reduces primer-artifact readout but does not remove laboratory contamination risk ([Piepenburg et al., original RPA study](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204)). CDC guidance for molecular amplification requires unidirectional movement through reagent preparation, sample preparation, amplification and detection areas and at least one NTC per run/unit; WHO likewise warns that amplicon aerosols can contaminate reagents, equipment and benches ([CDC molecular-genetic testing good laboratory practices](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [WHO molecular-testing dos and don'ts](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/case-management/diagnosis/nucleic-acid-amplification-based-diagnostics/dos-and-don-ts-for-molecular-testing)). A closed tube or sealed cartridge is a workflow control, not evidence that an assay is contamination-proof.

Readout changes what counts as a positive. Dye/gel methods can reveal product or total dsDNA but cannot reliably distinguish target from primer artefact; Exo fluorescence requires a correctly modified THF/fluorophore/quencher/3′-blocked probe and the intended nuclease; Nfo lateral flow requires the probe plus an opposing affinity-labelled primer and a valid strip control line. In an RPA-LFS primary study, a labelled-primer pair generated false-positive NTC strips, and even a probe format required screening because probe–primer complexes could generate signal; three deliberately introduced mismatches between probe and reverse primer eliminated that artefact in that assay without a significant amplification-efficiency loss. This `3-mismatch` result is an assay-specific engineering observation, not a universal probe rule ([Salmonella RPA-LFS artifact study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7324538/)).

Exo and Nfo are not interchangeable post-amplification reagents. The diagnostic review reports that Nfo can give lower signal and incomplete cleavage, which may leave product suitable for gel analysis, whereas Exo cleavage can degrade amplicon during handling; the intended probe, nuclease, termination method and readout therefore have to be validated together. Fpg is another distinct fluorescence branch rather than a drop-in sensitivity equivalent: a primary matched-target comparison reported `3–6 log10` lower sensitivity for Fpg than Exo in its tested viral panel ([RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/), [Development of a panel of RPA assays](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3666764/)).

A second current manufacturer branch is now available and must remain separate from TwistAmp. Thermo Fisher's Lyo-ready RPA Kit user guide (MAN1000697 Rev B, 6 March 2025) requires primers at least `30 nt` and gives an optimum `30–35 nt`, primer GC `30–70%`, amplicon GC `35–60%`, amplicon length `150–450 bp`, and advises avoiding repeats, secondary-structure regions, direct/inverted repeats, palindromes and 3′ primer-pair complementarity. It gives a broad amplification range of `34–45 °C` with `42 °C` optimum and a normal `20 min` hold; it also defines a named endpoint reaction with `0.3 µM` primers for singleplex, `0.1–0.3 µM` for multiplex optimization, `14 mM` MgCl₂, Bst polymerase `0.015–0.15 U/µL`, and a `42 °C/20 min` run ([Thermo Fisher Lyo-ready RPA Kit user guide](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN1000697-LyoReadyRPA-UG.pdf)). These values are formulation-specific and conflict in places with the narrower TwistAmp-oriented search window, so they are a selectable protocol overlay rather than a silent change to the `rpa` profile. For this named Thermo branch, the `35–60%` value is now enforced on the **whole predicted amplicon** as a post-search composition gate; it is not substituted for primer GC and does not become a universal RPA rule.

## Current implementation assessment

The profile captures the major length, GC, product and non-Tm direction described by the [RPA reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/) and [TwistAmp manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf). The chemistry boundary is also supported by the [foundational RPA mechanism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16756388/) and the [Exo/Nfo probe documentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/): a generic strand-displacement enzyme is not automatically an RPA chemistry, and a nuclease-dependent modified probe is not a plain DNA primer. Primer3's [manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html) supports retaining finite thermodynamic guards, but it does not provide a recombinase kinetic model; therefore Tm-centred PCR language must not be used as an RPA performance claim.

Require representative non-target challenge with the selected chemistry for an exclusivity claim ([RPA mismatch study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36116701/), [earlier RPA specificity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25481659/)). | `specificity.py` structured mismatch evidence and `validation_plan.py` empirical mismatch challenge. | The bounded ungapped scan does not model indels/bulges, all mismatch chemistries, recombinase kinetics or matrix effects. |
| Product size | hard starting window 100–200 bp | Keep the commonly useful window while documenting that assay format and read-out can change it ([TwistAmp manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | RPA profile constraints. | Probe/nfo formats and kits may impose different geometry. |
| Operating-temperature regime | kit-bound protocol input | Standard TwistAmp kits are configured for `37–42 °C`; lower temperatures may still produce product but slow the reaction and are not equally validated across kit families, with the Exo fluorescence system especially temperature-sensitive ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | `Cycling.isothermal_*`, validation plan and readout metadata. | A Primer3 Tm is not a substitute for the kit's isothermal temperature or a time-to-signal measurement. |
| Product-size lower and upper bounds | readout- and kinetics-dependent starting rule | The current formulation is described as favouring relatively short targets within `80–500 bp`; `100–200 bp` is preferred for rapid assays, while products above about `500 bp` generally amplify poorly and products below about `70–80 bp` are constrained by the primer lengths ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Profile keeps `100–200 bp` as the high-sensitivity starting window. | The wider manual envelope is not a universal acceptance range; an override requires the intended readout and empirical sensitivity/time validation. |
| Primer-screen design | staged experimental selection | The official manual proposes a primary screen of staggered forward/reverse candidates, then secondary and tertiary one-base/length variants; a stringent primary screen may use `8–10` candidates per direction, but it is not necessary to test every combination in every application ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | The engine returns a computational shortlist and a staged screening handoff. | Candidate count, selection rule, sensitivity, product/noise ratio and time-to-signal must be recorded for any claim of assay selection. |
| Primer noise and competition | explicit failure mechanism | Hairpins and primer dimers can become amplifiable low-molecular-weight artefacts, consume primers/nucleotides/polymerase/energy and compete with the target; therefore primer-noise risk is linked to sensitivity and not merely to cosmetic gel background ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Keep structure and interaction checks visible and require readout-specific screening. | The shared thermodynamic proxy cannot predict all RPA artefact kinetics or prove a clean NTC. |
| Oligo quality and lot verification | pre-analytical control | TwistDx notes supplier/batch variation in primer quality and recommends purified oligonucleotides for consistency-critical assays, with stock concentrations independently verified in repeat orders ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Add oligo purification, lot and concentration fields to the validation handoff. | Sequence-only design cannot verify synthesis quality, concentration or lot-to-lot performance. |
| Degenerate primers and mismatches | open, assay-specific | The manual gives no fixed limit for degenerate bases and advises minimal use; it also states that RPA can tolerate mismatches in some documented assays. Degeneracy and mismatch count must therefore remain explicit inputs with coverage and empirical screening rather than a universal hard cutoff ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Preserve degenerate coverage/provenance and report mismatch positions. | Tolerance depends on sequence, target, kit, readout and assay objective; a Primer3 Tm spread is not an RPA tolerance measurement. |
| RT-RPA | one-step chemistry and timing contract | The official manual permits RNA targets by adding a suitable RT active at `37–42 °C`, recommends approximately `40 °C`, delaying the first agitation to give RT time, considering RNase inhibitor and titrating RT amount; this is a one-step RT-RPA workflow, not a DNA-RPA default ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | RT format, RT enzyme, temperature, agitation delay and RNA controls must be recorded explicitly. | The plain DNA request cannot certify RNA integrity, RT efficiency or absence of genomic carryover. |
| Reaction chemistry | hard assay compatibility gate | Require a complete recombinase-compatible preset; generic strand displacement (for example the LAMP Bst mix) is not an RPA substitute. The RPA polymerase is not marked as Taq-like 5′→3′ exonuclease-positive; Exo/Nfo detection supplies a separate nuclease and probe contract ([Piepenburg et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16756388/), [RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/)). | `RpaCompatible` / `rpa_compatible`, `five_prime_exonuclease`, preset filtering and default-enzyme regression. | The catalogue describes supported reaction families, not validation of every commercial kit lot. Probe/Nfo formats still need their own input contract. |
| Controls and read-out | post-design required | NTC, non-targets, hold-time/temperature and detection chemistry must be validated experimentally ([TwistAmp manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | `_validation_plan("rpa")`, result UI. | The request does not model every kit/probe variant. |
| Recombinase/SSB/polymerase branch | chemistry identity gate | The foundational RPA branch requires a recombinase-loaded primer, mediator/SSB support and a strand-displacing polymerase; ATP hydrolysis drives the dynamic loading/disassembly environment. T4 UvsX/UvsY/gp32/Bsu is a historical component example, not a synonym for every commercial pellet ([Piepenburg et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204)). | `rpa-compatible` and named kit/protocol metadata. | A component list does not validate activity, lot equivalence, inhibitor tolerance or a readout. |
| Historical RPA component recipe | evidence exemplar, not default | The original reconstruction used `37 °C/120 min`, `300 nM` each primer, `10 mM` MgOAc, `200 µM` dNTPs, `3 mM` ATP, `20 mM` phosphocreatine, `100 ng/µl` creatine kinase, `600 ng/µl` gp32, `200 ng/µl` UvsX, `60 ng/µl` UvsY and `30 ng/µl` Bsu ([Piepenburg et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204)). | Numeric register only; never auto-fill the TwistAmp preset from this recipe. | Historical purified-component conditions are not interchangeable with TwistAmp Basic/Exo/Nfo/Fpg or SIBA. |
| SIBA branch | separate chemistry contract | SIBA requires two short amplification primers plus a recombinase-loaded IO; the IO carries a target-homologous region and `2′-O-methyl RNA` extension-control segment. An IO is not representable by the current two-flanking-primer schema ([SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/)). | Reject `siba` unless an IO sequence, modification map, orientation and readout are present. | SIBA primer/IO geometry and kinetics are not RPA primer geometry; no cross-branch reuse is implied. |
| SIBA temperature and MgOAc | named-assay optimization | In one *S. pyogenes* SIBA assay, detection occurred at `37–48 °C`, was fastest at `42–45 °C`, and MgOAc was fastest at `10–15 mM` but inhibitory above `20 mM`; the selected condition was `43 °C` and `12.5 mM` MgOAc ([S. pyogenes SIBA study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7099210/)). | Store temperature, MgOAc and assay identity in the SIBA validation record. | These are observations for one assay and kit/component system, not a universal SIBA preset. |
| Amplicon-size limit | branch-specific validation gate | Original component-level RPA failed at the tested `1,500 bp` or larger products and produced hairpin-mediated `2×/3×` products from a `300 bp` target under its `37 °C/120 min` recipe; manufacturer guidance separately prefers `100–200 bp` and generally below `500 bp` ([Piepenburg et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Keep `100–200 bp` as the profile start; require explicit override evidence for longer products. | The historical failure point is not a universal hard boundary and does not define SIBA's short-amplicon limit. |
| Inhibitors and background DNA | matrix-specific validation gate | Preserve the reported tolerance context (`50 g/L` hemoglobin, `4% v/v` ethanol, `0.5 U` heparin, serum) separately from the documented whole-blood/background-DNA inhibition; background-DNA tolerance varied by assay and was not rescued reliably by more time or primer ([Rohrman and Richards-Kortum](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ac504365v)). | Add matrix, dilution, background-DNA mass, target load and internal amplification control to validation. | “RPA is inhibitor tolerant” must not be used to release an unvalidated crude-matrix assay. |
| Urine and detergent interference | conflicting matrix evidence; no universal limit | A peer-reviewed synthesis reports no effect at `1.25%` urine, slight effects up to `5%`, inhibition at `10%` urine near `100 fg` target but not at `10 pg`, and total inhibition with `0.05% v/v` SDS; preserve target-load and assay context for each observation ([RPA comprehensive review](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2019/an/c8an01621f?page=search)). | Require an actual-matrix dilution series and process/internal control near the intended detection limit. | These values are review-summarized and conflict with reports of direct urine tolerance; they are not kit acceptance thresholds. |
| Sample lysis and buffer compatibility | protocol-dependent input control | TwistAmp lists `1%` Triton X-100, heat, or dilution with `0.2 M` NaOH/KOH as example lysis approaches, and warns that EDTA/EGTA, strong detergents, high salt and extreme acid/base can affect RPA ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Record lysis chemistry, dilution factor, residual buffer and matrix in the run handoff. | These examples are not safe carryover limits; near-LoD reactions are especially vulnerable to partial inhibition. |
| Carryover and aerosol contamination | mandatory workflow control | Use unidirectional clean-to-dirty workflow, separate pre-/post-amplification handling, NTCs in each run/unit and closed-tube detection where possible; opening an RPA product for lateral flow or a second reaction increases the contamination pathway ([CDC molecular-genetic testing good laboratory practices](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [WHO molecular-testing dos and don'ts](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/case-management/diagnosis/nucleic-acid-amplification-based-diagnostics/dos-and-don-ts-for-molecular-testing)). | Add `workflow_mode`, `ntc_position/count`, `tube_opened_after_amplification` and contamination-investigation fields. | Probe specificity does not neutralize aerosolized amplicon carryover; a clean NTC is run evidence, not a permanent guarantee. |
| Termination and post-amplification handling | protocol/readout safety control | TwistAmp lists heating at or above `85 °C`, MgOAc chelation with EDTA, or at least `1/4` dilution as termination options; dilution is not recommended for Exo because residual nucleases can degrade amplicon over time ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). | Record termination method and whether the tube is opened or diluted before readout. | Termination changes product integrity and does not substitute for contamination control or branch validation. |
| Nfo lateral-flow artifact control | readout-specific acceptance gate | LFS cannot size-discriminate signal molecules; labelled primer–dimers and primer–probe complexes can produce false positives. A probe, 3′ block/THF architecture and deliberate interaction screening are required; in one assay, `3` probe/reverse-primer mismatches removed the false signal ([Salmonella RPA-LFS artifact study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7324538/)). | Require NTC strips, non-targets, test-line and control-line status, and documented probe–primer interaction review. | The `3-mismatch` result is not a universal design cutoff and cannot be applied without preserving target-coverage effects. |
| Internal amplification/process control | required for matrix and run validity | A published quantitative RPA protocol describes an internal positive control and its co-amplification with the target; because multiplexed RPA can involve primer/recombinase competition, the IPC must be optimized and shown not to suppress target detection ([Development of qRPA with an internal positive control](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25867513/), [RPA review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7112910/)). | Require IPC identity, channel/readout, expected result and competition assessment in the validation handoff. | A positive control alone does not detect extraction failure or matrix inhibition in the sample unless the control is placed appropriately. |
| Exo/Fpg fluorescence branch | nuclease/probe/readout contract | Exo fluorescence depends on duplex formation and nuclease cleavage of a THF-containing, fluorophore/quencher-labelled, 3′-blocked probe; a primary comparison found Fpg assays had `3–6 log10` lower sensitivity than matched Exo assays for three viral targets ([Development of a panel of RPA assays](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3666764/)). | Keep `readout`, nuclease branch, probe chemistry and fluorophore/quencher as required metadata. | The sensitivity difference is target- and assay-specific; it is not a universal Exo-over-Fpg multiplier. |
| Detection capability and validation | standards-aligned evidence gate | For quantitative or near-zero detection claims, document LoB/LoD/LoQ, precision, linearity, specificity, robustness, controls and intended-use conditions using the applicable validation framework; CLSI EP17 addresses detection capability, ISO 20395 addresses nucleic-acid quantification performance, and ISO 17822 provides laboratory quality practice for NAAT pathogen testing ([CLSI EP17](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/ep17/), [ISO 20395](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html), [ISO 17822](https://committee.iso.org/standard/67022.html?browse=tc)). | Extend the RPA validation handoff with LoB/LoD/LoQ definitions, matrix panel, precision, inclusivity/exclusivity and robustness plan. | These standards are general/NAAT or qPCR/dPCR frameworks; they do not supply RPA primer, probe, temperature or concentration defaults. |

**Runtime oligo/readout contract.** Every executable GEN1 `flanking-pair` RPA branch now reports `oligo_contract=plain-acgt-two-primer`, `modified_probe_support=false` and a readout contract stating that detection modality is not inferred. The record is `limited`: RPA is no longer presented as ordinary PCR with a fabricated annealing temperature, and its Basic/Exo/Nfo/Fpg, core-component and SIBA boundaries are explicit ([Piepenburg et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204), [SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)). The shared engine can return a primer-pair design and a chemistry-specific validation handoff; it does not yet design modified Exo/Nfo probes, SIBA invasion oligos or claim a probe/readout result from plain ACGT text. That limitation remains until typed modified-oligo/IO contracts, branch-specific controls and chemistry-specific regression data exist ([RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/), [Salmonella RPA-LFS artifact study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7324538/)).

## Extended RPA design rules

The official TwistAmp design guidance treats primer screening as an empirical comparison, not a one-shot sequence calculation. For Basic assays it evaluates product yield and product/noise by gel; for Exo and Nfo it evaluates readout-specific signal, sensitivity, background and amplification time. The manual also gives a sequential screening strategy that can reduce the number of reactions without claiming that every possible pair was tested ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

RPA remains `limited` until a typed readout/probe model and kit-specific empirical calibration exist. The correct output is a chemistry-labelled shortlist plus a documented screening and validation plan, not a PCR-style annealing guarantee ([Piepenburg et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16756388/), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

The RPA temperature is a kinetic control, not a decorative protocol field. The official manual explains that temperatures below the recommended range can slow amplification enough for the reaction's energy supply to be consumed before detectable product accumulates, and that the effect differs by kit/readout; a returned pair must therefore carry the intended kit, readout and temperature together ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

The official screening workflow is deliberately staged: a computational candidate set is followed by primary, secondary and, when sensitivity demands it, tertiary wet-lab screens. The number of combinations actually tested, the intended readout and the measured sensitivity, product/noise ratio and time-to-signal must be preserved, because “candidate generated” and “assay selected” are different evidence states ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

A primary mismatch study tested `315` combinations in the RPA 3′-anchor region, defined from the pre-antepenultimate through the terminal position. In that assay system, terminal C:C and G:A mismatches had reported reaction-success probabilities of approximately `0.59` and `0.62`, respectively, and adjacent terminal/penultimate mismatch combinations were among the most damaging ([Higgins et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoldx.2022.08.005)). These figures are preserved as a mismatch-evidence exemplar: they justify exposing mismatch identity, position and multiplicity, but they do not provide a universal RPA cutoff because the result depends on primer sequence, target, kit, readout and reaction conditions ([Higgins et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoldx.2022.08.005)).

When `rpa_protocol=twistamp-basic` is selected for the `rpa` profile, the runtime records the current **TwistAmp Basic DNA** branch (TABAS03KIT): the current Basic Quick Guide gives a `39 °C` hold for `20 min`, with an agitation step after `4 min` for low-copy input, `480 nM` primer per direction from `2.4 µL` of each `10 µM` primer in a `50 µL` reaction, and `14 mM` final magnesium acetate from `2.5 µL` of `280 mM` MgOAc. Primer-length and amplicon-size guidance remain design-manual context, not a PCR-Tm rule. The withdrawn Basic-RT product identity is **not** silently merged into this executable branch; current TwistDx guidance handles RNA by adding a separately selected RT to a current TwistAmp chemistry ([TwistAmp Basic Quick Guide](https://www.twistdx.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/qg_basicrt_online_revb.pdf), [TwistAmp FAQ](https://www.twistdx.co.uk/support/faqs/), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).

## Deep-search enrichment — official RPA exception branches

TwistDx's current FAQ and assay-design documentation make two important exceptions explicit. First, PCR-length primers of approximately `18–23 nt` can work in RPA in many cases, although kinetics may be slower; TwistDx still recommends approximately `30–35 nt` primers to maximize the probability of rapid amplification. There is no formally established correlation between the sequence features that make an oligo a good PCR primer and those that make it a good RPA primer. This is an official exception branch and must not replace the longer-primer default ([TwistDx PCR-primer FAQ](https://www.twistdx.co.uk/faq/will-pcr-primers-work-in-rpa/), [TwistDx PCR-primer page](https://www.twistdx.co.uk/rpa/using-pcr-primers/)).

Second, the current Abbott/TwistDx assay-design manual states that current TwistAmp kits are designed for products in the `80–500 bp` range, that products above approximately `500 bp` generally do not amplify well with the current formulations, that `100–200 bp` is preferred for rapid assays, and that the practical lower size is about `70–80 bp` because of primer length. The same current manual also states that RPA as a chemistry can amplify products up to at least `1.5 kb` depending on conditions, while noting that the current TwistAmp kits are not optimized for such long products. This capability statement is condition-dependent and must not be converted into a current-kit default. It is also preserved alongside the historical experiment record below, which reports failure at the tested `1,500 bp` or larger products under the original purified-component conditions; the apparent tension is therefore retained as source- and condition-specific evidence rather than silently reconciled ([current Abbott/TwistDx assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf), [Piepenburg et al.](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204)).

## Verification record

The current verification record covers isothermal semantics, nullable temperatures, RPA quality scoring, the validation plan (including candidate screening and reaction optimization), the separate TwistAmp Basic and Liquid Basic handoffs, the Thermo Fisher Lyo-ready RPA/RT-RPA branch, published primer-envelope examples, variant masking, SIBA branch separation, inhibitor/matrix fields, contamination workflow and readout-specific controls. The Thermo branch is not an alias for TwistAmp: it owns its documented `30–35 nt`, `30–70%` GC and `150–450 bp` design envelope and, only when RNA input is explicit, its named SuperScript IV/RNaseOUT/RNase H one-pot RT-RPA step. TwistAmp Basic/Liquid remain DNA-only in this runtime. The report never presents PCR annealing advice as an RPA protocol. A chemistry-specific Exo/Nfo/Fpg probe or SIBA-IO generator remains deliberately out of scope for this plain-oligo engine; any future implementation must first add typed modification/label/IO contracts, orientation checks, THF or `2′-O-methyl` geometry checks, probe secondary-structure and primer–probe/IO interaction checks, branch-matched positive/negative controls and readout-specific tests ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf), [Thermo Fisher Lyo-ready RPA guide](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN1000697-LyoReadyRPA-UG.pdf), [SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

- The reviewed current manufacturer documents, including TwistAmp and Thermo Fisher Lyo-ready RPA, provide useful design envelopes and protocol-specific reaction conditions but no validated universal sequence-only algorithm that predicts recombinase loading, time-to-signal, product/noise ratio or specificity across RPA formulations; empirical candidate screening therefore remains mandatory ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf), [Thermo Lyo-ready RPA guide](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN1000697-LyoReadyRPA-UG.pdf), [RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/)).
- No defensible universal amplicon ceiling, primer concentration, probe concentration, MgOAc optimum, temperature, hold time or inhibitor threshold can be assigned across TwistAmp Basic/Exo/Nfo/Fpg, SIBA, other commercial RPA formulations, target sequences, matrices and readouts; the conflicting values remain named overlays or study-specific exemplars ([Original RPA study](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.0040204), [SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/), [Rohrman and Richards-Kortum](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ac504365v), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).
- A validated generic contamination-control chemistry for RPA is not established by the sources reviewed. dUTP/UNG is a documented PCR carryover strategy, but RPA's isothermal workflow, kit-specific composition and the need to preserve branch/readout performance mean it must not be exposed as a universal RPA switch without compatibility data; workflow separation, NTCs and sealed handling remain the supported controls ([CDC good laboratory practices](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [WHO molecular-testing guidance](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/case-management/diagnosis/nucleic-acid-amplification-based-diagnostics/dos-and-don-ts), [UDG carryover study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/037811199090145H), [TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf)).
- The current plain-ACGT contract cannot encode modified bases, THF/dSpacer placement, labels, quenchers, 3′ blocks, SIBA IO chemistry or post-amplification readout state; this is an implementation boundary, not a scientific claim, and remains the main reason the module status is `limited` ([RPA diagnostic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108464/), [SIBA original study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4242538/)). For the executable Thermo one-pot RT-RPA branch, an RNA-derived request also promotes no-RT and RNA-input/RT provenance to required validation evidence; the named recipe does not establish RNA integrity, RT yield or absence of DNA carryover.

### R12 empirical screening cohort and additional chemistry

The runtime now emits a deterministic, coordinate-diversity-aware short-list of up to four already-ranked acceptable RPA pairs for empirical screening. This **does not reorder the primary design ranking** and does not predict wet-lab sensitivity; it operationalizes the manufacturer design guidance that several strong candidate pairs should be characterized experimentally before one assay is selected. The G-Biosciences RPA kit is also a separate source-conditioned executable branch rather than inheriting TwistAmp/Thermo composition, and the Thermo Lyo-ready branch supports its reviewed 20-to-50-µL scaling context. Modified Exo/Nfo/Fpg oligos and SIBA remain recognized but non-executable until their distinct modified-oligo/third-oligo architectures are representable ([TwistAmp assay-design manual](https://www.globalpointofcare.abbott/content/dam/ardx/globalpointofcare/lp/2025/twistdx/support/docs/manuals/INASDM%20v1.0%20TwistAmp%20DNA%20Amplification%20Kits%20-%20Assay%20Design%20Manual%20INASDM.pdf), [Thermo Lyo-ready RPA guide](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN1000697-LyoReadyRPA-UG.pdf), [G-Biosciences RPA protocol](https://cdn.gbiosciences.com/pdfs/protocol/786-2155_v2.pdf)).
