## Evidence collected

### R5 dye-qPCR registry clarification (2026-09-03)

The historical module identifier `qpcr-sybr` is retained for API compatibility, but the runtime/UI now uses the broader term **dye-qPCR** because an executable Promega branch uses BRYT Green rather than SYBR Green I. The R15 snapshot contains **13 named dye-qPCR/one-step RT-qPCR branches**: Bio-Rad iTaq Universal SYBR Green, NEB Luna M3003, NEB Luna One-Step RT-qPCR E3005, Thermo PowerUp SYBR Green, **Thermo PowerTrack SYBR Green**, Promega GoTaq qPCR A6001/A6002, Promega GoTaq 1-Step RT-qPCR A6020, QIAGEN QuantiNova SYBR, Bio-Rad SsoAdvanced Universal SYBR, three instrument-conditioned Solis HOT FIREPol EvaGreen formulations (ROX, no-ROX and capillary), and **Vazyme SupRealQ Ultra Hunter SYBR Q713**. The A6020 branch is RNA-only and preserves its own 50–300 nM primer range, >=37 °C/15 min RT step, 95 °C/10 min activation and 40-cycle qPCR programme; qPCR-only GoTaq A6001/A6002 never silently inherits that RT recipe ([Promega GoTaq qPCR systems](https://www.promega.com/products/pcr/qpcr-and-rt-qpcr/qpcr-kits/), [A6020 TM355](https://www.promega.com/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/gotaq-onestep-rt-qpcr-system-protocol/)).

### PowerTrack source-conditioned runtime closure (2026-09-04)

`thermo-powertrack-sybr-a46xxx` is now an executable numeric branch from MAN0018825 Rev B.0. The runtime keeps `2X -> 1X` master mix, a `400 nM` primer starting point with reviewed `300–800 nM` range, explicit fast (`95 °C 5 s / 60 °C 30 s`) versus standard (`95 °C 15 s / 60 °C 60 s`) cycling, and the reviewed `10–20%` DNA-template fraction. The guide's **40X Yellow Sample Buffer is optional**: PCRStudio therefore does not include it in the product baseline; when explicitly selected it resolves to `1X` and derives `0.5 µL` in a `20 µL` reaction or `0.25 µL` in a `10 µL` reaction. This optionality is guarded by the cross-layer numeric audit so it cannot silently become mandatory. Source: [PowerTrack user guide MAN0018825](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0018825_PowerTrackSYBRGreenMasterMix_UG.pdf).

With an intercalating dye, every double-stranded product contributes signal; primer-dimers and non-specific amplicons are assay failures, not merely ugly gel bands ([qPCR primer-design review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29201647/)). Short amplicons, matched primer Tm and empirical melt-curve review are central. MIQE requires disclosure of target, primer sequences, controls, efficiency evidence, the calibration/standard-curve record when that method is used, and enough detail to reproduce the result ([MIQE](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19246619/), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). Design software can predict structure and specificity, but cannot establish efficiency or a unique melt peak without a run.

The 2025 [MIQE 2.0 revision](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/) makes the boundary more explicit: a reproducible qPCR record needs the raw fluorescence/analysis context, the method used to determine Cq and baseline, an explicit efficiency/quantity method, uncertainty and the validated quantitative range. LOD and LLOQ are separate empirical limits and become required when low-copy detection/quantification is relevant; MIQE 2.0 explicitly notes that they are not universal requirements when low target levels are not an issue. None of these is a primer-sequence property, so PCRStudio records them as measured or conditional follow-up rather than inventing defaults.

The dye is not target-specific: SYBR Green I fluorescence rises when it binds newly formed dsDNA, so off-target products and primer-dimers can contribute to the same signal. SYBR Green I and EvaGreen are both dsDNA-binding dyes but differ in binding properties; high dye concentration can interfere with amplification and promote delayed Cq or nonspecific products. Therefore a named dye/master-mix branch is required when transferring a protocol ([PowerUp SYBR User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf), [EvaGreen/SYBR characterization](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17996102/)).

Master-mix choice is itself an experimental factor: a comparative study of `70` assays found that `66/70` (`94.3%`) gave a single melt peak only under the manufacturer's recommended master-mix/protocol combination. This is study-scoped evidence for carrying vendor, mix, instrument and protocol together, not a probability that can be applied to a new assay ([comparative commercial-master-mix study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27259280/)).

Melt analysis is a specificity screen, not a sequence-derived identity test. The derivative peak reflects the temperature-dependent loss of dye-associated dsDNA fluorescence; product Tm depends on sequence, length, GC/AT composition, dye, salt, instrument and ramp. A single peak supports a homogeneous product but does not exclude two products with coincident Tm, and a low-Tm peak is not by itself proof of a primer-dimer. Orthogonal confirmation by expected-size gel and, when the assay is consequential or ambiguous, sequencing remains a validation option ([Ririe, Rasmussen & Wittwer 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/abio.1996.9916), [Ruiz-Villalba et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727009/), [Thermo specificity white paper](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/GSD/Reference-Materials/qpcr-gene-expression-specificity-white-paper.pdf)).

Inhibition is a sample- and assay-level property. A spike-in internal positive control or a dilution comparison can reveal delayed Cq or altered efficiency, but dilution also dilutes inhibitors and can make a standard-curve efficiency appear artificially high. Published work therefore recommends combining Cq deviation and efficiency evidence and retesting each sample type rather than adopting a universal ΔCq cutoff ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [King et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20041847/), [Nolan et al. SPUD assay](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16524557/)).

Reference genes are not universal housekeeping constants. Their stability must be evaluated in the actual tissue, treatment, developmental state, extraction/RT workflow and assay conditions; the original geNorm work supports a geometric mean of multiple validated controls, while NormFinder explicitly models within- and between-group variation. Efficiency correction matters because reference-gene rankings can change when raw Cq values are treated as if all assays were 100% efficient ([Vandesompele et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12184808/), [Andersen et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15289330/), [reference-gene software comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25825906/)).

## Current implementation assessment

The short-product, matched-Tm profile and stronger dimer constraints are aligned with the chemistry and with the generic primer-design window reiterated by [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), which gives a shared starting envelope of 18–24-nt primers, 40–60% GC, nearest-neighbour Tm values close to one another (ΔTm <3 °C), avoidance of stable secondary structures and primer interactions, and a typical amplicon range of 50–200 bp. The profile uses the chemistry-specific subset 59–65 °C, ≤3 °C pair difference and 80–200 bp. For an intercalating dye, the default floor is 80 bp: the [qPCR primer-design review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29201647/) specifically warns that products below roughly 80 bp can be difficult to distinguish from primer-dimers in a melt curve. This is a conservative starting envelope, not a universal impossibility line; an explicit validated override remains meaningful. A clean in-silico pair is still not described as experimentally specific or efficient: the result now carries an explicit `in-silico-only` validation plan with melt-curve, efficiency and no-template-control follow-up. Those are kept as requirements rather than fabricated measurements.

The runtime now exposes six explicit dye-qPCR / one-step RT-qPCR protocol identities rather than treating one master mix as universal bench authority: `bio-rad-itaq-sybr`, `neb-luna-universal-m3003`, `neb-luna-one-step-rt-qpcr-e3005`, `thermo-powerup-sybr-a2574x`, `promega-gotaq-qpcr-a600x`, and `promega-gotaq-one-step-rt-qpcr-a6020`. Each overlay carries only manufacturer-specific reaction/protocol facts that are actually documented; no proprietary ionic composition is inferred and the shared Primer3 thermodynamic model is not silently replaced. E3005 and A6020 are separate one-step RT-qPCR authorities and are executable only when the request explicitly declares RNA input (`from_rna=true`). DNA/cDNA-only qPCR uses a qPCR-only chemistry and never borrows an RT step from either one-step branch ([NEB Luna E3005](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/luna-universal-one-step-rt-qpcr-kit-protocol-e3005), [Promega GoTaq qPCR systems](https://www.promega.com/products/pcr/qpcr-and-rt-qpcr/qpcr-kits/), [Promega A6020 TM355](https://www.promega.com/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/gotaq-onestep-rt-qpcr-system-protocol/)).

The selected overlay is recorded in the result and portable report, while explicit user constraints remain authoritative and the validation plan remains visible. This separation prevents a vendor starting point from becoming a false claim of efficiency, a single melt peak, or compatibility with an unrecorded optical platform; those outcomes still require the run-level evidence described by MIQE 2.0. When the request is RNA-derived, PCRStudio promotes the no-RT control and RNA/RT provenance from conditional reminders to required validation evidence; for DNA-only qPCR they remain non-applicable/conditional rather than being fabricated ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

The executable E3005 record is pinned to NEB manual revision `5.0` (April 2025) for provenance. Its ordinary extension/read step is `60 °C / 30 s`, but the current NEB protocol explicitly calls for `60 s` on Applied Biosystems real-time instruments; PCRStudio therefore records the instrument-specific override rather than presenting `30 s` as universal. This timing difference belongs to the named bench protocol and does not alter primer ranking ([NEB E3005 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en/protocols/luna-universal-one-step-rt-qpcr-kit-protocol-e3005)).

For the E3005 one-step branch, the runtime also keeps genomic-DNA controls separate from primer geometry. NEB recommends a no-RT control for RNA-derived measurements (especially when a pair does not span an exon-exon junction), NTCs for meaningful positive interpretation, and DNase treatment or exon-junction redesign if no-RT amplification reveals genomic DNA. The optional Antarctic Thermolabile UDG carry-over branch follows the E3005-specific manual/product authority: set up at room temperature or use an added `25 °C / 2 min` pretreatment; UDG is not built into E3005 and selecting the kit does not by itself activate carry-over prevention ([NEB no-RT FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en/faqs/should-i-include-a-no-rt-control-reaction), [Luna optimization guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-de/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/optimization-tips-for-luna-one-step-rt-qpcr), [Luna troubleshooting](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/troubleshooting-guides/luna-one-step-rt-qpcr-troubleshooting-guide)). Exon-junction placement remains recommended when annotated and appropriate, not a universal hard requirement for every transcript. A broader Luna optimization page has also described a longer room-temperature UDG exposure; PCRStudio deliberately binds the executable E3005 record to the current E3005-specific manual/product instructions instead of merging generic guidance into a new hybrid protocol.

| GC, 3′ end and homopolymer | hard search filters | Retain moderate GC, no mandatory clamp, and homopolymer ceiling; dye signal makes nonspecific products costly ([qPCR primer-design review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29201647/)). | Profile and Primer3 adapter. | No sequence rule guarantees one melt peak. |
| Dimer / nonspecific product | computational gate plus specificity review | Keep structure checks and background scan; never represent a negative scan as a clean melt curve ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [SYBR assay study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4893258/)). | `thermo.py`, `specificity.py`, validation result. | Melt temperature and fluorescence require a run. |
| RT junction | optional hard geometry filter | When cDNA is selected, a supplied transcript boundary can be required to fall inside at least one primer in every pair. This makes the genomic-carryover safeguard explicit rather than inferring exon structure from `U`/`T` ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [ExonSurfer 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38867172/)). | `exon_junctions`, `transcript-junction` stage, request audit, API tests. | Coordinates must come from transcript annotation; this does not replace no-RT controls or genomic alignment. |
| MIQE controls and efficiency | post-design measured/conditional | NTC, melt curve, raw fluorescence/analysis provenance, explicit efficiency/quantity method, uncertainty and validated quantitative range belong in validation; a dilution-series standard curve is one accepted method rather than a universal requirement, and LOD/LLOQ are conditional on the application ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | `_validation_plan("qpcr-sybr")`, UI schema. | The engine does not run a plate, measure Cq or establish analytical limits. |
| Template secondary structure | sequence-context gate | Analyze the amplicon and at least `60 bp` flanking each primer-binding site for stable template structures; primer-only dimer checks are insufficient because structures near or inside the amplicon can suppress qPCR ([template-secondary-structure study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30101656/)). | Carry target context and structure-review status into preflight. | The `60 bp` recommendation is from a controlled study, not a universal thermodynamic cutoff. |
| Database specificity | in-silico specificity gate | Use a target accession/RefSeq transcript where possible, restrict organism and choose the smallest suitable database; Primer-BLAST checks forward/reverse, forward/forward and reverse/reverse pair products, but database redundancy, polymorphism and incomplete annotation can require user review ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/index.cgi?GROUP_TARGET=on), [NCBI primer guide](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/guide/howto/design-pcr-primers/)). | Record accession, database, organism/taxonomy, Entrez query and specificity-search date. | A negative database search is not experimental specificity and cannot detect unrepresented alleles or contaminants. |
| Melt-curve interpretation | run-level specificity gate | Record raw/derivative curve, peak temperatures, shoulders, NTC curve and instrument ramp. A single peak is supportive; multiple peaks/shoulders or sample/NTC disagreement require investigation, and coincident-Tm products can be missed ([Ririe et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/abio.1996.9916), [Ruiz-Villalba et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727009/), [PowerUp manual](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf)). | `_validation_plan("qpcr-sybr")`; melt artifact review. | No universal amplicon Tm or peak-separation threshold is defensible across dyes, mixes and instruments. |
| Inhibition | sample-level control and failure mode | Include a matrix-appropriate endogenous or exogenous internal control, or compare controlled sample dilutions; evaluate both Cq shift and efficiency because inhibitors can affect the two measures differently ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [King et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20041847/), [PCR inhibitors review](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2672.2012.05384.x)). | Record matrix, spike timing, control identity, expected Cq/efficiency and dilution factor. | A reference gene alone cannot distinguish inhibition from poor extraction, RNA degradation, low input or biological down-regulation. |
| Reference-gene validation | RT-qPCR normalization gate | Test candidate genes across all study groups; do not select by popularity. Use efficiency-aware quantities and a documented stability method; multiple validated controls may be combined by geometric mean ([Vandesompele et al. 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12184808/), [Andersen et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15289330/), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | Preserve candidate list, sample groups, efficiency method, stability outputs and chosen normalizer count. | Stability is study-specific; no primer design rule can certify a biologically invariant reference. |
| Efficiency analysis | quantitative validation gate | Use a dilution-series curve or an accepted amplification-curve method; retain slope, intercept, replicate structure, confidence interval, dynamic range and LLOQ/ULOQ. Do not infer efficiency from primer Tm, amplicon size or Cq alone ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [Ruijter et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19237396/)). | Downstream run record, not primer-pair score. | Baseline choice, inhibition, standard preparation and analysis model can bias the estimate. |
| Controls | contamination, extraction and assay-validity gates | Distinguish NTC, positive amplification control, extraction control, no-RT control, endogenous control and exogenous inhibition control; a control must be placed at the process stage whose failure it is meant to detect ([CDC molecular-testing good practices](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm), [AAVLD inhibition-monitoring review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31735123/)). | Require control type, spike stage, expected result, acceptance rule and invalidation action. | Control design depends on the sample matrix, RT format, target type and intended use. |

## Deep-search enrichment — literature-mined and ML primer assessment

**Primer PICKR (2026)** adds an empirical literature-evidence layer that is distinct from de-novo thermodynamic design. The Nature Communications resource mined more than `7 million` oligonucleotides from more than `400,000` papers, mapped `290,191` unique recurrent primers to `6,647` genes across `10` species, and experimentally evaluated `154` human primer pairs. In that study, pairs with a PICKR score `>80` achieved approximately `99%` amplification success. The paper also estimates that incomplete/paywalled supplementary extraction may have missed roughly `3–4 million` oligos, which is a direct evidence boundary for any claim of exhaustive literature-primer coverage. PCRStudio should use PICKR only as an external empirical-prior/citation layer; score thresholds must not become universal qPCR acceptance rules ([Primer PICKR](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73648-2)).

**PrimerAST (2026)** is a multivariate ML primer-quality precedent. The main article describes a dataset of `315` primer pairs and `16` engineered features including GC content, Tm, pair ΔTm, homopolymers, 3′-GC content, dimerization energy, hairpin stability and SNP-related metrics. The authors explicitly note the modest dataset and synthetic predicted-to-fail examples as limitations. PCRStudio may record an ML-assessment handoff but must not label model output as wet-lab validation or a calibrated universal probability of success ([PrimerAST](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-38238-8)).

## Extended qPCR/SYBR evidence rules

The primer design record must hand off to a run-level quantitative record. MIQE 2.0 requires transparent experimental details, raw-data availability where possible, efficiency-corrected quantities with prediction intervals, detection limits and dynamic ranges, together with normalization and quality-control information; ISO 20395:2019 covers assay design, in-silico and in-vitro specificity, data quality, validation and uncertainty for both qPCR and dPCR ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).

| Additional rule | Resolution for SYBR qPCR | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dye-specific specificity | Because an intercalating dye reports double-stranded DNA rather than a target-specific probe, primer-dimers and nonspecific products can contribute signal; require melt-curve review and appropriate controls ([Thermo SYBR guidance](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0013361_DyNAmo_SYBR_Green_qPCR_UG.pdf), [qPCR primer-design review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29201647/)). | In-silico structure scores cannot establish a single melt peak. |
| Cq and efficiency | Store the method used to determine baseline/threshold and convert Cq to efficiency-aware quantities where the analysis requires it; do not present a raw Cq as a universal concentration ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)). | The engine does not read fluorescence curves or estimate efficiency from a run. |
| Standard-curve design | For a dilution-series calibration, MIQE 2.0 describes at least `3` technical replicates per dilution, ideally covering `4–5` orders of magnitude, restricting reported quantities to the validated linear range between LLOQ and ULOQ, and reporting confidence intervals for the efficiency estimate. Add dilution levels, replicate count, standard identity, regression method, slope/intercept, confidence interval and validated range to the downstream qPCR record; these are run-design/reporting requirements and remain absent from the primer-only result until measured ([MIQE 2.0 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | The engine cannot construct or validate a standard, pipetting series, linear range or confidence interval. |
| Efficiency calculation | Treat efficiency as a standard-curve analysis field. For a Cq-versus-log10-concentration standard curve, MIQE 2.0 gives `%E = 100 × (−1 + 10^(−1/slope))`; retain the slope, intercept, confidence interval and dilution/calibration identity, and store the calculation method plus measured efficiency in the run-level record rather than the primer-design result ([MIQE 2.0 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | Pipetting error, standard integrity, matrix and the chosen linear range affect the estimate; the formula is not evidence that a primer pair has any particular efficiency. |
| Reagent and batch changes | MIQE 2.0 notes that results can change across manufacturers and recommends repeating optimization/validation when kits or suppliers change and revalidating when reagent, primer or probe batches change. Preserve vendor, product, lot/batch, document revision and revalidation status as first-class provenance; never inherit a validated label across an unverified chemistry or batch change ([MIQE 2.0 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | The primer engine can require these fields in the handoff but cannot perform the revalidation experiment. |
| Dynamic range and detection limits | Treat dynamic range, LOD and LOQ as measured assay properties with stated acceptance criteria, not as consequences of an 80–200 bp amplicon window ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)). | Matrix, instrument, extraction and target abundance affect the result. |
| LOD/LLOQ copy-number boundary | Treat this as a measured assay boundary rather than a Cq cutoff. MIQE 2.0 states that Poisson sampling prevents a qPCR LOD below `3` target copies, that variability increases below approximately `10` copies per reaction for a good assay, and that LOD/LLOQ should not be used as negative-result cutoffs for a qualitative assay. Record copy input, replicate outcomes, detection probability, precision/accuracy criteria and assay-specific Cq context in the downstream validation record; keep them out of primer-only scoring ([MIQE 2.0 full text](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | These are sampling and assay-validation limits; they do not predict sensitivity from primer sequence or amplicon size. |
| Melt-curve interpretation | A single peak supports—but does not alone prove—specific amplification; multiple peaks/shoulders or late NTC signal require investigation before quantification ([Thermo SYBR guidance](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0013361_DyNAmo_SYBR_Green_qPCR_UG.pdf)). | Peak shape and temperature are run- and instrument-dependent. |
| Normalization and controls | Record reference-target stability, NTC, positive control, no-RT control where applicable, replicate design and exclusion rules in the validation plan ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)). | The primer engine cannot choose a biologically stable reference gene without the study design. |
| Carryover contamination | If a kit uses dUTP/UNG, record that chemistry and keep reaction setup physically separated from product analysis; the choice is kit- and instrument-dependent, not a universal primer property ([Thermo DyNAmo SYBR manual](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0013361_DyNAmo_SYBR_Green_qPCR_UG.pdf)). | The current primer request does not model UNG compatibility or laboratory zoning. |
| Template and passive reference | Record template amount/volume, reaction volume and whether ROX is required by the instrument; Thermo's guide notes both cDNA-volume limits and instrument-specific ROX requirements ([Thermo DyNAmo SYBR manual](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/MAN0013361_DyNAmo_SYBR_Green_qPCR_UG.pdf)). | These are kit/instrument/sample variables and cannot be inferred from sequence alone. |
| Protocol branch by primer Tm | Do not treat a `59–65 °C` search window as proof that every returned pair is valid for the profile's two-step `60 °C` hold. The PowerUp guide gives fast/standard two-step conditions for primers with `Tm ≥60 °C`, but gives a separate standard three-step branch for primers with `Tm <60 °C` (`55–60 °C` anneal followed by `72 °C` extension) ([PowerUp SYBR Green User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf)). | The selected cycling branch, primer Tm method and master mix must travel together in the assay record. |
| Acquisition stage | Model fluorescence acquisition as an instrument/protocol field rather than assuming that the extension step is the read step. Bio-Rad's singleplex SYBR example places an `86 °C / 2 s` segment and a plate read after the `72 °C` extension, while other vendor protocols specify a read at the end of the anneal/extension hold ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf), [Luna M3003 manual](https://www.neb.com/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3003.pdf?rev=b504cccef6684f8ab5d3c5a6509a57ec)). | A design result cannot claim a valid acquisition programme unless the instrument and mix-specific protocol are known. |
| Final extension | There is no universal SYBR final-extension rule. Bio-Rad's documented singleplex SYBR example includes `72 °C / 10 min` before the melt curve and again after it, whereas the Luna protocol moves from cycling to the melt-curve stage without that final extension ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf), [Luna qPCR protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/protocols/luna-universal-qpcr-master-mix-protocol-m3003)). | The profile must record an explicit protocol decision and must not present omission or inclusion as a chemistry law. |
| Vendor overlay selection | Named kit instructions must be selected as a versioned overlay rather than merged into the shared profile. Bio-Rad iTaq and QIAGEN QuantiNova both target SYBR qPCR but differ in product-size guidance, acquisition/cycling timing and reaction-handling notes; persist vendor, product, document revision, instrument, template type and selected protocol branch in provenance ([iTaq protocol](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10000068167.pdf), [QuantiNova handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/quantinova-sybr-green-pcr-handbook)). | An overlay documents a starting condition; it does not constitute a measured validation result for the user's assay. |
| Multiplex competition | A multiplex qPCR design must be optimized and validated as a combined assay; Bio-Rad's current guide warns that competition can change assay efficiency and recommends joint validation rather than transferring singleplex performance. The current `qpcr-sybr` profile deliberately does **not** declare the generic `multiplex` modifier because the existing multiplex orchestrator models agarose/capillary/NGS size readouts rather than real-time dye/melt discrimination. A future qPCR-multiplex branch must require one shared named chemistry/RT authority, complete cross-primer review and measured joint efficiency/specificity/readout evidence ([Bio-Rad qPCR Assay Design Guide](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6894.pdf)). | Current runtime is simplex dye-qPCR. Do not use the generic endpoint multiplex route as evidence of amplification balance, SYBR melt discrimination, spectral separation, matrix competition or measured efficiency. |
| Dye-based multiplex boundary | NEB's dye-versus-probe guidance describes dye detection as reporting any double-stranded DNA and recommends a single amplicon for dye-based qPCR; published multiplex SYBR work shows that multiple products can be discriminated only under assay-specific conditions such as sufficiently separated melt temperatures and joint optimization. Treat `multiplex` as a validation branch requiring separate singleplex references, melt/gel evidence, competition testing and an explicit decision that dye-based detection is fit for the intended use ([NEB dye-versus-probe guidance](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/should-i-use-probe-or-dye-detection-for-my-qpcr-assays), [Giglio et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14602929/)). | A single multiplex melt peak is not proof that all products are present or resolved; a probe/channel design may be required instead. |
| Vendor Tm window and calculation context | Bio-Rad's SYBR design guidance gives `50–65 °C` and states that its nearest-neighbour calculation uses `50 mM` salt and `300 nM` oligonucleotide concentration; this disagrees with any statement that all qPCR manuals require a `60 °C` primer Tm ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf)). | Keep `59–65 °C` as this profile's deliberate starting envelope for its selected two-step programme, not as a universal qPCR boundary; report the salt, oligo concentration and Tm model. |
| Amplicon structure and repeated bases | Bio-Rad recommends checking amplicon secondary structure at the annealing temperature and avoiding templates with long single-base repeats; this supports a structure/repeat review of the product sequence, not only primer-level screening ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf)). | A product sequence can be carried forward for review, but a sequence-only result is not a measured melt curve or proof of amplification efficiency. |
| Template input and instrument normalization | Vendor instructions bind template amount, reaction volume and passive-reference handling to the selected mix and instrument. PowerUp specifies different cDNA/gDNA input guidance and warns that ROX handling differs between instrument families; Luna likewise documents instrument-compatible reference-dye behaviour ([PowerUp SYBR Green User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf), [Luna M3003 manual](https://www.neb.com/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3003.pdf?rev=b504cccef6684f8ab5d3c5a6509a57ec)). | These fields belong to the run setup and must not be silently inferred from the primer pair. |
| Two-step versus one-step RT-qPCR | One-step and two-step RT-qPCR differ in whether reverse transcription and qPCR occur in the same or separate tubes; in two-step workflows, the RT priming strategy can affect which transcript regions are represented. Bio-Rad therefore treats RT format, RT primer choice and transcript position as part of assay design ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf)). | The primer design record must state the intended RT format and preserve the no-RT/genomic-DNA control requirement; it cannot use a DNA-only result to certify an RT-qPCR assay. |

The module remains a primer-pair and preflight module. It must not emit a fabricated Cq, efficiency, melt-curve result, LOD/LOQ, dynamic range or normalized expression value; those values belong to the measured qPCR record ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).

The profile's two-step `60 °C` programme is therefore a controlled starting choice, not a claim that every vendor or every primer pair uses the same cycle. A pair whose calculated Tm is below the selected hold must be routed to an explicit protocol review, because at least one current vendor guide uses a separate anneal/extension branch for that case ([PowerUp SYBR Green User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf)).

The profile must preserve the distinction between amplification, acquisition and post-run specificity. The official Bio-Rad example demonstrates that a SYBR run may acquire fluorescence in a dedicated high-temperature segment and may include a vendor-specific final extension, while Luna documents a read at the end of its extension step and then a melt curve; neither example can be generalized to every master mix or instrument ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf), [Luna M3003 manual](https://www.neb.com/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3003.pdf?rev=b504cccef6684f8ab5d3c5a6509a57ec)).

The product sequence is also a validation input: secondary structure and repeated-base patterns can affect the assay, but the presence or absence of those motifs in silico cannot replace a melt curve, gel confirmation where appropriate, efficiency study, controls or raw fluorescence review ([Bio-Rad Real-Time PCR Applications Guide, Bulletin 5279](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_5279.pdf), [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

## Validation, control and failure-mode matrix

The following matrix is a handoff contract for the measured qPCR record. It intentionally separates an observed failure mode from a possible cause: SYBR fluorescence, amplification-curve shape and Cq cannot independently identify the product or rescue an invalid control ([Ruiz-Villalba et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727009/), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

| Observation / failure mode | Defensible interpretation and next action | Required evidence before quantitative use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NTC amplifies | Treat as contamination, primer-dimer or other nonspecific amplification until the NTC melt profile, sample profile and setup history are reviewed; a late Cq alone does not identify the cause ([PowerUp SYBR User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf), [Ruiz-Villalba et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727009/)). | NTC raw/derivative curve, product-size check where needed, contamination investigation and documented disposition. |
| Multiple peaks, shoulders or sample/NTC peak mismatch | Investigate primer-dimer and off-target products; optimize annealing temperature/primer concentration or redesign. A single peak is supportive but not sufficient because products can share Tm ([Ririe et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/abio.1996.9916), [Thermo specificity white paper](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/GSD/Reference-Materials/qpcr-gene-expression-specificity-white-paper.pdf)). | Raw and derivative melt curves plus orthogonal size/sequence evidence when ambiguity remains. |
| One sharp peak at the expected temperature | Accept as provisional homogeneity evidence only; do not infer target identity or exclude a coincident-Tm product from the peak alone ([Ririe et al. 1997](https://doi.org/10.1006/abio.1996.9916), [Thermo specificity white paper](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/GSD/Reference-Materials/qpcr-gene-expression-specificity-white-paper.pdf)). | In-silico specificity, expected-size gel or sequence confirmation for assay validation, and a validated run context. |
| High Cq, poor replicate precision or failed amplification | Check template amount/quality, inhibitors, pipetting, cycle count, primer-dimers and residual polymerase; PowerUp specifically recommends testing inhibitor-free template and gives `40` cycles as its default context ([PowerUp SYBR User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf)). | Template class/input, matrix, replicate data, instrument plot, control results and selected corrective action. |
| Dilution improves Cq or efficiency | This is compatible with inhibitor dilution but is not diagnostic without a matched control; MIQE 2.0 warns that dilution can inflate standard-curve efficiency, so compare control-target Cq and curve-derived efficiency ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [King et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20041847/)). | Dilution factor, expected-versus-observed control Cq, efficiency by method, matrix and recovery calculation. |
| Standard-curve slope `<−3.32` | The apparent efficiency is above `100%` under the MIQE equation; investigate dilution preparation, pipetting, baseline/threshold, inhibition and nonspecific amplification before acceptance ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | Standard identity, independent reaction replicates, slope/intercept, confidence interval, melt/NTC review and validated linear range. |
| Target and reference assay efficiencies differ | Do not use uncorrected `2^−ΔΔCq` as if both assays were `100%` efficient; use efficiency-corrected quantities or a validated alternative and report the method ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [efficiency-correction review](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/67/6/829/6247760)). | Measured efficiency for each assay, reference-gene stability evidence and per-sample normalized quantities. |
| Standard points fall outside the validated linear range | Do not report those quantities as calibrated values; the reported range is bounded by LLOQ and ULOQ, and LOD is a detection limit rather than a precision claim ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)). | LOD/LLOQ/ULOQ design, precision/accuracy criteria, copy or mass input and replicate outcomes. |
| No-RT control amplifies for an intron-spanning RT-qPCR assay | Investigate genomic DNA carryover, pseudogene amplification or assay contamination; no-RT is not optional evidence when genomic amplification cannot be excluded ([PowerUp SYBR User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)). | RT-minus result, genomic alignment, amplicon design and extraction/RT provenance. |
| Reference-gene stability changes by group or treatment | Do not retain a popular housekeeping gene by convention; re-evaluate candidate genes under the current study design and use an efficiency-aware stability method ([Andersen et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15289330/), [reference-gene software comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25825906/)). | Candidate panel, sample-group definitions, efficiency correction, stability outputs and normalization-factor diagnostics. |
| Multiplex SYBR melt curve shows only one product | Do not assume only one target amplified: preferential dye binding can hide another product, and discrimination requires sufficiently separated Tm plus joint optimization/validation ([Giglio et al. 2003](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14602929/), [Bio-Rad Bulletin 6894](https://www.bio-rad.com/sites/default/files/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/Bulletin_6894.pdf)). | Each singleplex product profile, multiplex melt/gel evidence, target balance, competition study and joint efficiency results. |
| Cross-platform absolute Cq shifts | Preserve platform and native analysis software; Thermo warns that absolute Cq values may differ across Applied Biosystems, Bio-Rad and Roche systems even when trends are consistent ([PowerUp SYBR User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0013511_PowerUp_mastermix_UG.pdf)). | Instrument model/software, calibration, optical-reference setting, run controls and cross-platform validation. |
| Long bench delay or altered primer/template/non-template concentration changes specificity | Treat the assay as not yet robust; nonspecific-product occurrence can depend on primer, template, non-template concentration and time on bench, and may be unrelated to Cq or calculated efficiency ([Ruiz-Villalba et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5727009/)). | Setup timing, concentrations, temperature history, replicate melt profiles and a documented robustness study. |

## Standards and authoritative sequence-database boundary

[MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148) is the current qPCR reporting revision used here; it covers sample handling, assay design, reverse transcription/template dilution, qPCR protocol, efficiency, melting analysis, data processing and controls. It is a reporting and good-practice framework, not a substitute for assay-specific wet-lab validation or a universal SYBR acceptance-limit table.

[ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html) remains the current published International Standard for qPCR and dPCR quantification of DNA and RNA targets and covers assay design, controls, specificity, data quality, normalization, precision, linearity, LOD, LOQ, trueness, robustness, traceability and uncertainty. Its scope explicitly does not provide acceptance criteria for biological sampling, extraction or application-specific matrices; those must remain visible in the assay record. As of this review (`2026-08-30`), ISO also lists the second-edition project [ISO/CD 20395.2](https://www.iso.org/standard/91706.html) as a **Committee Draft**. The draft expands/updates the same qPCR/dPCR performance domain, including analytical design, specificity, data-quality and acceptance-criteria topics, but it is non-normative until published; PCRStudio must therefore record `ISO 20395:2019` as the adopted standard while tracking `ISO/CD 20395.2` only as a revision watch.

[CLSI MM06, 3rd edition](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm06/) is a current clinical/infectious-disease quantitative molecular-method branch published on `2026-07-22`; it addresses standards, calibrators, validation, reporting, QC and interpretation, but explicitly does not establish a clinically acceptable LoQ because consensus is lacking. It is relevant when the intended use is infectious-disease testing, not a replacement for MIQE in research expression assays.

[CLSI MM03](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm03/) is the complementary molecular-method branch for nucleic-acid amplification procedures, including considerations for inhibitors/interfering substances, contamination and false-positive/false-negative control. It is a method-standard boundary, not a SYBR-specific numerical acceptance table; the assay record must still identify the matrix, control placement and intended use.

For sequence provenance, [NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/tools/primer-blast/index.cgi?GROUP_TARGET=on) accepts accessions, FASTA or Gene IDs, can retrieve RefSeq transcripts, allows organism/database restriction and checks forward/reverse as well as forward/forward and reverse/reverse primer-pair products. Its own guidance warns that polymorphic, partial, redundant or incomplete database records can require user adjudication. The result must therefore preserve accession, reference sequence version, organism/taxonomy, database, query, mismatch stringency and search date rather than reporting only “BLAST passed.”
