## Evidence collected

### R5 named-chemistry expansion (2026-09-03)

Generation 1 now exposes six current named long-range authorities—NEB LongAmp M0323, Takara PrimeSTAR GXL R050A, QIAGEN UltraRun 206442/206444, NEB Q5-XT M2499, Promega GoTaq Long M4021, and Thermo Fisher Platinum SuperFi II—plus the historical/discontinued Thermo K018x branch for reproducibility. Q5-XT preserves its rapid 5–15 s/kb protocol and target-length-dependent primer concentration; GoTaq Long remains a distinct hot-start Taq/proofreading blend rather than an alias of ordinary GoTaq; SuperFi II preserves its current up-to-20-kb authority, 60 °C starting annealing context, 15–30 s/kb extension and 0.2 µM primer starting point for >5-kb genomic targets. Vendor reach beyond the GEN1 5–20 kb software envelope is evidence only ([NEB Q5-XT M2499](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/pcr-using-q5-xt-hot-start-high-fidelity-2x-master-mix-m2499), [Promega GoTaq Long](https://www.promega.com/products/pcr/pcr-master-mixes/gotaq-long-pcr-master-mix/), [Thermo Platinum SuperFi II MAN0018859](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0018859_Platinum_SuperFi_II_DNA_Pol_UG.pdf)).

Long-range amplification is not obtained by changing only the product-size ceiling. Polymerase system, proofreading/fidelity, template integrity, extension time and cycling chemistry are coupled to the requested length ([Cheng et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.12.5695), [Green & Sambrook](https://doi.org/10.1101/pdb.prot095158)). Cheng et al. demonstrated very long products with a proofreading-containing system, while Green & Sambrook describe practical long-PCR setup and matching extension time to the enzyme system ([Long Fragment PCR](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4939-7060-5_3)).

The long-range literature does not support one engine-wide maximum length or one universal polymerase ranking. Earlier long-PCR work describes ordinary PCR as reliably covering only a few kilobases and attributes extension failure partly to misincorporation and loss of the non-proofreading polymerase from the template, while later protocol work reports substantially different performance among enzyme systems and template classes ([Waggott](https://doi.org/10.1385/0-89603-499-2:81), [Hogrefe & Borns](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-60761-944-4_2), [comparison of six long-range enzymes](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep05737)). The engine therefore treats processivity, proofreading, template class and locus difficulty as separate evidence fields rather than deriving capability from amplicon length alone.

Product lifecycle is also part of the evidence contract. QIAGEN states that its former `QIAGEN LongRange PCR Kit` line was discontinued in production by `2020-10-15` and names `UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit` as its recommended successor; legacy LongRange protocol values therefore remain historical/product-scoped evidence and must not be presented as the current QIAGEN kit recipe ([QIAGEN LongRange discontinuation/successor notice](https://www.qiagen.com/us/applications/pcr/knowledgebase-faqs/toptaq-hifi-longrange)). The current UltraRun handbook supplies a distinct three-step branch: `93 °C/3 min` activation; up to `35` cycles of `93 °C/30 s`, `55 °C/15 s` (approximately `5 °C` below primer Tm) and `68 °C/30 s/kb` for genomic targets; then `72 °C/10 min` final extension ([UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)).
The 02/2024 handbook also defines a standard two-step branch for primers in its stated `58–65 °C` Tm context: `93 °C/3 min` activation, up to `35` cycles of `93 °C/30 s` and `65 °C/30 s/kb`, then `72 °C/10 min`. Reaction setup is `20 µL` with `0.5 µM` each primer and `1×` of the supplied `4×` master mix; Q-Solution is an optional parallel trial for difficult/GC-rich targets, not a GC-triggered automatic edit. This evidence is now sufficient for a named executable UltraRun overlay, but PCRStudio keeps it inside the existing Generation-1 `5–20 kb` product window and does not infer proprietary buffer thermodynamics.

Current QIAGEN public materials are not fully numerically aligned on UltraRun reach: the discovery product page describes amplification up to `30 kb`, while the 09/2025 molecular-biology enzyme brochure and OEM portfolio list UltraRun at up to `40 kb` ([UltraRun product page](https://www.qiagen.com/us/products/discovery-and-translational-research/pcr-qpcr-dpcr/pcr-enzymes-and-kits/hifidelity-long-range-and-other-pcr/ultrarun-longrange-pcr-kit), [QIAGEN Enzymes for Molecular Biology 09/2025](https://www.qiagen.com/en-us/resources/download/brochureguide/qpro-10257-bro-enz-enzymes-for-molbiol-0925-ww), [QIAGEN OEM enzyme portfolio](https://www.qiagen.com/us/applications/oem-and-custom-solutions/enzymes/enzyme-portfolio)). PCRStudio must preserve that source conflict rather than silently selecting or averaging the values; a deployed protocol should follow the handbook/current kit record selected for the actual product and target.

Primer3 can enforce binding safeguards (Tm, self-complementarity, 3-prime constraints) ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html)), but it cannot prove that a polymerase will traverse a difficult genomic region. A result must distinguish computational compatibility from experimental validation; the current `experimental` profile status is therefore appropriate ([Green & Sambrook](https://doi.org/10.1101/pdb.prot095158)).

For off-target review, NCBI Primer-BLAST combines Primer3 design with BLAST and global alignment to screen forward–reverse, forward–forward and reverse–reverse primer combinations; NCBI recommends using a RefSeq accession when possible and selecting an organism and database appropriate to the intended template ([NCBI Primer-BLAST](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/guide/howto/design-pcr-primers/), [NCBI RefSeq](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/refseq/)). This improves the in-silico specificity record but remains unable to establish long-template integrity, polymerase traversal or wet-lab specificity.

### Amplicon-length regimes are protocol branches, not universal ceilings

The defensible regime map is: conventional PCR is commonly reliable only to about `3–4 kb`; QIAGEN labels `0.1–10 kb` as long-range and `>10 kb` as very long-range; and products above `20 kb` should be treated as an extended, kit-conditioned branch rather than as an engine-wide promise ([Waggott](https://doi.org/10.1385/0-89603-499-2:81), [QIAGEN LongRange protocol](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/Protocols/qiagen-longrange-pcr-kit-en), [Hogrefe & Borns](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-60761-944-4_2)). These boundaries organize protocol selection; they do not imply that every locus at a boundary has the same yield, specificity or fidelity.

Primary demonstrations span different template classes and therefore cannot be collapsed into one maximum: Barnes reported `35 kb` from lambda DNA, Cheng et al. reported up to `22 kb` from human genomic DNA and `42 kb` from lambda DNA, and Hogrefe and Borns described a fusion-polymerase workflow for genomic targets exceeding `20 kb` ([Barnes](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.6.2216), [Cheng et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.91.12.5695), [Hogrefe & Borns](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-60761-944-4_2)). These are demonstrations under named chemistries and protocols, not validation of the present locus or template.

### Template quality and handling are first-order constraints

For genomic long-range PCR, the **legacy** QIAGEN LongRange handbook required high-molecular-weight DNA with an average size of `>20–50 kb` for its very-long-range branch; the current UltraRun handbook instead accepts genomic DNA, cDNA, plasmid DNA and other DNA molecules while emphasizing that template quality, quantity and contaminants materially affect amplification. Thermo K018x likewise warns that high-quality, intact DNA is essential and that damaged template can create background ([legacy QIAGEN LongRange handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/en-qiagen-longrange-pcr-handbook), [UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww), [Thermo K0182 protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012921_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0182_UG.pdf)). A template record should therefore include source class, integrity/quality evidence, storage history and suspected inhibitors before a long-range result is called a chemistry failure.

Template mass is not interchangeable with template quality: Thermo gives `0.01–1 ng` plasmid/phage and `0.1–1 µg` genomic DNA as starting amounts, while its manual notes that phenol, EDTA and proteinase K carryover can inhibit polymerases; NEB additionally warns that higher DNA concentrations reduce specificity and increase extra bands, especially with many cycles ([Thermo K0182 protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012921_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0182_UG.pdf), [NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/ja-jp/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)). Dilution or cleanup is therefore a validation branch, not evidence that a larger input is always better.

### GC-rich failure is chemistry- and primer-dependent

Manufacturer guidance supports several non-equivalent rescue branches: NEB suggests `2–4 min` initial denaturation at `94 °C` for difficult/GC-rich templates and reports that DMSO or formamide may help; Thermo K018x allows product-scoped DMSO branches; current QIAGEN UltraRun supplies Q-Solution as an optional difficult/GC-rich-template additive rather than as an automatic sequence-derived edit ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [Thermo K0182 protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012921_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0182_UG.pdf), [UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)). No additive should be selected from bulk amplicon GC alone.

Proofreading is not a universal antidote to GC difficulty. Zhu et al. found that G-rich primer sequences including `GGGGG` and `GGGGHGG` can inhibit several proofreading polymerases through G-quadruplex-related binding while not inhibiting Taq in their experiments; lowering an inhibitory primer below about `0.1 µM` improved amplification in that test system ([Zhu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep28769)). This supports a targeted primer/chemistry comparison when a proofreading mix fails, not a universal recommendation to replace the enzyme or lower primer concentration.

## Deep-search enrichment — 2025–2026 long-range polymerase generation

The long-range atlas now includes three current product families that materially extend the vendor-specific length/speed landscape ([Thermo Scientific long-range PCR guidance](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/references/protocols/nucleic-acid-amplification-and-expression/pcr-protocol/long-pcr.html)).

**Q5-XT M2499.** NEB reports approximately `280× Taq` fidelity, `5–15 s/kb`, amplification of diverse targets up to `20 kb`, simple templates up to `30 kb`, and a universal `62 °C` annealing protocol. This is a distinct Q5-XT formulation and must not overwrite standard Q5 kinetics ([NEB Q5-XT](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/m2499-q5-xt-hot-start-high-fidelity-2x-master-mix)).

**KOD Long KML-101.** TOYOBO's KOD Long PCR Master Mix is a UKOD-based `2×` hot-start master mix with an elongation accelerator. The current product/manual supports a length-conditioned runtime branch: `5 s/kb` below `10 kb` and `10 s/kb` at/above `10 kb`, while reporting high fidelity on targets above `10 kb`, blunt-end products, crude-sample support, and demonstrated amplification through approximately `47.5 kb`; the PCR product was also used for long-read sequencing in the vendor example. The `47.5 kb` value is a vendor-demonstrated target, not a universal ceiling or guaranteed reach ([TOYOBO KOD Long](https://www.toyobo-global.com/products/lifescience/products/pcr_022/index.html), [KML-101 manual](https://www.toyobo-global.com/sites/default/static_root/products/lifescience/support/manual/KML-101_Manual_EN.pdf)).

**KOD One and Platinum SuperFi II.** TOYOBO lists KOD One around `40 kb` and provides a vendor example spanning `17.5–40 kb` human-genomic targets using `68 °C` extension at `10 s/kb`; Thermo Fisher lists Platinum SuperFi II at `≤20 kb` routine amplification and up to `40 kb` with additional optimization, with `15–30 s/kb` and `60 °C` universal annealing. These records remain chemistry- and example-specific ([TOYOBO KOD One product matrix](https://www.toyobo-global.com/products/lifescience/products/pcr.html), [TOYOBO 17.5–40 kb example](https://lifescience.toyobo.co.jp/embodiment/detail.php?embodiment_id=143), [Thermo Fisher Platinum SuperFi II](https://www.thermofisher.com/ar/en/home/life-science/pcr/pcr-enzymes-master-mixes.html)).

## Current implementation assessment

- The executable GEN1 long-range surface is a named multi-protocol registry: current NEB LongAmp, Takara PrimeSTAR GXL and QIAGEN UltraRun branches plus the historical/discontinued Thermo K0181/K0182 branch for reproducibility. No one vendor recipe is the universal long-range protocol.
- The base `20–36 nt`, `55–74 °C`, `40–60% GC`, `5–20 kb` profile is a cross-protocol Primer3 screening envelope. A selected protocol may narrow primer constraints, but it does not replace the shared thermodynamic screening reaction with reconstructed proprietary buffer chemistry.
- Bench cycling, primer concentration, template input, additives and extension rules come only from the selected named protocol. When no reviewed protocol is selected, the executable long-range assay fails closed rather than emitting a generic programme.
- The `5–20 kb` GEN1 product range is an application envelope, not a processivity promise. Supplier reach remains chemistry-, template- and locus-specific and requires post-PCR full-length/identity evidence.
- Sequence design cannot measure template integrity, inhibitor burden, yield or fidelity in the user's sample; those remain validation evidence rather than sequence-only scores.

## Decision from the current supplier protocols

The current implementation does **not** nominate a single supplier as the long-range default. Candidate discovery uses a bounded cross-protocol sequence-screening envelope; an explicit named protocol then contributes only the constraints and bench metadata that its manufacturer actually documents. Current executable branches are NEB LongAmp, Takara PrimeSTAR GXL, QIAGEN UltraRun, NEB Q5-XT, Promega GoTaq Long and Thermo Platinum SuperFi II long-range. Thermo K0181/K0182 remains executable only as a historical/discontinued reproducibility branch ([NEB Q5-XT](https://www.neb.com/en-us/products/m2499-q5-xt-hot-start-high-fidelity-2x-master-mix), [Promega GoTaq Long](https://www.promega.com/products/pcr/taq-polymerase/gotaq-long-range-pcr-master-mix/), [QIAGEN UltraRun handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)).

This separation is deliberate. LongAmp publishes a wider primer-length interval than the current Primer3 executable ceiling; Takara publishes its own product-length-dependent primer/cycling guidance; UltraRun publishes distinct two-step/three-step branches and an optional Q-Solution path; K018x publishes its historical two-/three-step and auto-extension behavior. Converting any one of these into a universal long-PCR annealing hold, dNTP baseline, additive rule or extension law would overstate the evidence ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [Takara PrimeSTAR GXL manual](https://takara.co.kr/file/manual/pdf/R050A_e.v1906Da.pdf), [QIAGEN UltraRun handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)).

The profile therefore keeps only the shared design envelope and generic validation questions. Reaction volume, primer concentration, Mg/dNTP values, template input, additives, activation, annealing/extension and cycle programme are emitted from the selected protocol record. Proprietary buffer composition is not reconstructed into Primer3. The `5–20 kb` product window remains a Generation-1 design boundary even when a supplier advertises a wider reach ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html), [QIAGEN UltraRun handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)).

Historical K018x and discontinued QIAGEN LongRange evidence remains valuable for reproduction and for understanding long-PCR failure modes, but it is explicitly labeled legacy. It must not silently override the current LongAmp/PrimeSTAR/UltraRun branches or be presented as current QIAGEN operating guidance ([Thermo K0181 manual](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012920_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0181_UG.pdf), [QIAGEN discontinued-products notice](https://www.qiagen.com/us/applications/pcr/knowledgebase-faqs/toptaq-hifi-longrange)).

## Extended long-range rules

Long-range performance is jointly limited by polymerase processivity, template integrity, locus difficulty and the selected reaction conditions; a larger product-size window alone is not a long-range model ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [Green & Sambrook](https://doi.org/10.1101/pdb.prot095158)). The profile therefore exposes the enzyme family and template-quality prerequisite as first-class evidence, while keeping any reach claim kit-specific.

| Additional rule | Resolution for Long-range PCR | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kit-specific reach | Use the selected supplier's documented reach as an envelope. For example, the current LongAmp Taq protocol reports targets up to 30 kb and states that template quality is essential; this must not be copied as a universal capability of every proofreading mix ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200)). | The application does not benchmark every polymerase, locus or template type. |
| Template-class/kit pairing | Record whether the template is genomic, plasmid, viral, cDNA or another class before selecting a reach claim. Thermo and NEB publish different reach and input guidance by template class; current UltraRun explicitly accepts genomic DNA, cDNA, plasmid DNA and other DNA molecules and gives a `1 µg–10 pg` input span dependent on target abundance. The result must therefore bind reach/input metadata to the selected kit and template class rather than display one engine-wide maximum ([Thermo K0181 protocol](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012920_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0181_UG.pdf), [NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww)). | FASTA content alone cannot establish molecular integrity, extraction quality, target abundance or whether a supplier's demonstrated reach applies. |
| Template amount and integrity | Record template source, integrity/fragmentation risk and input amount. NEB gives different starting amounts for genomic versus plasmid/viral DNA and distinguishes targets up to and above 15 kb ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200)). | FASTA cannot reveal shearing, inhibitors, extraction loss or sample damage. |
| Mg²⁺, additives and dNTPs | Keep Mg²⁺, additives and dNTPs in the kit reaction record, not in a generic primer rule. The LongAmp protocol describes `1.5–2.0 mM` Mg²⁺ as a common starting range, `0.3 mM` each dNTP and incremental Mg²⁺ optimisation; Thermo and QIAGEN protocols use different conditions ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [QIAGEN LongRange protocol](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/Protocols/qiagen-longrange-pcr-kit-en)). | Additives can rescue some difficult templates and harm others; no sequence-only rule selects them reliably. |
| Difficult-template branch | Treat high-GC, high-secondary-structure and low-template cases as a validation-only protocol branch. NEB LongAmp recommends `2–4 min` initial denaturation at `94 °C` for GC-rich targets and notes that DMSO or formamide may help, while Phusion guidance separately documents GC-buffer/additive optimisation. Record the selected kit, template difficulty, denaturation branch, additive/buffer identity and changed annealing model; do not auto-select an additive from FASTA composition alone ([NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [NEB Phusion optimisation](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-phusion-high-fidelity-dna-polymerase)). | Sequence composition is only a trigger for review; it cannot predict whether an additive rescues the user's locus or preserves fidelity. |
| Fidelity claim | A proofreading-capable blend is a reason to select a fidelity-aware preset, not proof of a particular error rate. Direct comparisons show long-PCR mixtures can be more accurate than Taq yet less accurate than a proofreading polymerase alone ([PCR fidelity study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8836181/)). | If the product will be cloned or used for a sequence claim, clone/amplicon sequence verification remains mandatory. |
| Extension model | Use the supplier's extension table when a kit is selected and preserve the model identity; a `seconds/kb` approximation is not a kinetic law for all enzymes ([NEB LongAmp extension guidance](https://www.neb.com/faqs/what-is-the-extension-rate-when-using-longamp-taq-dna-polymerase), [Thermo K0181 manual](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012920_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0181_UG.pdf)). | The current engine cannot infer locus-specific pausing or polymerase loading. |

These rules keep the long-range result scientifically honest: it may report a kit-conditioned design envelope and a complete validation handoff, but it must not label a multi-kilobase result “validated” without observed full-length product, identity and fidelity evidence ([Green & Sambrook](https://doi.org/10.1101/pdb.prot095158)).

## Controls, validation and failure decision record

A long-range call should be treated as valid only when the chemistry, template and analysis controls agree. A gel band at the expected migration position demonstrates neither sequence identity nor absence of truncated products; the control plan must therefore separate amplification validity, size confirmation, identity and fidelity ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf), [FDA sequencing-method validation guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/121751/download)).

| Control or failure signature | Required evidence / scoped action | Stop or acceptance rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positive amplification control | Run a known target/template through the same reaction and analysis path; the gel positive should be a known-size DNA segment, preferably the same size as the target ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf)). | If the positive control fails to produce its specific product, associated samples are invalid until control integrity, PCR design and reagents are investigated and the batch is rerun ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf)). |
| NTC / reagent blank / extraction blank | Include a negative control containing buffers and reagent water for the PCR/gel path; add step-specific negative controls if contamination cannot be localized ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf)). | Specific amplification in a negative control invalidates associated positive calls until contamination is identified and eliminated ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf)). |
| Size confirmation | Run a DNA ladder on every gel and ensure the expected product lies within the standard's covered size range; do not extrapolate beyond the ladder ([EPA PCR QA/QC guidance](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-07/documents/epa-qaqc-pcr.pdf)). | Report “size-consistent” only when the marker supports the estimate; do not call a band full-length from migration alone. |
| Template integrity / inhibition | Record template class, integrity/degradation evidence, storage history and cleanup; current UltraRun explicitly notes sensitivity to impurities including proteins, phenol/chloroform, salts, ethanol and EDTA, while historical very-long-range workflows may additionally require demonstrably high-molecular-weight DNA. When failure is unexplained, compare an appropriate dilution or cleanup branch because mass, inhibitors and fragmentation affect long-PCR success ([UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww), [legacy QIAGEN LongRange handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/en-qiagen-longrange-pcr-handbook), [Thermo K0182 protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012921_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0182_UG.pdf)). | Do not classify a failed reaction as polymerase-limited until template integrity and inhibitor risk have been assessed. |
| GC / secondary structure rescue | For current QIAGEN UltraRun, treat Q-Solution as the kit-provided optional difficult/GC-rich-template branch and record whether it was used; for NEB/Thermo branches, record the selected additive concentration and altered denaturation program rather than auto-selecting an additive ([UltraRun LongRange PCR Kit Handbook, 02/2024](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/hb-2686-002-hb-ultrarun-longrangepcr-0224-ww), [NEB LongAmp protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/longamp-taq-pcr-kit-e5200), [Thermo K0182 protocol](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0012921_Long_PCR_Enzyme_k0182_UG.pdf)). | Accept a rescue branch only after comparing yield, specificity and product identity; sequence composition alone does not prove that Q-Solution or another additive will improve the user's locus. |
| No product / weak product | Follow the selected manufacturer's branch order: confirm reagents/template, then test kit-scoped template input, additive, enzyme and cycle changes; QIAGEN documents enzyme changes in `0.5 U` steps and cycle changes in `5-cycle` steps for low product ([QIAGEN LongRange handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/en-qiagen-longrange-pcr-handbook)). | Record each changed parameter and retain a no-change comparator; do not interpret increased band intensity alone as improved full-length yield. |
| Multiband / smear | Check annealing and extension against the selected kit, reduce template or cycle count when indicated, and use appropriate electrophoresis; QIAGEN notes that fragments above approximately `30 kb` may require field-inversion or pulsed-field separation ([QIAGEN LongRange handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/en-qiagen-longrange-pcr-handbook)). | A multiband or unresolved smear is not an accepted full-length product, even when one band is near the expected size. |
| Sequence identity / fidelity | For a sequence-based claim, require a single distinct expected-size PCR product and define minimum sequence length, ambiguity thresholds and quality criteria; FDA guidance recommends bidirectional sequencing for higher confidence and describes Q20 as approximately `99%` accuracy for most platforms ([FDA sequencing-method validation guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/121751/download)). | Do not label the long-range product validated from in-silico primer specificity or gel size alone. |

## Standards and guideline boundary

ISO 20395:2019 was checked as an authoritative nucleic-acid method standard, but its stated scope is quantitative real-time PCR and digital PCR. It is therefore relevant only if this module later makes quantitative claims; its covered validation concepts include controls, nucleic-acid purity/integrity, in-silico and in-vitro specificity, precision, linearity, trueness and robustness, and it does not supply endpoint long-range PCR chemistry defaults ([ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).

For clinical or regulated molecular-genetic use, CLSI MM01 provides the broader validation, quality-assurance and reporting context, while CLSI MM17 addresses validation, verification, controls and reference materials for multiplex nucleic-acid assays; neither document establishes the product-specific length, dNTP, Mg²⁺ or extension values recorded here ([CLSI MM01](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm01/), [CLSI MM17 overview](https://clsi.org/resources/insights-blog/nucleic-acid-testing-validation-and-verification/)). The present profile remains a research design/validation record unless a laboratory supplies its own intended-use validation.
