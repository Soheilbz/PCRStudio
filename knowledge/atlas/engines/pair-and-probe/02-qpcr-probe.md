## Scope


> **R17 execution boundary (2026-09-05):** Conventional hydrolysis-probe design is computationally executable under the versioned `thermofisher-taqman-conventional` and `idt-primetime-conventional` authorities. `taqman-mgb` is `external-authority-required`: PCRStudio exports a hash-bound candidate set and may rank MGB probes only after importing MGB-aware Tm values from the declared external authority for that exact candidate set. PCRStudio does not substitute ordinary-DNA Primer3 thermodynamics for the MGB effect. Reporter/quencher/instrument compatibility, multiplex interaction evidence and MIQE 2.0 validation remain explicit layers; computational status does not imply wet-lab validation ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

Hydrolysis-probe qPCR designs a primer pair and an internal probe as one assay. The probe contributes sequence and fluorescence constraints beyond the pair, and the selected polymerase must provide the required 5-prime exonuclease activity; primer-only success cannot be displayed as a finished quantitative assay ([Huggett et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25253910/), [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

The module owns probe topology, reporter/quencher metadata, probe–primer spacing, channel mapping and quantitative validation. It inherits shared pair-search and background rules but must not copy SYBR melt-curve assumptions into a probe assay ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

## Probe-chemistry boundary

The current module is specifically a hydrolysis-probe contract. A TaqMan-style probe is a separate oligo carrying a reporter and quencher whose signal changes after cleavage by the polymerase 5′-nuclease activity; a Scorpion is a primer-linked unimolecular probe, while a molecular beacon uses a stem-loop and hybridization-driven opening. These formats can all be fluorescent, but their topology, required modifications, interaction model and signal mechanism are not interchangeable ([Applied Biosystems reagent guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_046739.pdf), [Scorpion mechanism study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC110766/)).

Consequently, `probe_chemistry=taqman-mgb` may resolve only the typed hydrolysis/MGB/NFQ overlay. A request naming `scorpion`, `molecular-beacon`, FRET or another hybridization/fluorogenic format must be refused or routed to a dedicated chemistry contract; it must not be represented as an ordinary internal probe by dropping its stem, blocker, primer linkage or readout rules ([Scorpion mechanism study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC110766/), [LGC molecular beacon specifications](https://oligos.biosearchtech.com/products/custom-dna-probes-for-qpcr/molecular-beacons)).

This is an implementation boundary, not a claim that hydrolysis probes are universally superior. The selected instrument, spectral compensation, reporter and quencher compatibility, and measured efficiency remain platform- and assay-specific validation inputs ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

## Hydrolysis-probe architecture and placement rules

A canonical linear hydrolysis probe is a non-extendable, internally hybridizing oligonucleotide carrying a reporter at or near its 5′ end and a quencher at or near its 3′ end. While the intact probe is hybridized, reporter emission is quenched; during extension through a correctly bound probe, a compatible polymerase's 5′→3′ nuclease activity separates reporter and quencher and the released signal is measured. The signal therefore requires the correct target sequence, a compatible probe topology, and a polymerase/master-mix branch that supports cleavage; amplification alone is not evidence of a valid probe signal ([Holland et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1871133/), [Applied Biosystems reagent guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_046739.pdf), [NEB Luna Probe manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3004.pdf?hash=BEF81B4D37886156CF14A31E2AF48A92&rev=7d05adcfb11e49ec9bdd948ddaaec009)).

The probe binding interval must be wholly inside the intended amplicon and must not overlap either primer. Applied Biosystems recommends placing primers as close to the probe as practical while avoiding overlap, and a study of 5′ nuclease assays measured a change in signal ratio when the upstream primer–probe distance was reduced from `127` to `4 nt`; that experiment is evidence that geometry can matter, not a universal minimum or optimum distance for every polymerase and buffer ([TaqMan Assay Multiplex PCR Optimization Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/taqman_optimization_man.pdf), [Hoffmann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223296/)).

Probe selection should precede primer-pair selection when the assay is a hydrolysis design: first identify a unique, stable, variant-appropriate probe site, then choose primers that flank it without overlap and remain compatible with the selected annealing/extension programme. The named Applied Biosystems guidance recommends short amplicons of `50–150 bp`, primer Tm of `58–60 °C`, probe Tm of `68–70 °C`, probe GC of `30–80%`, no guanine at the probe 5′ end, and fewer than `4` consecutive guanines; its primer rule also limits G/C in the five nucleotides at the 3′ end to no more than `2`. These are a TaqMan/MGB starting overlay, not chemistry-independent hard laws ([Primer Express design guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/cms_040955.pdf), [TaqMan Assay Multiplex PCR Optimization Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/taqman_optimization_man.pdf)).

The probe–primer Tm relationship must be stored as a scoped rule rather than a single global constant. IDT states that a probe should be at least `6 °C` warmer than its flanking primers and no longer than `28 bases`, while QIAGEN's multiplex guidance uses an `8–10 °C` separation and Bio-Rad's iTaq guide uses a `10 °C` separation; Applied Biosystems' MGB overlay expresses the same design intent as `58–60 °C` primers and `68–70 °C` probes. The engine must preserve the selected chemistry, Tm model, salt conditions and vendor revision with the result instead of silently averaging these intervals ([IDT qPCR design FAQ](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/support/faqs/how-can-i-design-primers-and-probes-for-qPCR), [QIAGEN multiplex PCR handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/quantinova-multiplex-pcr-kit), [Bio-Rad iTaq Universal Probes manual](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10023651.pdf), [Primer Express design guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/cms_040955.pdf)).

The probe should be screened for self-hairpins, probe–primer heterodimers, primer–primer interactions, repetitive runs, and target secondary structure; the binding strand should be chosen with the vendor's stated base-composition rules, and the sequence should be aligned against the intended reference and relevant background database. A clean in-silico screen does not prove that the probe will be hydrolysed, fluoresce on the instrument, or retain inclusivity in unrepresented variants ([IDT qPCR design FAQ](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/support/faqs/how-can-i-design-primers-and-probes-for-qPCR), [QIAGEN multiplex PCR handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/en-US/resources/download/KitHandbook/quantinova-multiplex-pcr-kit), [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

## Reporter, quencher and modification branches

The following branches are chemically related but must not be collapsed into a single `probe` type. A modification changes duplex stability, mismatch discrimination, quenching, synthesis/purification requirements or the valid instrument/readout combinations; each branch therefore carries its own sequence notation, Tm model, label topology, supplier/product and validation status ([Kutyavin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10606668/), [Applied Biosystems Introduction to Gene Expression guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4454239_IntrotoGeneEx_GSG.pdf)).

| Chemistry branch | What the atlas must retain | Evidence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `plain-dual-labelled-DNA` | Unmodified probe sequence, 5′ reporter, 3′ quencher, linker/topology, calculated Tm and purification | There is no universal probe length or Tm; the sequence and buffer determine the duplex and signal ([Holland et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1871133/), [IDT qPCR design FAQ](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/support/faqs/how-can-i-design-primers-and-probes-for-qPCR)). |
| `MGB-NFQ` | 3′ MGB/NFQ identity, probe sequence, MGB-compatible Tm calculation, reporter, 5′-G flag and length | A cited Applied Biosystems overlay uses `13–25 nt`, `68–70 °C` and `30–80%` GC; MGB raises stability and can support shorter probes, but the range is not a universal rule ([Applied Biosystems Introduction to Gene Expression guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4454239_IntrotoGeneEx_GSG.pdf), [Kutyavin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10606668/)). |
| `LNA-dark-quencher` | Exact LNA positions/count, backbone notation, quencher, reporter, salt/Tm model and mismatch position | A primary study reports approximately `+3–8 °C` duplex stabilization per LNA modification in its assay context; number and position of LNA residues remain sequence- and context-dependent ([JAK2 LNA hydrolysis-probe study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893637/), [LNA influenza assay](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19797710/)). |
| `BHQ/BHQplus` | BHQ family, reporter spectral band, linker and whether the probe is dual-labelled or double-quenched | LGC describes BHQ as a non-fluorescent quencher family intended to reduce background across a broad visible range; catalogue compatibility still requires instrument and reporter validation ([LGC BHQ dyes](https://www.biosearchtech.com/support/nac/black-hole-quencher-dyes), [BHQ Probe Master Mix manual](https://biosearchassets.biosearchtech.com/assetsv6/bhq-probe-master-mix-user-manual.pdf)). |
| `ZEN/Iowa-Black` or other double-quenched designs | Internal quencher, terminal quencher, reporter, exact linker positions and supplier | IDT publishes separate dye–quencher combinations and assay-scoped performance; the named pair cannot be generalized to every instrument or probe sequence ([IDT PrimeTime probe assays](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/products/qpcr-and-pcr/gene-expression/primetime-custom-probe-based-assays)). |
| `allele-specific-hydrolysis` | One probe per allele or target state, distinct reporter mapping, shared/validated primers, genotype-call policy | Different reporter dyes can identify allele-specific probe cleavage in one reaction, but genotype clusters and call boundaries require assay/platform validation ([Livak](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10084106/), [Applied Biosystems allelic-discrimination guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4304449_TaqManPCRMM_UG.pdf)). |

MGB and LNA are affinity modifications, not generic “higher specificity” switches. MGB chemistry can produce a stable short duplex and improve discrimination in a sequence-dependent manner; LNA can increase affinity and mismatch discrimination but its effect depends on residue count, position, neighbouring sequence and the PCR mix. The document must therefore preserve the exact modification map and must not calculate a modified-probe Tm with the ordinary unmodified-DNA model ([Kutyavin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10606668/), [JAK2 LNA hydrolysis-probe study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893637/), [Zip nucleic acid hydrolysis-probe study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2853127/)).

The 5′ guanine rule is a signal-quality rule for the cited dual-labelled probe chemistries: a guanine adjacent to the reporter can reduce signal, and an experimental 5′-nuclease study measured lower reporter/quencher ratios for probes beginning with G. The rule must be represented as a chemistry-scoped warning or hard constraint selected by the overlay; it must not be applied to unrelated probe topologies without evidence ([Hoffmann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223296/), [Primer Express design guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/cms_040955.pdf)).

## Master-mix, polymerase and carryover branches

The enzyme gate is necessary but not sufficient. The selected product must provide a validated 5′→3′ nuclease-capable polymerase in a buffer compatible with the probe and instrument; hot-start behaviour, Mg²⁺, dNTP/dUTP choice, passive reference dye, ROX/CXR policy, reverse transcriptase, cycling programme and inhibitor tolerance are product-level fields. A polymerase that amplifies the target but does not cleave the probe is a silent false-negative readout for this module ([Holland et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1871133/), [NEB Luna Probe manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3004.pdf?hash=BEF81B4D37886156CF14A31E2AF48A92&rev=7d05adcfb11e49ec9bdd948ddaaec009), [TaqMan Fast Advanced Master Mix guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0025706_TaqManFastAdvMM_UG.pdf)).

The dUTP/UNG decision is a complete workflow property. UNG can remove uracil-containing carryover amplicons only when the relevant prior amplicons were produced with dUTP-compatible chemistry; it does not repair probe degradation, distinguish a genuine low-copy target from contamination, or replace spatial separation and NTC controls. The selected product must record dUTP support, UNG presence, activation hold, carryover scope and any polymerase restriction ([TaqMan Fast Advanced Master Mix guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/MAN0025706_TaqManFastAdvMM_UG.pdf), [NEB Luna Probe manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3004.pdf?hash=BEF81B4D37886156CF14A31E2AF48A92&rev=7d05adcfb11e49ec9bdd948ddaaec009)).

## Multiplex optics and channel contract

Multiplex probe design is a joint optical and biochemical problem. Every target requires a distinct reporter/channel mapping supported by the instrument, and the record must include passive-reference policy, spectral calibration or compensation status, probe concentration, primer concentration, target copy imbalance and singleplex comparator results. Selecting different dye names is not enough when the instrument lacks the channel or when bleed-through and competition change the Cq or amplitude ([NEB Luna Probe manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3004.pdf?hash=BEF81B4D37886156CF14A31E2AF48A92&rev=7d05adcfb11e49ec9bdd948ddaaec009), [QIAGEN QuantiNova handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/resources/download.aspx?id=5167d782-9fef-4202-bc79-95f358be7d8c&lang=en), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

ROX or CXR is a passive reference, not a target reporter. QIAGEN documents high- and low-ROX operating branches as `1:20` and `1:200` dilutions of its reference solution, while Promega documents `30 nM` and `500 nM` final CXR branches for different instruments; Bio-Rad's iTaq product includes a blend of passive reference dyes and is marketed for ROX-dependent and ROX-independent systems. These values must travel with the master mix and instrument profile, and a probe using ROX as its target reporter must be checked for reference-dye interference before use ([QIAGEN QuantiNova handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/resources/download.aspx?id=5167d782-9fef-4202-bc79-95f358be7d8c&lang=en), [GoTaq Probe manual](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/gotaq-probe-qpcr-master-mix-protocol.pdf?la=en&rev=b49b9d878c2243c7aae3b1f7a5454ff6&sc_lang=en), [Bio-Rad iTaq Universal Probes manual](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10023651.pdf)).

For each multiplex target, the atlas must retain the singleplex and multiplex standard curves, per-target efficiency, dynamic range, LOD/LOQ, Cq distribution, control outcomes and any concentration rebalancing. An assay-level average can hide suppression of a low-copy target by a high-copy target, optical cross-talk, or a probe-specific mismatch; the multiplex result is therefore not promoted from a set of singleplex designs without a comparative run ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [Bio-Rad iTaq Universal Probes manual](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10023651.pdf), [two-probe hydrolysis study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5264587/)).

## Controls, validation and failure-mode matrix

The validation object must distinguish analytical design from measured performance. ISO 20395 covers assay design, in-silico and in-vitro specificity, controls, data quality, acceptance criteria, linearity, precision, trueness, robustness, LOD/LOQ, traceability and measurement uncertainty for qPCR and dPCR; CLSI MM06-Ed3 adds quantitative molecular-method guidance for standards, calibrators, analytical/clinical verification, quality assurance and reporting. Neither standard supplies one universal probe Tm, reporter matrix or clinically acceptable LoQ, so the module must store scope and validation evidence rather than emit a generic “validated” label ([ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html), [CLSI MM06-Ed3](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm06/)).

| No-amplification control | Use a probe-preserving control strategy where appropriate to detect probe degradation or signal-path failure, and distinguish it from absence of target | MIQE précis describes NACs as useful for monitoring probe degradation in probe assays ([MIQE précis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2955025/)). |

For control interpretation, the LGC/Biosearch manual separates external quality controls (known-result material carried through a process stage) from internal quality controls (spiked material used to reveal inhibition). Its matrix treats positive-EQC/negative-NTC as a successful run, positive-EQC/positive-NTC as contamination requiring repeat of positive samples, negative-EQC/positive-NTC as a failed run, and negative-EQC/negative-NTC as a run in which only positive sample calls can be passed while negative samples are retested. Its IQC matrix also distinguishes a true negative with an uninhibited control from a false negative when the IQC fails, and recommends dilution/re-extraction until the IQC returns to its normal level for quantitative interpretation. This is a product-manual decision framework, not a universal regulatory call rule ([BHQ Probe Master Mix manual](https://biosearchassets.biosearchtech.com/assetsv6/bhq-probe-master-mix-user-manual.pdf)).

## Vendor protocol overlay: Applied Biosystems TaqMan

Applied Biosystems guidance gives a concrete custom-assay starting point: PCR-primer Tm of `58–60 °C`, probe Tm around `68–70 °C`, and an amplicon of approximately `50–150 bp` for the cited multiplex guide. The TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix guide describes a common singleplex starting concentration of `900 nM` for each primer and `250 nM` for the fluorescent probe, while SNP genotyping products may use a different probe concentration. These values are vendor/product overlays, not universal hydrolysis-probe laws ([TaqMan Assay Multiplex PCR Optimization Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/taqman_optimization_man.pdf), [TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4304449_TaqManPCRMM_UG.pdf)).

The module must therefore preserve `probe_chemistry`, reporter/quencher, instrument/channel set, primer/probe concentrations and the selected vendor guide as one protocol record. It must not treat a primer Tm match or a 50–150 bp product as proof of amplification efficiency, absence of inhibition or quantitative validity ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

The current named overlay is `taqman-mgb`. It records the Applied Biosystems TaqMan MGB/NFQ chemistry, reporter choices, 58–60 °C primer and 68–70 °C probe vendor windows, a 13–25 nt MGB-probe window, and a 50–150 bp product envelope. **Generation-1 does not calculate MGB Tm internally.** Primer3's internal-oligo thermodynamics model an unmodified oligonucleotide and do not encode the MGB modification, whereas Applied Biosystems directs MGB Tm/design checks through an MGB-specific Primer Express document/model. PCRStudio therefore exports stable MGB candidates, binds the export with `candidate_set_sha256`, and accepts ranking values only when an external MGB-aware authority returns matching candidate IDs/hash plus tool/version/time provenance ([Primer3 manual](https://primer3.org/manual.html), [Applied Biosystems Real-Time PCR Systems Reagent Guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_052263.pdf), [TaqMan MGB product/support](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4316033)). The 13–25 nt range and the 5′-non-G rule are vendor design guidance; the product lower bound is still subject to the engine's requirement that primers leave physical room for an internal probe ([Applied Biosystems Introduction to Gene Expression guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4454239_IntrotoGeneEx_GSG.pdf), [Applied Biosystems Real-Time PCR Systems Reagent Guide](https://tools.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_052263.pdf)).

The overlay keeps explicit user-entered primer and probe constraints authoritative, and the result/report carry the selected chemistry and starting concentrations without pretending that a compatible instrument, spectral compensation, amplification efficiency, limit of detection or channel assignment has been measured. Reporter options are catalogue choices, not a claim that every instrument supports every dye ([Applied Biosystems TaqMan MGB product](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4316033), [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

### Named Applied Biosystems Universal Master Mix protocol branch

The Universal PCR Master Mix guide is a product-specific protocol, not a general hydrolysis-probe law. For a custom quantitation assay it specifies a 2X master mix at `1X`, a custom-primer optimization grid of `50`, `300` and `900 nM` for each primer, and a probe optimization range of `50–250 nM`. Each condition is run with at least `4` technical replicates. The guide's worked reaction volumes are `10`, `20` or `50 µL`; its DNA template example is `1–100 ng` per reaction. These values must be retained as a selected product-protocol branch, not silently substituted for the profile baseline or for the commonly supplied 20X gene-expression assay concentration (`900 nM` each primer and `250 nM` probe) ([TaqMan Universal PCR Master Mix User Guide](https://www.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/4304449_TaqManPCRMM_UG.pdf), [Thermo Fisher assay-concentration FAQ](https://www.thermofisher.com/order/catalog/product/4304972/faqs)).

For that product branch, the guide gives standard-mode cycling of `50°C for 2 min` when AmpErase UNG is present, `95°C for 10 min` polymerase activation, then `40` cycles of `95°C for 15 s` and `60°C for 1 min`. ## Template-neighbourhood and calibration-material context — 2026 evidence

A 2026 Scientific Reports study shows that a qPCR assay can be biased even when the **amplicon itself is not GC-rich** if the target remains physically linked to a neighbouring high-GC region. In that experiment, quantitation depended on whether the standard-curve material and sample shared comparable flanking/fragmentation context. Therefore the probe-assay record should retain calibrator material type, topology/fragmentation or restriction-digestion state, relevant neighbouring sequence context when known, and whether the calibration material is demonstrably commutable with the intended sample. Additive rescue is a study-specific optimization branch, not a universal qPCR recipe ([Scientific Reports 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-55452-6)).

## Numeric quantitative-validation record

MIQE 2.0 adds numeric validation fields that must remain part of the assay record rather than being reduced to a generic “validated” flag. For method optimization it describes a `1.5–4 mM` Mg²⁺ exploration range and `100–800 nM` primer exploration range; these are optimization windows, not automatic final concentrations. A standard curve should ideally use at least `3` technical replicates at every dilution, with independently prepared mixes and separate wells, and should span `4–5 orders of magnitude` of template concentration. The record must retain the dilution series, replicate count, slope, efficiency, confidence interval, prediction interval and the points excluded from the linear range ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

The same guidance sets important interpretation boundaries: the LOD cannot be below `3 target copies` by Poisson sampling, variability increases below `10 copies/reaction`, and an example assay at `90%` efficiency reaches its LLOQ at `35 cycles` and LOD at `37 cycles`; at `70%` efficiency the respective values are `42` and `45 cycles`. These are analytical interpretation examples and lower-bound constraints, not universal cutoffs for every instrument or matrix. The module must expose them as source-scoped validation metadata and must never convert them into a silent positive/negative calling rule ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

For multiplex use, each target must retain its own efficiency, dynamic range, LOD/LOQ, Cq distribution and control results, and the multiplex result must be compared with the corresponding singleplex result. A combined assay-level efficiency number is insufficient when targets behave differently ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

## Evidence

The cited sources support co-designing primers and hydrolysis probe chemistry and requiring a cleavage-capable enzyme, but they do not justify one instrument-independent probe Tm, reporter compatibility matrix or efficiency value. Those values remain tied to the selected assay and platform ([Huggett et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25253910/), [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).

## Heavy-pass enrichment — primer/probe evolutionary drift

A probe assay remains sequence-valid only relative to the sequence population it was evaluated against. A 2026 SARS-CoV-2 study analyzed `20` primer/probe sets against `135,852` high-quality genomes from `10` countries across variants and time periods and documented substantial geographic and temporal heterogeneity in mutations affecting primer/probe targets. The authors recommended monitoring every `6–12 months` and considering redesign when target-site mutations reached `≥10%` frequency. PCRStudio retains those values only as **study-specific surveillance recommendations for that use case**, not universal redesign thresholds ([J Med Virol 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42312714/)).

For assays exposed to evolving targets, retain `design_date`, reference/database release, geographic/time sampling boundary, primer/probe-site mutation frequencies, last drift scan and any redesign/revalidation decision. A mismatch alert is a sequence-risk signal; it does not by itself quantify the resulting Cq shift, sensitivity loss or false-negative rate without empirical evidence ([WHO molecular-assay validation guidance](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-HTM-NIV-2012.4)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

The following gaps are deliberately left unresolved because the available literature and vendor documents do not provide a universal value, or because a run-specific input is required. They are not permission to invent defaults ([MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html), [CLSI MM06-Ed3](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm06/)).

- `[unresolved evidence gap]` No universal numeric minimum/optimum primer–probe distance is established across sequences, polymerases and buffers; the engine currently records a non-overlap gate and keeps the measured distance for ranking ([TaqMan Assay Multiplex PCR Optimization Guide](https://documents.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/LSG/manuals/taqman_optimization_man.pdf), [Hoffmann et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12223296/)).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` There is no single validated Tm correction for MGB, LNA, BHQ, ZEN or other modified probes across all salt, Mg²⁺, dNTP, polymerase and instrument conditions; exact modification position and the calculation model must remain part of the chemistry branch ([Kutyavin et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10606668/), [JAK2 LNA hydrolysis-probe study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2893637/), [IDT qPCR design FAQ](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/support/faqs/how-can-i-design-primers-and-probes-for-qPCR)).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` A complete instrument-by-reporter-by-quencher compatibility and spectral-compensation matrix is not inferable from dye catalogue names; it must be supplied by the instrument/product branch and verified experimentally ([IDT PrimeTime probe assays](https://www.idtdna.com/pages/products/qpcr-and-pcr/gene-expression/primetime-custom-probe-based-assays), [QIAGEN QuantiNova handbook](https://www.qiagen.com/resources/download.aspx?id=5167d782-9fef-4202-bc79-95f358be7d8c&lang=en)).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` There is no universal primer/probe concentration, Mg²⁺, cycle count, ROX level or template-mass setting. The recorded values are named-product starting conditions and must not be promoted to engine-wide defaults ([NEB Luna Probe manual](https://www.neb.com/en/-/media/nebus/files/manuals/manualm3004.pdf?hash=BEF81B4D37886156CF14A31E2AF48A92&rev=7d05adcfb11e49ec9bdd948ddaaec009), [Bio-Rad iTaq Universal Probes manual](https://www.bio-rad.com/webroot/web/pdf/lsr/literature/10023651.pdf), [GoTaq Probe manual](https://worldwide.promega.com/-/media/files/resources/protocols/technical-manuals/101/gotaq-probe-qpcr-master-mix-protocol.pdf?la=en&rev=b49b9d878c2243c7aae3b1f7a5454ff6&sc_lang=en)).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` The current profile has only the named `taqman-mgb` runtime overlay; LNA, BHQ/double-quenched, allele-specific and vendor-specific ROX/CXR branches are atlas evidence but are not represented as selectable runtime protocols until their typed fields and validation behaviour are defined ([internal software-contract; no external source]).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` The current module does not receive an actual instrument calibration/compensation file, raw fluorescence, baseline method, threshold decision, standard material identity, matrix panel, efficiency confidence interval or measured LOD/LOQ. The result must remain `limited` until those run-level records exist ([MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148), [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/standard/67893.html)).
- `[unresolved evidence gap]` No universal clinical or biological Cq cutoff is defensible for this general research profile; intended-use decision limits, reference materials and clinical verification belong to the assay owner and applicable regulatory/quality framework ([CLSI MM06-Ed3](https://clsi.org/shop/standards/mm06/), [MIQE 2.0](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40272429/)).
