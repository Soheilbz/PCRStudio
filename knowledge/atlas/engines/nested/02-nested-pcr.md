## Scope

Nested PCR designs an outer pair and an inner pair for the same target. The inner product must lie inside the outer product, and the two rounds must be reported as a coupled scheme. This is not one ordinary pair with extra primers; the second round changes the specificity and contamination questions. That distinction is why the two rounds are represented as a coupled workflow rather than flattened into one pair ([Snounou et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8264734/)).

The current Generation-1 module owns outer/inner constraint windows, product relationship, explicit two-tube topology, transfer assumptions, cycling values and validation requirements. Shared sequence intake, coordinate, topology, safety and provenance rules are inherited from the engine contract where applicable. The ownership boundary follows published nested-PCR workflows in which the second pair is constrained by the first product ([QIAGEN CLC Nested PCR manual](https://resources.qiagenbioinformatics.com/manuals/clcgenomicsworkbench/current/index.php?manual=Nested_PCR.html)).

## Module-owned geometry and inputs

The request must identify whether the reaction is run in one tube or two and must supply the target region that the inner round has to contain. The engine checks that the inner pair is placed within the outer product, that the chosen windows are satisfiable, and that the selected mode's background scan matches the physical workflow ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)):

- two-tube mode checks the outer and inner rounds separately and records the transfer boundary;
- one-tube literature branches are reference-only in Generation-1 and require a separate named protocol contract before execution;
- `nested` requires four distinct primer binding events; `semi-nested` permits one primer to be reused while the other is internal. A pair of unrelated reactions is not promoted to either topology merely because their products overlap ([Thermo Fisher nested-PCR support](https://www.thermofisher.com/au/en/home/technical-resources/technical-reference-library/pcr-cdna-synthesis-support-center/end-point-pcr-primers-support/end-point-pcr-primers-support-getting-started.html); [WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf)).

No generic flanking-pair constraint grid is copied here. The module record is the authority for the two round-specific values, transfer/cycle settings and any supported override. Those values cannot be safely inherited from ordinary PCR because the two rounds have different physical inputs ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)).

## Topology states and design algorithm

The strict topology is represented as two primer pairs with four distinct binding sites, ordered on the reference as outer-forward < inner-forward < inner-reverse < outer-reverse. The inner amplicon interval must be a proper sub-interval of the outer interval; the software must retain the exact coordinates and strand, not infer nesting from product lengths alone ([QIAGEN CLC Nested PCR manual](https://resources.qiagenbioinformatics.com/manuals/clcgenomicsworkbench/current/index.php?manual=Nested_PCR.html); [Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf)).

The semi-nested state has one inner primer and one primer reused from round one. It is a valid, deliberately weaker topology with a different interaction and contamination profile; it must be reported as `semi-nested`, never silently labelled `nested` ([Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf); [WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf)).

Two-tube operation physically transfers a fraction of the first-round material to a second reaction. A one-tube design is not a synonym for “no contamination risk”: it must declare how the round switch occurs, such as staged primer availability or a validated thermal scheme, and must still scan all four oligos and both possible products ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246); [Simplification of nested PCR for *M. tuberculosis*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0035-9203(93)90478-9)).

The candidate search is therefore staged: find a valid outer pair, enumerate inner candidates only inside each viable outer product, then evaluate the coupled four-primer system against the supplied background and the declared workflow. A candidate that passes as an isolated pair but fails the coupled interaction or topology check is rejected rather than downgraded to an ordinary PCR pair ([Snounou et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8264734/); [Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)).

## Primer design and chemistry branches

There is no chemistry-independent nested-PCR recipe. Each round carries its own polymerase, buffer, Mg²⁺/dNTP relationship, primer concentration, annealing rule, extension rule and cycle count. A high-fidelity enzyme is appropriate when the amplicon is intended for cloning or sequencing, while a standard or hot-start Taq branch may be selected for routine endpoint detection; the choice must be visible per round because enzyme properties and buffer recommendations differ ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases); [Thermo Fisher Phusion PCR protocol](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/references/protocols/nucleic-acid-amplification-and-expression-profiling/pcr-protocol/phusion-pcr-protocol.html)).

The design layer should expose the following chemistry branches without pretending that vendor starting values are assay acceptance limits ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)):

| Branch | Documented starting values | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Q5 / Q5 Hot Start / Phusion | `0.5 µM` each primer; `25–35` cycles; initial `98 °C/30 s`; `5–10 s` denaturation; annealing commonly `0–3 °C` above the lowest primer Tm; extension `15–30 s/kb` depending on template complexity; final `72 °C/2–5 min` | NEB starting guidance for proofreading enzymes; apply independently to each round and validate on the actual target ([NEB Q5 protocol](https://www.neb.com/en-us/protocols/pcr-using-q5-high-fidelity-dna-polymerase-m0491); [NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)). |
| OneTaq / OneTaq Hot Start | `1.5–2.0 mM` Mg²⁺ is typical; `200 µM` each dNTP; `25–35` cycles; `94 °C/15 s`, `55 °C/15–30 s`, `68 °C/1 min/kb` in the representative `1,000 bp` program | NEB representative values, not a nested universal default ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)). |
| LongAmp Taq | `300 µM` each dNTP; `1–5 U/50 µL`; extension around `50 s/kb` at `65 °C` in the vendor guidance | Long-product branch; use only when both target length and template complexity are compatible ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)). |
| Thermo/Applied Biosystems endpoint or sequencing workflow | nested or semi-nested second amplification from `1% or less` of first-round product; internal primer design is used to reduce background before sequencing | Workflow guidance, not evidence for a universal transfer fraction or readout threshold ([Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf)). |

The primer candidate layer should start from a sequence-specific calculation, then inspect genomic/background homology, 3′ complementarity, hairpins, cross-round dimers, repeated bases, polymorphisms and product structure. Vendor guidance gives useful search bounds—rather than guarantees—including `20–30` nucleotides, `40–60%` GC, primer Tm `50–72 °C`, pair Tm within `5 °C`, and `0.05–1.0 µM` final primer concentration; these bounds must remain attached to the selected polymerase branch ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)).

## Controls, validation and contamination gate

The two rounds require separate control identities and an explicit inheritance policy. A first-round negative does not replace a second-round negative, and a clean second-round master mix does not prove that the first-round sample or transfer was clean. WOAH's hemi-nested example explicitly carries first-round controls forward and adds a separate second-round PCR negative and NTC; this is the default control topology for a high-risk two-round workflow ([WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf)).

| Control or gate | Required interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| First-round positive, negative and NTC | Establish that extraction/sample handling, first-round reagents and target amplification behaved as expected ([WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf); [WHO PCR recommendations](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/recommendations-for-laboratories-testing-by-pcr-for-presence-of-sars-coronavirus--rna)). |
| Second-round positive, negative and NTC | Detect contamination of the second-round mix and verify that the inner chemistry/readout works; a positive second-round control cannot validate a failed first round ([WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf)). |
| Extraction/process negative | Follow the complete extraction and amplification path when sample preparation is in scope; distinguish process contamination from master-mix contamination ([ISO/TS 16099](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en); [WHO PCR recommendations](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/recommendations-for-laboratories-testing-by-pcr-for-presence-of-sars-coronavirus--rna)). |
| Weak positive or inhibition control | Use a low-level positive in the sample matrix to expose inhibition or matrix-dependent loss; a clean NTC alone cannot establish sample validity ([WHO PCR recommendations](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/recommendations-for-laboratories-testing-by-pcr-for-presence-of-sars-coronavirus--rna); [ISO/TS 16099](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)). |
| Orthogonal confirmation | For a claimed positive, repeat from the original sample and/or confirm a second target/independent laboratory when the application is diagnostic or high consequence ([WHO PCR recommendations](https://www.who.int/publications/m/item/recommendations-for-laboratories-testing-by-pcr-for-presence-of-sars-coronavirus--rna); [Albert and Fenyö 1990](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/jcm.28.7.1560-1564.1990)). |

The contamination gate is hard: if a relevant negative or NTC amplifies, the run is invalid or requires documented investigation and repeat; the positive sample must not be rescued merely because its band is stronger. Separate pre-PCR, template-addition and post-PCR areas, dedicated pipettes/filter tips, unidirectional movement, careful tube centrifugation before opening and a dedicated containment area for round-two inoculation are recommended controls for nested workflows ([WHO Global Malaria Programme laboratory advice](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/case-management/diagnosis/nucleic-acid-amplification-based-diagnostics/dos-and-don-ts-for-molecular-testing); [Longo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(90)90145-h)).

The dUTP/UDG overlay is not automatically compatible with every nested workflow. The prevention mechanism requires prior incorporation of uracil in contaminating products and treatment of subsequent preassembled reactions with UDG followed by heat inactivation; intentional transfer of a desired first- round product, enzyme compatibility and the exact product protocol must all be declared before enabling it ([Longo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(90)90145-h); [Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf)).

Validation must be scoped to the intended claim. For qualitative detection, evaluate inclusivity/exclusivity where relevant, analytical sensitivity near the decision point, analytical specificity, precision/repeatability, robustness, matrix inhibition, carryover, and agreement with an orthogonal method. For quantitative or real-time nested variants, add calibration, linearity, efficiency, LoD/LoQ, uncertainty and reporting requirements from the applicable standard; ISO 20395 is a quantification standard and must not be used to manufacture endpoint-PCR acceptance limits ([ISO/TS 16099](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en); [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/06/78/67893.html?browse=tc); [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148)).

## Failure modes and recovery logic

The module must preserve the distinction between first-round failure, second-round failure and a contaminated negative. A missing outer band can mean template loss, inhibition, wrong outer chemistry or insufficient first-round cycling; a clean outer band with no inner band can mean incorrect inner coordinates, incompatible inner chemistry, transfer loss or a true target variant. A band in an NTC is a contamination event until disproven, not an ordinary low-specificity result ([WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf); [WHO Global Malaria Programme laboratory advice](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/case-management/diagnosis/nucleic-acid-amplification-based-diagnostics/dos-and-don-ts-for-molecular-testing)).

Nested amplification can improve apparent sensitivity while increasing the number of handling steps and the opportunity for carryover. A published HIV-1 contamination study observed false positives after high-copy material was followed by blank material, and repeat testing implicated first-to-second-round carryover; this supports a warning and an investigation workflow, not a fixed universal false-positive rate ([Cross-contamination during nested HIV-1 PCR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2815236/)).

For sequencing, a single expected band is not sufficient if the amplicon is heterogeneous or contains primer-dimer/side products. The product should be checked on the appropriate gel or fragment method and the sequence readout reviewed for mixed peaks; an internal/nested sequencing primer can improve readout specificity but does not correct a contaminated PCR product ([Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf)).

## Unresolved evidence gaps

The literature and supplier documents establish the nested relationship and many valid protocol branches, but they do not establish one portable inner- outer spacing, transfer fraction, cycle count, Tm separation or concentration recipe. These remain protocol- and target-specific inputs rather than missing defaults to be guessed by the engine ([Zazzi et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1006/mcpr.1993.1064); [Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf); [NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)).

There is no universal performance claim for nested PCR across targets, matrices, polymerases or readouts. Sensitivity gain, specificity, inhibition, repeatability, carryover rate, inclusivity/exclusivity and clinical agreement must be measured for the named assay and intended use; the cited HIV and lyssavirus examples are not transferable acceptance limits ([Albert and Fenyö 1990](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/jcm.28.7.1560-1564.1990); [WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf); [ISO/TS 16099](https://www.iso.org/obp/ui?_escaped_fragment_=iso:std:iso:ts:16099:ed-1:v1:en)).

The current implementation intentionally executes only the two-tube topology. One-tube literature branches are retained as scientific references, not as hidden development code or a policy-mode option. Transfer loss, cross-round competition and contamination probability still require assay-specific validation ([Longo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(90)90145-h); [Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)).

Quantitative nested variants additionally lack a portable rule for preserving the outer round in exponential phase, selecting a calibration model, setting LoD/LoQ and propagating uncertainty after transfer. The `10%` outer-product value is a model-specific quantitative safeguard, while MIQE 2.0 and ISO 20395 require validation and reporting for the intended quantitative method rather than a universal nested threshold ([Haff 1994](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.3.6.332); [MIQE 2.0](https://academic.oup.com/clinchem/article/71/6/634/8119148); [ISO 20395:2019](https://www.iso.org/cms/live/live/en/sites/isoorg/contents/data/standard/06/78/67893.html?browse=tc)).

## Protocol evidence overlays

The following overlays are deliberately documentation-level examples. They make the parameter space concrete without turning one disease assay into a default for all targets ([NEB PCR optimization guidelines](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/tools-and-resources/usage-guidelines/guidelines-for-pcr-optimization-with-thermophilic-dna-polymerases)):

| Overlay | Evidence-backed facts | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WOAH hemi-nested lyssavirus RT-PCR | First round uses a `49 µL` master mix plus `1 µL` RNA; first-round cycling includes `50 °C/30 min`, `95 °C/15 min`, then `45` cycles; second round uses a `49 µL` master mix plus `1 µL` undiluted first-round product and `35` cycles; endpoint is read on `1.5–2%` agarose | Fully validated/reproducible named rabies/lyssavirus workflow; not a portable nested default ([WOAH Terrestrial Manual 2023](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahm/3.01.18_RABIES.pdf)). |
| CDC SARS-CoV-2 amplicon sequencing | First-round reaction totals `25 µL` with `5 µL` RNA, and second-round reaction totals `25 µL` with `2 µL` first-round product; first round uses `40` cycles and second round `30` cycles; `3 µL` of each second-round reaction is checked on `1%` agarose or a fragment analyser | Sequencing workflow with 38 amplicon reactions per sample; retain its primer and enzyme identities ([CDC SARS-CoV-2 sequencing supplementary protocol](https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/26/10/20-1800-app1.pdf)). |
| Thermo/Applied Biosystems sequencing template | Nested/semi-nested amplification can use `1% or less` of the first PCR product; primers should generally be at least `18` bases, with `30–80%` GC in the sequencing-template guidance and avoidance of runs of `4` or more Gs | Sequencing-template guidance; these primer bounds are not a universal endpoint nested-PCR design rule ([Thermo Fisher Chemistry Guide](https://tools.qa.thermofisher.com/content/sfs/manuals/cms_041003.pdf)). |
| HIV-1 optimized nested model | A dedicated primary study evaluated first/second cycle counts, transferred preamplified fraction, primer, dNTP and Taq concentrations as separate variables | Use the study as evidence that these variables need optimization; its optimal values are assay/model-specific and must not be invented when the full protocol is unavailable ([Zazzi et al. 1993](https://doi.org/10.1006/mcpr.1993.1064)). |
| Quantitative nested PCR | The outer phase should remain in exponential amplification; one published quantitative model limited maximum outer-product concentration to approximately `10%` of outer-primer molarity | Only for a quantitatively designed nested assay; endpoint nested PCR must not inherit this as a general cycle rule ([Haff 1994](https://doi.org/10.1101/gr.3.6.332)). |

```text
request
  → typed target and mode validation
  → outer-round candidate search
  → inner search restricted to each viable outer product
  → nested geometry and product-order checks
  → mode-specific background and interaction scan
  → coupled scheme ordering, cycling, validation plan and provenance
```

The engine must stop with a named refusal when no inner room exists, when the inner product is not genuinely nested, or when a mode-specific input is missing. It must not silently fall back to a single pair. This refusal preserves the specificity mechanism described for nested assays ([Snounou et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8264734/)).

## Output and claim boundary

Every scheme reports both primer pairs, round labels, coordinates, product lengths, nesting margin, mode, reaction/cycling resolution, specificity scope, cross-round interactions, warnings, validation obligations and provenance. Reporting both rounds is necessary to audit the physical scheme ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)).

Nested design cannot establish contamination control, transfer efficiency, false-positive rate, full-length yield or clinical specificity. Those require the selected workflow, controls and bench evidence ([Longo et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(90)90145-h)).

## Module-owned evidence and decisions

| Decision | Evidence-backed interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Primer geometry | The required order is outer-forward < inner-forward < inner-reverse < outer-reverse, with no shared primer footprint on the same strand. Ordering alone is insufficient because an overlap would undermine the inner-round specificity mechanism. [QIAGEN CLC Nested PCR manual](https://resources.qiagenbioinformatics.com/manuals/clcgenomicsworkbench/current/index.php?manual=Nested_PCR.html); [Shatleh-Rantisi et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246). |
| Minimum inner offset | No larger universal gap is supported. Published working designs span an abutting inner/outer boundary and much larger offsets; the engine therefore treats non-overlap as the floor and does not invent a preferred gap. [US 6451578](https://patents.google.com/patent/US6451578); [Snounou et al. 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8264734/). |
| Two-tube versus single-tube Tm relationship | Two separate reactions need no cross-round Tm relationship. A one-tube thermal-switch topology would require protocol-specific outer/inner thermal separation, but Generation-1 release mode does not execute that topology without a named, sourced and versioned protocol. [Shatleh-Rantisi et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246); [JAALAS 2022](https://doi.org/10.30802/AALAS-JAALAS-21-000117). |
| Inner specificity scope | Inner primers are checked against the full supplied background, not only the outer product, because round-two input can retain the original genomic template. This is a derived workflow rule and is labelled as such. [Snounou et al. 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8264734/). |
| Carry-over and UNG | Round-two UNG/dUTP carry-over treatment is not silently enabled: the intended round-one product is deliberately carried into round two. Any prevention strategy must be selected and reported as a protocol decision. [Longo et al. 1990](https://doi.org/10.1016/0378-1119(90)90145-h). |
| Degraded or ancient template | Nesting is not automatically a remedy for degraded material. A longer outer product and re-amplification can increase fragment-selection and chimera risks; warn rather than refuse. [Malmstrom et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1093/molbev/msm015); [Paabo et al. 1990](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2307682/). |

Protocol values, per-round constraints, concentration ratios and polymerase properties remain module-owned and must be resolved in the profile/result. Where sources disagree, the disagreement is shown to the user instead of being averaged into a false universal default. This keeps protocol disagreement visible instead of manufacturing a shared number without evidence ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246)).

No universal cycle count, transfer volume, annealing temperature, nesting margin or concentration ratio is assigned. Generation-1 does not carry a private one-tube acceptance heuristic; that topology remains reference-only until a named, sourced and versioned protocol contract is implemented ([Shatleh-Rantisi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.heliyon.2020.e03246); [JAALAS 2022](https://doi.org/10.30802/AALAS-JAALAS-21-000117)).

## Protocol-specific transfer and cleanup overlays

The transfer step must be represented as a bounded protocol choice, not as an implicit dilution. Thermo Fisher's nested-PCR guidance describes a second amplification using internal primers and `1% or less` of the first reaction as template; the software therefore records the selected transfer fraction and refuses to invent one when the workflow is not declared ([Thermo Fisher nested-PCR support](https://www.thermofisher.com/au/en/home/technical-resources/technical-reference-library/pcr-cdna-synthesis-support-center/end-point-pcr-primers-support/end-point-pcr-primers-support-getting-started.html)).

Primer removal between rounds is an optional, named intervention rather than a shared nested-PCR rule. NEB documents an Msz Exonuclease I treatment of up to `20 µL` of first-round product at `55 °C for 15 min`, followed by `80 °C for 1 min`, before adding the second-round primers; this overlay may be selected only with its enzyme identity and protocol version, and must not be silently applied to every two-tube run ([NEB Msz Exonuclease I FAQ](https://www.neb.com/faqs/can-msz-exonuclease-i-remove-primers-in-nested-pcr-reactions)).

The current NEB Thermolabile Exonuclease I FAQ describes a different product and a different treatment: `1 µL` added to up to `20 µL` of first-round product, `37 °C for 10 min`, followed by `80 °C for 1 min`, after which a new primer set can be added. This must not be merged with the Msz values above merely because both products remove residual primers. The protocol record therefore needs the exact exonuclease product/catalogue and version before it can resolve the temperature, time, volume or inactivation fields ([NEB Thermolabile Exonuclease I FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/can-thermolabile-exonuclease-i-remove-primers-in-nested-pcr-reactions)).

The runtime currently keeps both cleanup paths outside the default nested profile. A future overlay must be product-typed and must retain the first-round volume, enzyme identity, incubation, inactivation and second-round transfer as one record; no cleanup is inferred from `workflow_mode=two-tube` ([NEB Msz Exonuclease I FAQ](https://www.neb.com/faqs/can-msz-exonuclease-i-remove-primers-in-nested-pcr-reactions), [NEB Thermolabile Exonuclease I FAQ](https://www.neb.com/en-gb/faqs/can-thermolabile-exonuclease-i-remove-primers-in-nested-pcr-reactions)).

## Laboratory contamination-control overlay for successive amplification

CDC molecular-testing good laboratory practice explicitly treats nested PCR as a successive-amplification workflow with elevated carry-over risk when first-round tubes are opened. It calls for a unidirectional workflow and specific procedures to prevent cross-contamination, with physically separated preparation/amplification/product-handling functions as appropriate. At minimum, a no-template control is included in amplification and carried through subsequent detection; where possible, an additional NTC is included at extraction, and if a run spans multiple test units such as plates, each unit should include an NTC when the system permits it. These are laboratory process controls, not primer-design properties, and they remain mandatory handoff metadata whenever the two-tube branch is selected ([CDC Good Laboratory Practices for molecular genetic testing](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/rr5806a1.htm)).

## R16 implementation contract

R16 represents Nested PCR as two independent reactions linked by a typed transfer. Round 1 and Round 2 can carry different polymerase/reaction/thermal-program identities. Transfer modes include direct, diluted, purified product, custom SOP and the named Msz Exonuclease I or Thermolabile Exonuclease I cleanup overlays; exact cleanup temperature/time/volume values resolve only from their matching authority record ([nested PCR contamination study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2815236/)).

Specificity is causal as well as per-primer: an outer off-target product is reconstructed and re-scanned for usable inner sites to produce a `nested_false_product_graph`. Transfer/run provenance and contamination controls are evidence fields. Generic one-tube Nested remains fail-closed; anonymous `dUTP/UNG` remains strategy-only until a named, compatible chemistry provides an executable protocol. No transfer efficiency, LoD or contamination rate is inferred from primer geometry ([nested PCR contamination study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2815236/)).
